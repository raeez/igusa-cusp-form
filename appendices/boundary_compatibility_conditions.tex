\section{The \texorpdfstring{\(N=1\)}{N=1} boundary comparison}
\label{sec:n1-boundary-compatibility-conditions}

A compact Hall realization must compare its source operations with
the denominator datum, in addition to reproducing a scalar series.
Here the geometric base is a smooth complex K3 surface \(S\) times a
smooth elliptic curve \(E\). Singular K3 limits, nodal or cuspidal
elliptic curves, imprimitive multiple covers, the compact Hall cusp
and multi-component charge degenerations each require their own
coefficient and specialization maps.

The formal automorphic target has the ordered support
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}={}&
 \{(n,l,m):m>0,\ n\ge0,\ l\in\mathbb Z\}\\
 &\cup\{(n,l,0):n>0,\ l\in\mathbb Z\}
 \cup\{(0,l,0):l<0\}.
 \end{split}
\]
Only the degrees with \(f(nm,l)\ne0\) contribute factors. They
generate the positive cone
\[
 C_+=\{(n,l,m):n,m,n+m-l\ge0\},\qquad
 h(n,l,m)=2(n+m)-l.
\]
Positive-height completion is
\[
 \widehat{\mathbb Z[C_+]}=\mathbb Z[[qr,sr,r^{-1}]].
\]
The Lorentzian index map is
\[
 \alpha(n,l,m)=2nf_2-lf_3+2mf_{-2},
 \qquad
 (\alpha(\gamma),\alpha(\eta))
       =-2\langle\gamma,\eta\rangle_{\mathrm{ind}},
\]
so \(\alpha(n,l,m)^2=-2(4nm-l^2)\).
The simple roots and Weyl vector are
\[
 \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\quad\delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\quad\delta_3=f_3,
 \qquad
 \rho=f_2-\tfrac12f_3+f_{-2},\quad(\rho,\delta_i)=-1.
\]
These formulas fix the lattice, chamber and completion before the
comparison is formulated.

The scalar and algebraic targets are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Borch}_{\theta}(\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{Weil}})=\Delta_5,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})
       =64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z),\\
 \operatorname{wt}(\Delta_5)=5,
 \qquad Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}=Z^X_{\mathrm{DT}}
       =-4096\Delta_5^{-2}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The last equality has the primitive nonzero-K3-class and variable
conventions of Chapter~\ref{ch:zbps-realization}. More generally a
specified scalar branch may be written
\(Z^X_{\square}=C_{\square}\Delta_5^{-2}\); its normalization
constant does not define the geometric coefficient theory.

Put \(W=W^{(2)}(\Lambda_{II}^{2,1})\). The orientation comparison
requires
\[
 \epsilon_o|_W=\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}|_W
\]
for the homogeneous orthogonal character, including its line action.
Weyl transport of a chosen root pair and the section-compatible wall
charts supply the geometric side. Rescaling \(\Delta_5\) changes
none of these character values.

There are three distinct algebraic recipients. The denominator
algebra is the graded Lie superalgebra \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\).
Its ordinary enveloping algebra uses the usual Koszul tensor. The
associative Ran object \(\mathcal A_{\Delta,E}\) of
Section~\ref{subsec:koszul-source} uses its specified chiral
convolution; comparison with a Beilinson--Drinfeld current envelope
requires the separate current construction stated there. A notation
such as \(U_E^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak g\otimes\mathcal O_E)\)
does not identify these recipients or supply a geometric source.

For a Hall--Drinfeld double, one must construct a compatible
opposite half and the full pairing and cross relations in their
actual tensor category. Serre duality of deformation complexes does
not alone construct that double. The invariant form on the primitive
Lie algebra has a different role from a Hopf-adjoint pairing; imposing
both identities on the same ordinary primitive form would contradict
the real \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) sector.

The recognition criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-recognition} asks for actual geometric
representatives, the Borcherds relations, generation and equality of
the relation kernel. It preserves both parity dimensions and the
invariant form, and then induces the ordinary filtered enveloping
isomorphism. It does not infer these hypotheses from a product
exponent. Even when all signed dimensions agree, additional even and
odd root vectors could cancel in the scalar series. The fixed
presentation and its ordinary denominator determine the target
parities; a geometric map must still identify them.

Finite degree sets retain partial operations with their actual output
spaces. They are not automatically Lie subalgebras, and a symmetric
root cutoff need not be a quotient preserving brackets. Relation
identities must retain every intermediate degree. Quotienting a
free presentation by a collection of defects constructs that
quotient; it does not prove the same defects vanish on an
independently defined compact source. Faithfulness and the
absence of additional source relations remain necessary.

The inverse-limit comparison also needs a common cofinal system,
compatible maps and a specified completion. Ordinary cohomology
commutes with that derived limit only under the corresponding
exactness hypotheses. Centres, associators, supported field
operations, states and protected traces require their own comparisons;
they do not follow from the ordinary primitive recognition theorem.

The eight Gritsenko--Cl\'ery input pairs and the cyclic CHL orders
are different indexing systems, as explained in
Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}. In particular, the labels
\(F_5,F_6,F_7,F_8\) by themselves provide no map to a CHL
denominator, no identified simple real roots or Weyl-sum side, and
no compact geometric realization. An order-four multiplier can be
realized on the corresponding character cover, but this operation
alone constructs no nonabelian denominator algebra. Applying a
separate CHL denominator theorem requires matching its actual seed,
level, multiplier, cusp, lattice and presentation. A row map
\(\Phi_j^{\mathrm{cmp}}\leftrightarrow F_i\) must be constructed;
it is not part of the \(N=1\) theorem.

The \(N=1\) automorphic and denominator data thus specify a concrete
target. The compact problem is to build the coefficient, orientation,
primitive, field and state maps that reach it while preserving their
stated operations.
