\section{Hall correspondences, orientations and reduced classes}
\label{app:hall-correspondences}

An extension correspondence can support several different operations.
Ordinary cohomology, compactly supported cohomology and Borel--Moore
homology require different coefficient maps. A virtual cycle supplies
none of these maps by itself. We state the critical Hall theorem with
its coefficient comparison, then explain the reduced stable-pair
construction on $X=S\times E$, where $S$ is a smooth projective K3
surface and $E$ is an elliptic curve.

We work over $\C$ with rational constructible coefficients. A chosen
nonzero form $\Omega_S$ on $S$ and a nonzero one-form on $E$ trivialize
$K_X$. Thus $X$ has complex dimension three. The shifted symplectic
structure on its derived moduli of perfect complexes has degree $-1$:
the Calabi--Yau formula is $2-\dim_\C X$ \cite{PTVV2013}.
For a fourfold it would instead be $-2$. The virtual-class theories
of \cite{BorisovJoyce,OhThomasI,OhThomasII} and the toric
fourfold DT/PT correspondence of \cite{CaoKoolMonavari} concern
that different dimension. The algebraic
charges of coherent sheaves lie in the support described in
Definition~\ref{def:algebraic-effective-hall-support}, with the
Mukai pairing on the K3 factor \cite{Mukai1984}. Neither a
virtual-cycle construction nor a formal quiver chart enlarges that
support automatically.

\subsection{Oriented critical coefficients}
\label{app:bbdjs}

A $d$-critical locus $(M,s)$ is a classical space locally presented as
the critical locus of a function on a smooth space, with compatible
critical structures on overlaps. It has a canonical line $K_{M,s}$
on $M^{\mathrm{red}}$. An orientation is a line $L$ and an isomorphism
$L^{\otimes2}\simeq K_{M,s}$. For a critical chart
$R\hookrightarrow U$ with function $f$, the restriction of $K_{M,s}$
is $K_U^{\otimes2}|_{R^{\mathrm{red}}}$.

Joyce--Song construct analytic critical charts for sheaf moduli
under their Calabi--Yau hypotheses, including
$H^1(\mathcal O_X)=0$ \cite[Theorems~5.4--5.5]{JoyceSong}.
That local construction does not supply the global oriented
coefficient object in the following theorem. In particular,
$S\times E$ does not satisfy the displayed vanishing hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Oriented vanishing cycles]
\label{thm:bbdjs}
An oriented complex algebraic $d$-critical locus $(M,s,L)$ carries a
perverse sheaf $\Phi_{M,s,L}$ whose restriction to each critical chart
is the perverse vanishing-cycle sheaf of $f$, twisted by the sign local
system of compatible identifications
$L|_{R^{\mathrm{red}}}\simeq K_U|_{R^{\mathrm{red}}}$.
These local sheaves glue canonically with their duality and monodromy
structures \cite[Theorem~6.9]{BBDJS2015}.
\end{theorem}

The twist in this statement is a rank-one rational local system
associated to a $\mu_2$-torsor. It is not tensor product with the
underlying algebraic line $L$. The construction for oriented
$d$-critical Artin stacks uses smooth descent and the stack version
of these coefficient comparisons; we use the conventions of
\cite[\S4]{KinjoParkSafronovCoHA}. In particular, the phrase
``vanishing-cycle coefficients'' includes the orientation and its
chosen descent isomorphisms.

For a continuous family of real elliptic operators on a compact
manifold, the determinant line instead has an orientation double
cover \cite[Definition~1.2]{JoyceTanakaUpmeier}. A comparison with
critical orientation data must identify these two orientation
problems; a trivialization in one does not specify that comparison.

The Thom--Sebastiani theorem compares the critical coefficients of
$f\boxplus g$ on a product with the external tensor product of the
coefficients of $f$ and $g$ \cite[Theorem~2.13]{BBDJS2015}.
For constructible derived coefficients this is the natural
monodromy-compatible comparison of \cite[\S0]{MasseyST}.
An extension stack is generally not that product. Its Hall operation
uses an additional comparison on the extension correspondence.

\subsection{The critical Hall product}
\label{app:hall-product}

Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite-type stable dg category with a
$3$-Calabi--Yau structure, and let $\mathcal M_{\mathcal C}$ denote
its derived moduli stack of pseudo-perfect modules. Choose an open
substack $\mathcal M$ closed under direct sums and containing zero.
Write $\Gamma=\pi_0(\mathcal M)$ and
$\mathcal M=\coprod_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathcal M_\alpha$.
Direct sum makes $\Gamma$ a commutative monoid.
The two-step filtration stack gives the correspondence
\[
 \mathcal M_\alpha\times\mathcal M_\beta
 \xleftarrow{\ p\ }\mathcal F_{\alpha,\beta}
 \xrightarrow{\ q\ }\mathcal M_{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
Here $p$ records the associated graded objects in the fixed order,
and $q$ records the filtered object's underlying object.

An orientation of $\mathcal M$ induces an orientation on a graded
pair through this Lagrangian correspondence. Orientation data
identifies it with the external product of the two orientations and
identifies the orientation at zero with the trivial one. Such data
are \emph{strong} when the two induced identifications on three
factors agree, and both unit diagrams commute
\cite[Definition~8.4]{KinjoParkSafronovCoHA}.

We use the cohomological convention $K[d]^i=K^{i+d}$. Let
$\kappa(\alpha,\beta)$ denote the integer shift in the hyperbolic
coefficient comparison
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:D-critical-hall-comparison}
 (\Phi_\alpha\boxtimes\Phi_\beta)[\kappa(\alpha,\beta)]
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } Rp_*q^!\Phi_{\alpha+\beta}.
\end{equation}
This definition fixes the sign of every shift below.
The index satisfies
\[
 \kappa(\alpha,\beta)+\kappa(\alpha+\beta,\gamma)
 =\kappa(\beta,\gamma)+\kappa(\alpha,\beta+\gamma),
 \qquad \kappa(0,\alpha)=\kappa(\alpha,0)=0.
\]
For $\Gamma$-graded complexes define the corresponding tensor product by
\[
 (V\otimes_\kappa W)_\gamma
 =\bigoplus_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}
       (V_\alpha\otimes W_\beta)[\kappa(\alpha,\beta)].
\]
The cocycle identity supplies its associativity constraint.

\begin{theorem}[Critical cohomological Hall multiplication]
\label{thm:hall-product}
Assume that $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{cl}}$ is a quasi-separated Artin
stack with affine stabilizers, that it is $\Theta$-reductive, and
that its zero object is a closed point substack. Equip $\mathcal M$
with strong orientation data. Then the coefficients in
\eqref{eq:D-critical-hall-comparison} exist and define a unital
associative multiplication
\[
 H\otimes_\kappa H\longrightarrow H,
 \qquad
 H=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}
 H^\bullet(\mathcal M_\alpha,\Phi_\alpha).
\]
The unit comes from the zero object. The multiplication is the
critical Hall construction of
\cite[Theorem~8.6, Corollary~8.8 and Proposition~8.29]
{KinjoParkSafronovCoHA}, with shifts specified by
\eqref{eq:D-critical-hall-comparison}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cited theorem constructs the hyperbolic comparison and its
compatibility with three-step filtrations. Its adjoint is
\[
 p^*(\Phi_\alpha\boxtimes\Phi_\beta)
       [\kappa(\alpha,\beta)]
 \longrightarrow q^!\Phi_{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
The $\Theta$-reductivity hypothesis makes $q$ proper on each
component used here. Apply ordinary cohomology, then the counit
$Rq_!q^!\Phi_{\alpha+\beta}\to\Phi_{\alpha+\beta}$, using
$Rq_!=Rq_*$ on that component. The resulting map has source
$(H_\alpha\otimes H_\beta)[\kappa(\alpha,\beta)]$.
The two compositions for a triple are induced by the same
three-step filtration correspondence. The associativity assertion
for the coefficient comparison and the strong orientation
identification make these maps equal. The two zero-endpoint
filtrations give the unit identities.
\end{proof}

For a smooth projective threefold with trivial canonical bundle,
Joyce--Upmeier construct orientation data on the moduli of sheaves and
perfect complexes \cite[Theorem~4.4]{JoyceUpmeier}. The existence of
strong orientation data is a further condition; its obstruction is
formulated in \cite[Theorem~3.10]{JoyceUpmeier}. Without this
condition the critical multiplication has the sign ambiguity described
in \cite[Remark~8.7 and Example~8.9]{KinjoParkSafronovCoHA}.
For Ginzburg categories of quivers with potential, strong orientation
data and comparison with the quiver product of
\cite{KSCoHA} are proved
in \cite[Proposition~8.34, Corollary~8.36 and Proposition~8.37]
{KinjoParkSafronovCoHA}.

The theorem uses ordinary critical cohomology. A product on compactly
supported cohomology, on dual compactly supported cohomology, or on a
reduced translation quotient needs its own coefficient comparison and
support functoriality. In particular, a properness assertion about a
Quot scheme must refer to coherent sheaves, a fixed Hilbert polynomial,
and closed conditions on the quotients. It does not prove properness
for an arbitrary open stability condition or an arbitrary tilted heart.

\subsection{Collision coproducts and primitives}
\label{app:hall-coproduct}

A multiplication does not specify a coproduct. A candidate collision
correspondence has the reverse form
\[
 \mathcal M_\gamma\xleftarrow{\ r\ }
 \mathcal C_{\alpha,\beta}\xrightarrow{\ s\ }
 \mathcal M_\alpha\times\mathcal M_\beta,
 \qquad \alpha+\beta=\gamma.
\]
To use it one must give a coefficient morphism with the variance and
shift required by the chosen homology theory, and identify its iterates
on triple collisions. The extension correspondence alone provides
neither of these data.

\begin{theorem}[A supplied collision coproduct]
\label{thm:hall-coproduct}
Suppose the collision correspondences induce maps
$\Delta:H\to H\otimes H$ in a specified symmetric monoidal
category, and that their coefficient comparisons satisfy
coassociativity, both counit identities, and compatibility with the
Hall product and its unit. Suppose also that the counit is an algebra
map. Then the resulting unital algebra and counital coalgebra form
a bialgebra. This conclusion applies to the compact reduced Hall
object when the geometric and coefficient hypotheses of
Definition~\ref{def:compact-geometric-hall-bialgebra-datum} are
satisfied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compose the coefficient morphisms along the two triple-collision
correspondences. Their identification gives coassociativity.
The zero-output correspondences give the counit equations. The
compatibility correspondence for multiplication and collision gives
\[
 \Delta m=(m\otimes m)(1\otimes\tau\otimes1)(\Delta\otimes\Delta),
\]
where $\tau$ is the symmetry of the chosen category. The stated unit
and counit compatibilities are the remaining bialgebra equations.
\end{proof}

The symmetry and tensor product in this statement are part of its
hypotheses. An algebra in the index-twisted category of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hall-product} is not, without a compatible
regrading or comparison, an ordinary Hopf superalgebra.

\subsection{Primitive Lie superalgebras}
\label{app:hall-primitives}

Now let $H$ be a bialgebra in super vector spaces over a field of
characteristic zero. Put $\overline H=\ker\varepsilon$ and let
$\pi=1-\eta\varepsilon:H\to\overline H$ be the augmentation
projection. The reduced coproduct on $\overline H$ is
$\overline\Delta=(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta$.

\begin{theorem}[Primitive closure]
\label{thm:hall-primitives-formal}
The space
\[
 P(H)=\ker(\overline\Delta:\overline H
                          \longrightarrow\overline H\otimes\overline H)
\]
is a Lie superalgebra under
$[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx$. A compatible differential makes it a dg
Lie superalgebra. No antipode is needed for this conclusion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in\overline H$, the counit and coaugmentation equations give
$\Delta x=x\otimes1+1\otimes x+\overline\Delta x$.
For homogeneous primitives $x,y$, multiplicativity of $\Delta$ gives
\[
 \Delta(xy)=xy\otimes1+x\otimes y
       +(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x+1\otimes xy.
\]
Subtract the same formula with $x,y$ interchanged and multiplied by
$(-1)^{|x||y|}$. The mixed terms cancel, proving primitive closure.
The super Jacobi identity follows by expanding the commutator in the
associative algebra. A differential compatible with $m$ and $\Delta$
preserves both $\overline H$ and its primitive kernel.
\end{proof}

This result concerns the primitive kernel of a specified coproduct.
It does not identify that kernel with BPS cohomology, the primitive
part of a word-cut bar construction, or the denominator Lie
superalgebra. Those comparisons retain the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-recognition}.

\subsection{Cosection localization and reduction}
\label{app:cosection}

\begin{definition}[Cosection and degeneracy locus]
\label{def:cosection-app-D}
Let $M$ be a Deligne--Mumford stack with a perfect obstruction theory
$\mathbb E\to\mathbb L_M$ of amplitude $[-1,0]$. Its obstruction
sheaf is $\mathrm{Ob}_M=h^1(\mathbb E^\vee)$.
A cosection is an $\mathcal O_M$-linear map
$\sigma:\mathrm{Ob}_M\to\mathcal O_M$.
Let $U\subset M$ be the open locus where it is surjective, and let
$Z=M\setminus U$ be its degeneracy locus.
\end{definition}

Write $\mathfrak E=h^1/h^0(\mathbb E^\vee)$ for the obstruction
bundle stack. The relevant cone stack is
\[
 \mathfrak E(\sigma)=\mathfrak E|_Z\,\cup\,
            \ker(\sigma:\mathfrak E|_U\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_U).
\]
It contains the full obstruction bundle stack over $Z$. It is not the
kernel of the obstruction sheaf regarded as a perfect complex.

\begin{theorem}[Kiem--Li localization]
\label{thm:cosection-localisation}
The intrinsic normal cone lies in $\mathfrak E(\sigma)$, and the
localized Gysin map gives
\[
 [M]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathrm{loc}}\in A_{\mathrm{vd}}(Z),
 \qquad i_*[M]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathrm{loc}}=[M]^{\mathrm{vir}},
\]
where $i:Z\hookrightarrow M$ and $\mathrm{vd}=\operatorname{rk}\mathbb E$
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{KiemLi2013}. If $\sigma$ is everywhere
surjective, then $[M]^{\mathrm{vir}}=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cone containment and localized Gysin construction are the cited
theorem. Compatibility of the localized Gysin map with the ordinary
zero-section Gysin map gives the pushforward identity. If $U=M$,
then $Z$ is empty, so its Chow group and the localized class vanish.
\end{proof}

Removing a trivial obstruction direction is a different construction.
It requires a map of obstruction complexes and a compatible reduced
perfect obstruction theory on the specified moduli space. When this
removes a rank-one obstruction summand, the virtual dimension rises
by one. The localized class above retains the original virtual
dimension and is supported on $Z$; it is not this reduced class.

For a perfect complex $F$ on $X$, the familiar candidate
semiregularity functional is
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^2_X(F,F)\longrightarrow H^3(X,\Omega_X^1)
 \xrightarrow{\ \Omega_S\wedge-\ }H^3(X,K_X)
 \xrightarrow{\ \int_X\ }\C.
\]
The first arrow is composition with the Atiyah class followed by
trace. Its restriction to the relevant obstruction complex, its
surjectivity in families and the resulting reduced theory must be
specified. The nonvanishing of $\Omega_S$ alone proves none of
these assertions. The formal Borcherds variable $s$ does not define
the degeneracy locus of this cosection.

\begin{theorem}[Reduced stable pairs on $S\times E$]
\label{thm:reduced-virtual-class-K3xE}
Let $\beta\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)$ be a nonzero curve class,
$d\geq0$, and $n\in\Z$. Set
$P=P_n(S\times E,(\beta,d))$. There is a reduced class
$[P]^{\mathrm{red}}\in A_1(P)$. The translation action has finite
stabilizers, and its Deligne--Mumford quotient $P/E$ carries a
zero-dimensional virtual class such that
\[
 \pi^*[P/E]^{\mathrm{vir}}=[P]^{\mathrm{red}},
 \qquad \pi:P\longrightarrow P/E.
\]
Its degree is the orbifold Euler characteristic weighted by the
Behrend function of $P/E$. It equals the reduced invariant obtained
by integrating the normalized incidence insertion on $[P]^{\mathrm{red}}$
\cite[Theorems~1--2]{OberdieckReducedPT}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Oberdieck constructs an isotrivial map $P\to E$, equivariant for a
nonzero multiple of translation. Its fibre $K$ over zero has a
symmetric perfect obstruction theory, and $P/E\simeq K/G$ for a
finite group $G$. The class
$|G|^{-1}(K\to P/E)_*[K]^{\mathrm{vir}}$ is independent of the
chosen isotrivial map. The virtual-class formula in terms of the
Fulton Chern class gives its pullback as $[P]^{\mathrm{red}}$.
Behrend's formula on $K$, together with finite descent, identifies
its degree with the weighted Euler characteristic of the quotient.
The normalized incidence insertion intersects an elliptic orbit
with degree one, giving the second description of that degree.
These are the constructions and comparisons of
\cite[\S3]{OberdieckReducedPT}.
\end{proof}

The theorem holds for nonzero $\beta$ without requiring it to be
primitive. The primitive-class hypothesis enters the particular
Igusa generating-series identity discussed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic}. Its normalization is
$Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}=-4096\Delta_5^{-2}$, after the specified DT/PT
and GW/PT comparisons and expansion conventions. A numerical
identity for these reduced invariants does not supply critical Hall
coefficient morphisms.

\begin{proposition}[Reduced critical coefficients require a critical structure]
\label{prop:bbdjs-reduced-K3xE}
Suppose a reduced translation quotient is equipped with a
$d$-critical structure and an orientation compatible with its
local critical charts. Then Theorem~\ref{thm:bbdjs}, or its Artin
stack version on the stated domain, gives its critical coefficient
sheaf. A Hall multiplication on these coefficients further requires
the oriented coefficient comparison on extension flags and its
associativity and unit coherences.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the oriented critical-sheaf theorem to the supplied structure.
The Hall maps are then constructed only when the coefficient
comparison and support conditions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:hall-product}, or the specified reduced analogue,
are available. A symmetric perfect obstruction theory or a virtual
class alone is not among the hypotheses that construct these
comparison morphisms.
\end{proof}

The reduced stable-pair theorem supplies a virtual class on its
particular moduli spaces. It does not state that every reduced
semistable Hall stack is an oriented $d$-critical stack, or that
removing one obstruction direction preserves a shifted symplectic
structure. The desired compact coefficient theory must construct
these structures and their maps on the actual extension and collision
diagrams.

\subsection{Stable envelopes and quantum groups}
\label{app:stable-envelope}

Let $M$ be a smooth holomorphic symplectic variety with a proper map
to an affine variety. Let $A\subset T$ be tori acting on $M$, with
$A$ preserving the symplectic form, and assume the equivariance and
$T$-formality hypotheses of \cite[\S3.1]{MaulikOkounkov}.
The nonzero $A$-weights in the normal bundle of $M^A$ determine
hyperplanes in $\operatorname{Cochar}(A)\otimes\R$. Their
complement has chambers. Choose a chamber $\mathcal C$ and a
polarization of the fixed locus. The fixed components need not be
points.

\begin{definition}[Stable envelope]
\label{def:stable-envelope}
The stable envelope is the $H_T^\bullet(\mathrm{pt})$-linear map
\[
 \operatorname{Stab}_{\mathcal C}:
 H_T^\bullet(M^A)\longrightarrow H_T^\bullet(M)
\]
characterized by attracting support, the signed Euler class of the
negative normal bundle on each fixed component, and a strict bound
on the degree in the $A$-parameters on lower fixed components.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes]
\label{thm:maulik-okounkov}
Under these hypotheses and choices, the stable envelope exists and
is unique \cite[Theorem~3.3.4]{MaulikOkounkov}. After equivariant
localization it is invertible, and chamber comparisons
\[
 R_{\mathcal C',\mathcal C}
 =\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathcal C'}^{-1}
       \operatorname{Stab}_{\mathcal C}
\]
satisfy the chamber composition identities. For the framing
factorizations of Nakajima quiver varieties
\cite{Nakajima1994,Nakajima1998}, these identities give
the spectral-parameter Yang--Baxter equation
\cite[\S4.1.8--9]{MaulikOkounkov}.
The resulting geometric Yangian $Y_Q$ acts on the direct sums of
quiver-variety cohomologies used in that construction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Existence and uniqueness are the cited stable-envelope theorem.
Localization makes the triangular restriction matrix invertible.
Successive chamber comparisons cancel their intermediate stable
envelopes. On a three-factor framing decomposition, the two routes
between opposite chambers factor into the three pairwise
$R$-matrices, giving the Yang--Baxter equation. The Yangian is
constructed from these operators in
\cite[Chapters~5--6]{MaulikOkounkov}.
\end{proof}

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_Q$ obtained from the classical
$R$-matrix contains the quiver Kac--Moody algebra and can be larger;
see \cite[\S1.2.8]{MaulikOkounkov}. It must not be identified with
that Kac--Moody algebra for an arbitrary quiver. The
Schiffmann--Vasserot construction uses framed rank-$r$ torsion-free
sheaves on $\mathbb P^2$, framed along its line at infinity.
Let $M_{r,n}$ be their instanton moduli, and localize equivariant
cohomology over $K_r=\C(x,y,e_1,\ldots,e_r)$. Their specialized
algebra $\mathbf{SH}^{(r)}_{K_r}$ acts faithfully on
$L^{(r)}_{K_r}=\bigoplus_n H_*^{\widetilde T}(M_{r,n})\otimes K_r$.
It embeds into the specified degreewise completed current algebra
of $W_{\kappa-r}(\mathfrak{gl}_r)$, with $\kappa=-y/x$, and
induces its Verma-module action on $L^{(r)}_{K_r}$
\cite[Introduction, Theorems~A--C]{SchiffmannVasserot2013}.
This preserves the distinction between a convolution algebra,
its completion, and a representation of a vertex algebra.
These theorems provide concrete quantum-group models; a comparison
with a compact reduced $S\times E$ Hall algebra requires further
geometric and coefficient maps.

A compact comparison would have to specify an integral form, a
coproduct and pairing, the completed Drinfeld double, its Cartan
subalgebra and the geometric operators defining the comparison.
Equality of an index or of a denominator does not determine these
structures. Definition~\ref{def:integral-form-stable-envelope-comparison-datum}
keeps this problem separate from the construction of the reference
Yangian.

\subsection{The remaining compact comparisons}
\label{app:hall-level-separations}
\label{app:hall-local-obstruction-calculations}

Three established constructions enter the compact problem: the
critical Hall product under strong orientation hypotheses, the
reduced stable-pair virtual class, and the quantum groups acting on
quiver-variety cohomology. Their comparison requires maps on
coefficients. The formal cyclic charts provide a local domain on
which to construct and test those maps; they do not identify the
three constructions globally.

For the reduced $S\times E$ theory, the first equations are the
coefficient comparison on extensions and its equality on the two
three-step flag diagrams, including the orientation identifications.
The collision coproduct has a separate three-output equation and a
compatibility equation with multiplication. Elliptic descent is
relative to a common $E$-torsor and has unit $BE$. A final comparison
with the Igusa denominator also needs the primitive Lie form,
operator realization, protected state and modular line described in
the main text. These requirements concern different mathematical
objects; satisfying one leaves the others to be constructed.
