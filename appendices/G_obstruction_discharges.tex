\section{Orientation, wall and trace comparisons}
\label{app:obstruction-discharges}

A square root of a determinant, a Pfaffian section and a protected
trace require different constructions. The square root must descend
through the geometric quotient. The section must arise from a skew
complex and agree with the automorphic section in a specified line.
The trace requires a state and a cyclic operation on that state.
This appendix gives the comparison criteria and their consequences.
It does not assume that the complete compact datum exists.

\subsection{The geometric recipients}
\label{app:G-setup}

Fix a smooth projective K3 surface \(S\), an elliptic curve \(E\)
and \(X=S\times E\). Choose a holomorphic two-form \(\sigma_S\)
on \(S\) and a nonzero one-form on \(E\), hence a Calabi--Yau
three-form on \(X\). The stability heart and bounded geometric
loci are those of Appendix~\ref{app:stability-support}.
The symmetric Mukai form on \(H^*(S,\mathbb Z)\) is distinct
from the skew Euler form of the threefold category.

At each stage \(R\), specify the object, extension, mixed, wrapped
and higher flag stacks, their coefficient categories and transition
maps. A notation such as \(\mathfrak M_R^{\mathrm{red}}\)
includes an actual reduction construction. The ambient shifted
symplectic structure on \(\operatorname{Perf}(X)\) does not make
every retained substack quasi-smooth, reduced or oriented.
In particular, the scalar and dual scalar directions must be
accounted for before using a two-term obstruction theory.

\subsection{Specialization to zero-dimensional objects}
\label{app:G-D0-discharge}

The threefold D0 sector consists of zero-dimensional objects on
\(X\); for sheaves its charge is their length. It is different
from \(\operatorname{Hilb}^n(S)\), which supplies a surface
comparison. A degeneration from curve classes to this sector must
first specify its geometric recipient.

There is an elementary obstruction in a fixed recipient. In a flat
family of coherent sheaves on a fixed polarized projective
threefold, the Hilbert polynomial is constant. A nonzero effective
one-cycle has a positive linear Hilbert coefficient, while a
zero-dimensional sheaf has a constant Hilbert polynomial. Such a
flat family cannot specialize the former to the latter. Varying
only stability does not change this conserved charge. A proposed
D0 comparison must therefore either stay within the D0 charge or
construct a different geometric or categorical degeneration and
its charge map.

\begin{definition}[A D0 specialization system]
\label{def:G-D0-HN-system}
A datum \(D0_{\mathrm{HN}}\) consists of the following compatible
objects and maps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(D0.1)}] For every stage \(R\), a specified family of
geometric or categorical recipients over a pointed curve, with a
chosen D0 subcategory in the special fibre. If the family is
geometric and flatness is required, flatness is part of the datum.
Its charge specialization is specified and respects the preceding
Hilbert-polynomial constraint in a fixed sheaf recipient.
\item[\textup{(D0.2)}] Object and full flag loci over that curve,
with transition maps \(\rho_{RR'}\) in their actual variance,
composition isomorphisms, and the properness or support conditions
needed by their coefficient functors.
\item[\textup{(D0.3)}] Perfect reduced deformation complexes and
oriented critical charts on the loci where critical coefficients
are used, together with comparison maps from the ambient theory.
Their amplitude and nondegeneracy are hypotheses on these loci.
\item[\textup{(D0.4)}] The actual semiregularity morphisms, their
fibres and their compatibility with flags and specialization.
A surjectivity theorem for nonzero curve class is used only there;
it does not supply surjectivity in the D0 sector.
\item[\textup{(D0.5)}] Reduced constructible coefficients
\(\Phi_R^{\mathrm{red}}\) and specialization morphisms, including
an equivalence wherever one is required by the comparison.
Their extension and higher flag maps commute with specialization.
\item[\textup{(D0.6)}] Root pairs
\((o_R,o_R^{\otimes2}\simeq\mathscr D_R^{\mathrm{red}})\),
with their cross-determinant factors, square-compatible transition
maps and higher flag coherences.
\item[\textup{(D0.7)}] Compatible skew complexes, Pfaffian lines
and sections, quotient-after-correspondence maps, compact-support
operations and protected integration maps wherever the proposed
comparison uses them.
\item[\textup{(D0.8)}] The exactness and effectivity hypotheses
needed for each asserted limit: derived inverse-limit comparisons
for coefficient complexes, and effective descent of root pairs and
sections if an actual infinite geometric recipient is claimed.
\end{enumerate}
These clauses define a system in the specified recipient. They do
not assert a flat degeneration of nonzero curve charge to D0
sheaves on the unchanged threefold.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compatible D0 specialization]
\label{thm:G-D0}
A datum \(D0_{\mathrm{HN}}\) defines a pro-system of reduced
coefficients, orientation pairs, Pfaffian sections and the supplied
operations, together with its specialization comparison. On any
recipient where \textup{(D0.8)} gives effectivity, the corresponding
limit carries these structures and maps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The composition and higher flag comparisons make the stagewise
objects a diagram in their coefficient and Picard categories.
The specialization maps give a morphism of these diagrams.
The same is true for every operation square by
\textup{(D0.5)}--\textup{(D0.7)}. This constructs the pro-system.
Effectivity and passage through cohomology then use precisely the
comparisons in \textup{(D0.8)}. For countable coefficient towers,
the possible extra term is \(\varprojlim{}^1H^{q-1}\), as in
Section~\ref{subsec:eight-finite-constructions}. There is no general
Mittag--Leffler assertion about a gerbe or a functor without first
specifying the corresponding diagram.
\end{proof}

The ambient shifted symplectic theorem \cite{PTVV2013}, orientation
theory \cite{JoyceUpmeier,JoyceTanakaUpmeier}, the oriented
critical-sheaf construction \cite{BBDJS2015} and cosection
localization \cite{KiemLi2013} enter at different steps.
Cosection localization of a virtual cycle does not itself construct
reduced vanishing-cycle coefficients or their specialization.

\begin{remark}[The surface scalar comparison]
\label{rem:G-D0-elliptic-genus}
The Hilbert--Chow map
\(\operatorname{Hilb}^n(S)\to\operatorname{Sym}^n(S)\) and the
Hilbert-scheme elliptic classes of
Chapter~\ref{ch:zbps-realization} give an actual K3 state and
scalar calculation. The \(\chi_y\)-genus is a specialization of
that elliptic genus, not the whole genus. Reduced K3 curve
invariants \cite{MaulikPandharipande} and threefold D0
specialization are further, distinct constructions. None of these
scalar formulas provides the maps in \(D0_{\mathrm{HN}}\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Roots on the translation quotient}
\label{app:G-O1-discharge}

Let \(B_R\) be a retained locus before translation quotient and
\(\mathscr D_R\) its actual reduced determinant line. Its root
problem belongs to \([B_R/E]\). Denote the Kummer obstruction by
\(\delta(\mathscr D_R)\in H^2([B_R/E],\mu_2)\).
If this class vanishes, the isomorphism classes of root pairs form an
\(H^1([B_R/E],\mu_2)\)-torsor. A choice of pair is still needed.

\begin{definition}[Quotient orientation]
\label{def:G-O1-quot}
The datum \(O1_{\mathrm{quot}}\) consists of roots of the actual
reduced determinant lines on the quotient object and flag stacks,
with their square maps, cross-determinant comparisons and coherent
transition isomorphisms. Equivalently, on a presenting groupoid it
consists of root cocycles and chosen primitives for their defects,
compatible with every overlap, flag and stage map. Coarse descent,
when required, additionally asks that stabilizers act trivially on
the chosen root fibres.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Strong reduced orientation]
\label{def:G-strong-reduced-orientation}
A strong reduced orientation is such a quotient root system whose
extension comparisons are associative on all retained flags and
compatible with the reduced critical coefficient maps. No
\(\operatorname{Spin}^c\) or \(\operatorname{Pin}^c\) structure
is included unless its quadratic bundle and lift are separately
constructed.
\end{definition}

A free \(E\)-action does not justify setting an independently
written class in \(H^2(BE,\mu_2)\) to zero. It is its image in
the actual quotient problem that matters. For example,
\([E/E]=\mathrm{pt}\). Likewise, an edge computation at a finite
stabilizer applies only after ordinary, mixed and differential
terms in the equivariant obstruction have been accounted for.

\begin{lemma}[The Klein-four detection equations]
\label{lem:G-Klein-four}
For \(E[2]=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\), write
\[
 \beta=b_{20}x_1^2+b_{11}x_1x_2+b_{02}x_2^2
       \in H^2(BE[2],\mathbb F_2),\qquad |x_i|=1.
\]
If \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) are its coefficients on the two coordinate
subgroups and the diagonal subgroup, then
\[
 b_{20}=r_1,\qquad b_{02}=r_2,\qquad
 b_{11}=r_1+r_2+r_3.
\]
Thus \(\beta=0\) exactly when all three restrictions vanish.
For a chosen root character
\(\lambda=\lambda_1x_1+\lambda_2x_2\), the three restriction
values are instead \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_1+\lambda_2\).
The two vanishing problems are independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The ring is \(H^*(BE[2],\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2[x_1,x_2]\).
The restrictions substitute \((t,0),(0,t),(t,t)\).
These substitutions give both formulas. A zero degree-two class
admits, for example, the nonzero character \((1,0)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The full two-primary stabilizer]
\label{lem:G-two-primary-stabilizer}
For \(E[N]=(\mathbb Z/N)^2\), odd \(N\) gives no
positive-degree mod-two group cohomology. If \(2^a\parallel N\),
the calculation reduces to \((\mathbb Z/2^a)^2\).
For \(a=1\) the preceding lemma applies. For \(a\ge2\),
\[
 H^*(B(\mathbb Z/2^a)^2,\mathbb F_2)
 =\Lambda(x_1,x_2)\otimes\mathbb F_2[y_1,y_2],
 \qquad |x_i|=1,\quad |y_i|=2.
\]
Consequently an edge obstruction and a root character have the forms
\[
 \beta=A_1y_1+A_{12}x_1x_2+A_2y_2,
 \qquad \lambda=\lambda_1x_1+\lambda_2x_2.
\]
Their simultaneous vanishing requires all five coefficients to
vanish. The coefficient \(A_{12}\) is invisible on every cyclic
subgroup.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Averaging removes the odd-primary part. The cyclic mod-two
cohomology ring for order \(2^a\), \(a\ge2\), is
\(\Lambda(x)\otimes\mathbb F_2[y]\); K\"unneth gives the ring
above. On a cyclic subgroup of order at least four, both
restricted degree-one classes are multiples of one exterior class,
whose square vanishes. On an order-two subgroup their restrictions
are zero. Hence \(x_1x_2\) is invisible in either case.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Detection and gluing]
\label{rem:G-N-2-vs-N-3}
These are equations for the indicated classifying-space edge.
They do not provide its geometric reduction, a primitive for a
cocycle, or compatibility of chosen primitives on intersecting
strata. The exterior-generator calculation requires \(4\mid N\);
for \(N\equiv2\pmod4\), the Klein-four calculation applies.
The degree-one condition is needed for the requested trivial
stabilizer action, not for every root on a quotient stack.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Quotient orientation criterion]
\label{thm:G-O1}
The coherent roots and flag comparisons of
\(O1_{\mathrm{quot}}\) descend to a strong reduced orientation
on the quotient stack. If their stabilizer characters vanish and
the stated coarse descent theorem applies to the retained locus,
they descend further to its coarse recipient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Descent of line bundles with square maps is descent in the Picard
groupoid. The supplied overlap cocycles give its descent datum;
the flag coherences descend the cross-determinant comparisons.
This proves stack descent. The extra trivial stabilizer action
and the coarse descent hypotheses give the second assertion.
There is no multiplication formula omitting the cross determinant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Weyl transport}
\label{app:G-O1-plus-discharge}

Use the type-II Coxeter subgroup
\(W=\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\mid s_i^2=1\rangle\).
Its full lattice isometry group also contains chamber symmetries;
these two groups have different character comparisons.
A geometric \(W\)-action requires maps of the actual root pairs
on the quotient action groupoid, with their square maps and
coefficient and flag compatibilities.

\begin{theorem}[Equivariant root transport]
\label{thm:G-O1-plus}
Suppose the chosen lifts of
Definition~\ref{def:O1-plus-weyl-transport} have a specified
trivialization of their action-groupoid defect and zero remaining
quotient-torsor defects. They define an honest \(W\)-equivariant
root system. If its residual scalar generator values are constant
and each section-compatible simple skew wall has value \(-1\),
its character is
\[
 \epsilon_o(w)=\det_W(w).
\]
This is the restriction of the homogeneous orthogonal character
\(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}\) to \(W\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Correcting the lifts by the chosen primitive makes their
compositions satisfy the action law. The quotient-torsor equations
keep the root and coefficient comparisons in that same action.
After its residual action has been identified with a scalar
character, its value on a reduced word is the product of the
values on the generators. Cancellation of \(s_i^2\) preserves
that product. Every value \(-1\) therefore gives \((-1)^{\ell(w)}\),
which is the determinant character of the reflection representation.
The character of \(\Delta_5\) has these generator values by
Appendix~\ref{app:pfaffian-wall-normal-form}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Which information a character retains]
\label{rem:G-O1-plus-reduction}
The abelianization \(W_{\mathrm{ab}}=(\mathbb Z/2)^3\)
classifies the constant sign characters. It does not classify root
obstructions on the geometric action groupoid. The products of two
distinct simple reflections are parabolic of infinite order; there
is no order-three braid relation. Extending the root action to
\(W\rtimes S_3\) requires chamber-symmetry lifts. The two
extensions \(\epsilon_o(w)\) and
\(\epsilon_o(w)\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\) have the same
restriction to \(W\), while the automorphic homogeneous character
chooses the trivial \(S_3\)-factor. Its symplectic multiplier
must still be kept with the weight-five automorphy factor.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Skew wall charts and their global comparison}
\label{app:G-O2-discharge}

\begin{definition}[A type-II wall atlas]
\label{def:G-O2-atlas}
A datum \(O2_{\mathrm{atlas}}\) consists of actual wall objects
with their full charges, wall semistability and reduced deformation
maps. At each simple wall it gives a chart with normal coordinate
\(u\), a skew normal block
\[
 D_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad [V\xrightarrow{D_u}V^\vee]\text{ in degrees }[-1,0],
\]
an invertible tangential Pfaffian \(\upsilon\), and a specified
reflection lift carrying the total section
\(\upsilon u\) to the reflected section. The tangential unit is
invariant in this comparison. The charts have root-pair and
section isomorphisms on overlaps, a trivialized sign defect on
triple overlaps, and compatible flag, Weyl, boundary and stage maps.
A proposed twelve-sign geometric parametrization is additional
data: it is a bijection from its actual geometric index set to
\(\{\pm1\}^{12}\), with the asserted multiplicities and coverage.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Transport of a simple skew wall]
\label{thm:G-O2-normal-form}
A wall atlas with the preceding Weyl transport has a simple
Pfaffian zero and residual reflection sign \(-1\) at every
transported type-II wall.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the normal frame, \(\operatorname{pf}(D_u)=u\).
The rank-two lift \(R=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\) satisfies
\(R^tD_uR=D_{-u}\) and has determinant \(-1\) on the
Pfaffian frame. Multiplication by the invariant tangential unit
preserves the simple zero and the sign. The supplied chart
isomorphisms preserve the divisor order, and character conjugacy
gives the same sign at \(ws_iw^{-1}\). A scalar complex
\([\mathbb C\xrightarrow{u}\mathbb C]\) has determinant \(u\)
and is not this skew normal block.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The two scalar counts]
\label{thm:G-R1-R2}
The abstract sign set \(\Omega_{12}=\{\pm1\}^{12}\) has
\(4096\) elements, and the normalized theta product satisfies
\[
 [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5=64,
 \qquad [qrs]\Delta_5^2=4096.
\]
A geometric wall index set has cardinality \(4096\) if it is
supplied with the bijection in \(O2_{\mathrm{atlas}}\).
The equality of the two integers does not construct that bijection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are two independent choices in each of twelve coordinates.
The theta coefficient is Lemma~\ref{lem:bkm-vacuum-coefficient};
squaring the lowest monomial gives \(64^2\). The geometric
assertion follows only from the stated bijection.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A count, a character and a scalar]
\label{rem:G-R1-R2}
The root-lattice quotient \(L_{\mathrm{II}}/2L_{\mathrm{II}}\)
has eight elements. It is neither \(\Omega_{12}\) nor a
constructed wall atlas. A sign character, a geometric multiplicity
and the normalization \(D_5=\Delta_5/64\) retain different data,
even when their resulting scalar formulas agree.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Gluing the transported wall sections]
\label{thm:G-O2-orbit-transport}
The overlap, stabilizer and action coherences of
\(O2_{\mathrm{atlas}}\) glue the transported wall sections on
the declared geometric recipient. A twelve-sign parametrization,
when supplied, retains its cardinality under this transport.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The overlap maps glue the lines and sections because their triple
composites are the identity after the chosen correction. Two Weyl
paths with the same endpoint differ by the specified stabilizer
comparison; its coherence identifies the transported sections.
This is the required path independence. An orbit-stabilizer count
without those geometric identifications would not prove it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The finite Pfaffian and the assembled criterion}

\begin{definition}[Finite geometric Pfaffian data]
\label{def:G-finite-pfaffian-section}
The datum \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is that of
Definition~\ref{def:finite-geometric-pfaffian-datum}: actual reduced
skew perfect complexes, root lines, Pfaffian sections and their
stage comparisons, together with an independently defined map to
the modular domain and line isomorphisms to
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{aut}}^5\otimes\varepsilon_5\).
The finite expansions include the full boundary factors and the
leading coefficient \(64\). Every inverse-limit or analytic
conclusion uses its stated exactness, convergence and continuation
hypotheses. A manufactured product of hyperbolic blocks gives the
formal reference section but not this geometric comparison.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The complete comparison datum]
\label{def:G-retained-obstruction-datum}
Write
\[
 \mathfrak R_X^{\mathrm{DI}}=
 (D0_{\mathrm{HN}},O1_{\mathrm{quot}},\mathcal T_W,
       O2_{\mathrm{atlas}},P_{\mathrm{fin}},Z_{\mathrm{HB}}),
\]
where \(\mathcal T_W\) is the coherent root transport just
specified and \(Z_{\mathrm{HB}}\) is the state and trace
comparison defined below. All components live on compatible
geometric systems. The first five concern the Pfaffian and its
orientation; the last is additional trace data.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Relative Pfaffian criterion]
\label{thm:G-four-obstruction-criterion}
Suppose the first five components of
\(\mathfrak R_X^{\mathrm{DI}}\) satisfy their comparison
conditions. Then the geometric Pfaffian has the root transport and
normalized automorphic section of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-pfaffian-dirac}. Its character on \(W\)
is \(\det_W\). Primitive recognition remains conditional on
\textup{(S1)}--\textup{(S10)}, and the trace requires
\(Z_{\mathrm{HB}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The D0 system supplies its specialization maps, quotient orientation
descends the roots, and Weyl transport identifies their wall actions.
The wall atlas glues the local skew sections. The independent
Pfaffian-line comparison and compatible normalized finite expansions
in \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) then identify the section with
\(\Delta_5\) on their declared formal or analytic domain.
The character assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:G-O1-plus}.
None of these steps constructs primitive representatives or a
cyclic state.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Algebras, centres and cyclic states}
\label{app:G-vol2-residual}

The coalgebra \(C_X\) and its associative cobar
\(A_X=\Omega C_X\) have different types. A boundary algebra
\(A_b\) can be compared with \(A_X\); its bar can be compared
with \(C_X\). A filtered quasi-isomorphism from an algebra to
that coalgebra is not an algebra comparison.

For an ordinary dg algebra, the derived centre is
\[
 Z^{\mathrm{der}}(A_b)
 =R\operatorname{Hom}_{A_b\otimes A_b^{\mathrm{op}}}(A_b,A_b).
\]
A chiral or factorization centre needs the corresponding enriched
bimodule category and internal operations. The ordinary formula
does not identify it with a physical bulk. The interface comparison
of \cite{ChiralBarCobarVolI} uses an actual coefficient-linear
strong monoidal equivalence with the required enrichment. Those
hypotheses, and both supported actions when a junction is used,
remain conditions on the compact source.

Even a nondegenerate degree \(-3\) pairing
\[
 Z\otimes Z\longrightarrow\mathbb C[-3]
\]
is a bilinear map, not a prepared state, a sewing operation or a
partition trace. A cyclic refinement and a state on which the
charged operators act must be supplied. The finite modes of
Chapter~\ref{ch:zbps-realization} illustrate the distinction:
the elliptic coefficient bundles and their geometric joining maps
exist before a compact boundary intertwiner is known.

\begin{definition}[A Hall--Borcherds trace comparison]
\label{def:G-vol2-hall-borcherds}
A trace comparison is an operation-preserving map from an
independently defined compact Hall state and its cyclic operations
to a specified boundary state, preserving charges, parity, support,
completion and normalization. If a centre acts on the boundary
state, its action and the compatibility of this map with that
action are part of the comparison. The resulting protected
partition function is the trace on the state, with its stated
vacuum and regularization conventions.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[A trace and an acting algebra]
\label{rem:G-level-Z-vs-gravity-line}
A trace comparison does not construct a gravity-line operator
algebra. Conversely, an acting algebra does not select a cyclic
state or a trace functional. Centre cochains, cyclic chains and
endomorphisms of the state have separate roles; no equality of
their scalar characters identifies them.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A conditional trace calculation}
\label{app:G-vol2-discharge}

\begin{definition}[A compatible cyclic state datum]
\label{def:G-level-Z-trace-datum}
The datum \(Z_{\mathrm{HB}}\) specifies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(Z1)}] The compact coalgebra \(C_X\), an associative
source \(A_X=\Omega C_X\), and a boundary algebra \(A_b\),
with actual filtered comparisons in the declared ordinary or
factorization recipient. Any bar comparison has coalgebra type.
\item[\textup{(Z2)}] The coefficient tensor, unit, support and
completion, the stage transition maps, and the convergence or
exactness needed for the asserted trace and limit.
\item[\textup{(Z3)}] The enriched centre comparison and its action
on an independently defined compact state \(\mathcal H_X\),
whenever a centre interpretation is required. Its hypotheses and
maps are supplied on that same recipient.
\item[\textup{(Z4)}] A cyclic chain functional or equivalent
specified cyclic structure, the chosen state or boundary condition,
and its sewing maps. A degree \(-3\) Calabi--Yau pairing, if
used, has a comparison to these maps; it does not replace them.
\item[\textup{(Z5)}] A charge- and parity-preserving comparison
of the completed state, or of a convergent associated graded, with
\[
 \widehat{\operatorname{Sym}}_{\mathrm{sup}}
       \left(\bigoplus_{\gamma>0}V_\gamma^{\oplus2}\right),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sdim}V_\gamma=f_\gamma,
\]
where each \(V_\gamma\) is a finite-dimensional super vector
space and \(f_\gamma\) is the exponent in the product for
\(D_5\) in Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic}. It intertwines the cyclic
trace and fixes the normal-ordering scalar and monomial to
\(64^{-2}x^{-2\rho}\), with
\(x^\rho=q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}\). The comparison retains the
axis factors and every relevant stage map.
\end{enumerate}
Clause \textup{(Z5)} is a substantive state comparison. It is not
implied by matching a cyclic pairing or by prescribing a desired
scalar partition function.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Trace from the state comparison]
\label{thm:G-vol2-discharge}
Given \(Z_{\mathrm{HB}}\), its normalized protected trace is
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{prot},\mathcal H_X}
       =64^{-2}x^{-2\rho}
          \prod_{\gamma>0}(1-x^\gamma)^{-2f_\gamma}
       =\Delta_5^{-2}
\]
in the stated cusp completion, and on any analytic domain to which
the supplied comparison continues it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An even oscillator of degree \(\gamma\) contributes
\((1-x^\gamma)^{-1}\) to the supertrace of its symmetric
algebra. An odd oscillator contributes \(1-x^\gamma\).
The doubled spaces in \textup{(Z5)} therefore contribute
\((1-x^\gamma)^{-2f_\gamma}\). Positive-height local
finiteness makes every fixed-height coefficient a finite
calculation. The state and cyclic comparisons identify this trace
with the geometric one; their convergence hypotheses justify any
associated-graded or analytic passage. The prescribed vacuum
monomial and scalar then give the inverse square of the complete
Borcherds product.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint Pfaffian and trace consequence]
\label{thm:G-pfaffian-dirac-relative}
If all components of \(\mathfrak R_X^{\mathrm{DI}}\) exist with
the stated compatibilities, the geometric Pfaffian section is
\(\Delta_5\), its \(W\)-character is \(\det_W\), and the
protected state trace is \(\Delta_5^{-2}\). In the separate
convention of Convention~\ref{conv:op-chamber}, the corresponding
OP scalar representative is \(-4096\Delta_5^{-2}\).
These conclusions do not assert primitive recognition or construct
a gravity-line operator algebra.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorems~\ref{thm:G-four-obstruction-criterion} and
\ref{thm:G-vol2-discharge} to their compatible data. The OP factor
is the declared change of scalar convention. Recognition needs
its presentation map, and an acting gravity algebra needs its
operator and module construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The additional input in a trace theorem]
\label{rem:G-conditioning-clean}
A centre theorem identifies a specified enriched endomorphism
object. An orientation theorem provides a root with coherent
transports. Neither theorem supplies \(\mathcal H_X\), the
cyclic functional, the sewing maps or the state comparison in
\textup{(Z5)}. These remain compact geometric constructions even
when the scalar Borcherds product is already known.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Compatibility and the remaining construction}
\label{app:G-retained-data-compatibility}
\label{app:G-relative-theorem}

The compatibility problem is to construct the preceding objects
on one system of compact moduli and correspondence stacks.
Orientation and critical-sheaf theorems apply on their stated
recipients. The fourfold orientation calculation of
\cite{CaoKoolMonavari} does not replace the threefold reduced
orientation problem. The reduced stable-pair comparison of
\cite{OberdieckK3xE} and the curve-counting formula of \cite{OB}
retain their numerical scope and normalization; they do not
construct a chiral state intertwiner.

The first missing coefficient map is the global reduced oriented
pull--push on compact extension flags, with the common translation
torsor and higher coherences. A coproduct needs its own support and
coefficient comparison. The ordinary-Koszul cooperation obstruction

After that construction, primitive recognition still asks for
actual representatives, relations, both parity dimensions,
invariant forms, radicals, generation and PBW compatibility.
The modular comparison still asks for the geometric line and
section. The trace comparison still asks for the cyclic state and
its sewing map. These are the maps by which the established local,
algebraic and automorphic constructions could become one compact
realization.

The standard current and conformal examples have their own domains:
affine Kac--Moody algebras at noncritical level, principal
\(W\)-algebras, Virasoro algebras, rational \(c=24\) models
including the Monster theory, and cosets with the required
finiteness hypotheses. A theorem on those objects does not place
the compact hybrid Hall recipient in its domain. The
Saito--Kurokawa arithmetic of Proposition~\ref{prop:zbps-sk-hecke}
and the selected input pairs of

automorphic comparisons at their separately stated scopes.
