\section{Jacobi coefficients and automorphic products}
\label{app:borcherds-product-expansion}

The product is determined by its Jacobi input, coefficient cone and
cusp normalization.  This appendix gives the explicit calculations
used in Chapters~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic} and
\ref{ch:singular-theta-lift}.  It then places the Igusa form in the
eight-form classification of diagonal-divisor forms, keeping the
Jacobi index, congruence level and character of each form.

\subsection{The Fourier coefficients of
\texorpdfstring{\(\phi_{0,1}\)}{phi01}}

Use \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\), \(r=e^{2\pi iz}\) and
\[
 \phi_{0,1}
 =4\sum_{a=2}^4\frac{\theta_a(\tau,z)^2}{\theta_a(\tau,0)^2}
 =\sum_{n\geq0,\ell\in\Z}f(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell.
\]
The genus-one theta series are those in
Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic}.
Their initial terms are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q^{-1/8}\theta_2(\tau,z)
 &=r^{1/2}+r^{-1/2}
   +q(r^{3/2}+r^{-3/2})+O(q^3),\\
 \theta_3(\tau,z)
 &=1+q^{1/2}(r+r^{-1})+q^2(r^2+r^{-2})+O(q^{9/2}),\\
 \theta_4(\tau,z)
 &=1-q^{1/2}(r+r^{-1})+q^2(r^2+r^{-2})+O(q^{9/2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The same expansions at \(r=1\) give the denominators.

For the first quotient, cancellation of \(q^{1/4}\) leaves
a denominator \(4(1+2q+q^2+O(q^3))\).
Its inverse through \(q^2\) is
\(\tfrac14(1-2q+3q^2)\).
For the other two quotients the terms with odd powers of
\(q^{1/2}\) cancel in their sum.

Put \(R_j=r^j+r^{-j}\).
Expansion gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &4\theta_2(\tau,z)^2/\theta_2(\tau,0)^2&
 4\theta_3(\tau,z)^2/\theta_3(\tau,0)^2+
 4\theta_4(\tau,z)^2/\theta_4(\tau,0)^2\\ \hline
 q^0&R_1+2&8\\
 q^1&2R_2-4&8R_2-64R_1+112\\
 q^2&R_3-4R_2-R_1+8&112R_2-512R_1+800
\end{array}
\]
and hence
\[
 \phi_{0,1}
 =R_1+10+q(10R_2-64R_1+108)
       +q^2(R_3+108R_2-513R_1+808)+O(q^3).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
 &\ell=-3&-2&-1&0&1&2&3\\ \hline
 n=0&0&0&1&10&1&0&0\\
 n=1&0&10&-64&108&-64&10&0\\
 n=2&1&108&-513&808&-513&108&1
\end{array}
\]
The first row fixes the Borcherds weight and divisor.
The next rows determine the first imaginary-root exponents.

The elliptic transformation law gives
\[
 f(n,\ell)=f(n+a\ell+a^2,\ell+2a),\qquad
 f(n,\ell)=f(n,-\ell).
\]
Reducing \(\ell\) to \(0\) or \(1\) shows that \(f(n,\ell)\)
depends only on \(4n-\ell^2\), and that it vanishes for
\(4n-\ell^2<-1\).
In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f(0,0)&=f(1,\pm2)=10,&
 f(1,0)&=f(2,\pm2)=108,\\
 f(0,\pm1)&=f(2,\pm3)=1,&
 f(1,\pm3)&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
In the theta decomposition
\(\phi_{0,1}=h_0\theta_{1,0}+h_1\theta_{1,1}\),
the functions \(h_0,h_1\) depend on \(\tau\) alone:
\[
 h_0=10+108q+808q^2+\cdots,\qquad
 h_1=q^{-1/4}(1-64q-513q^2+\cdots).
\]
The negative numbers are Euler superdimensions in the product
interpretation.  They do not determine the separate parity dimensions
of an arbitrary coefficient complex.

\subsection{The active cone and the simple walls}

For
\(Z=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}z_1&z_2\\z_2&z_3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\),
write \(q=e^{2\pi iz_1}\), \(r=e^{2\pi iz_2}\),
\(s=e^{2\pi iz_3}\).  The ordering convention is
\[
\begin{split}
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}={}&
 \{(n,\ell,m):m>0,\ n\geq0\}\\
 &{}\cup\{(n,\ell,0):n>0\}
 \cup\{(0,\ell,0):\ell<0\}.
\end{split}
\]
The unrestricted coordinate \(\ell\) is integral in each sector.
These sectors form a semigroup under addition.
Only indices in
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}
 =\{(n,\ell,m)\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}:f(nm,\ell)\ne0\}
\]
contribute nontrivial factors.

In the basis \((f_2,f_3,f_{-2})\), fix
\((f_2,f_{-2})=-1\), \(f_3^2=2\), with all other pairings zero.
The product-root map and its norm are
\[
 \alpha(n,\ell,m)=2nf_2-\ell f_3+2mf_{-2},\qquad
 \alpha(n,\ell,m)^2=2\ell^2-8nm.
\]
For
\(\delta_1=2f_2-f_3\), \(\delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3\),
\(\delta_3=f_3\), this becomes
\[
 \alpha(n,\ell,m)
 =n\delta_1+m\delta_2+(n+m-\ell)\delta_3.
\]

The support inequality \(\ell^2\leq4nm+1\) implies
\(n+m-\ell\geq0\) at every active index with \(n+m>0\).
The remaining active index is \((0,-1,0)\).
Thus the total root height
\[
 h=2(n+m)-\ell
\]
is positive on every factor, and only finitely many factors occur
below a fixed height.  The associated completed ring is
\(\Z[[qr,sr,r^{-1}]]\).
The larger ordering semigroup itself does not have finite
decomposition sets.

\begin{proposition}[Images of the simple type-II walls]
\label{prop:type-ii-wall-images}
The inverse images of the simple roots are
\[
 \gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}(\delta_1)=(1,1,0),\qquad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}(\delta_2)=(0,1,1),\qquad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}(\delta_3)=(0,-1,0).
\]
Their product exponents are all \(1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The coefficients in the simple-root basis are
\((n,m,n+m-\ell)\).
Setting these successively equal to \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\)
and \((0,0,1)\) gives the three triples.
Their exponents are \(f(0,1)\), \(f(0,1)\) and \(f(0,-1)\),
respectively.
\end{proof}

Some further product degrees illustrate the distinction between a
root norm and its signed multiplicity:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r}
 (n,\ell,m)&\alpha(n,\ell,m)^2&f(nm,\ell)\\ \hline
 (1,1,0)&2&1\\
 (0,1,1)&2&1\\
 (0,-1,0)&2&1\\
 (0,0,m),\ m\geq1&0&10\\
 (1,0,1)&-8&108\\
 (1,1,1)&-6&-64\\
 (1,2,1)&0&10\\
 (1,3,1)&10&0\\
 (2,1,1)&-14&-513\\
 (1,2,2)&-8&108
\end{array}
\]
The inactive row \((1,3,1)\) is allowed by the ordering
convention but contributes no factor.
The active set need not itself be an additive monoid.
Its containment in the positive simple-root monoid is what makes
the product and its logarithm well-defined.

\subsection{The product and its theta phase}

\begin{theorem}[Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin product]
\label{thm:borcherds-product-delta5}
In the theta convention
\[
 \nu_{a,b}(Z)=
 \sum_{u\in\Z^2}e^{\pi i((u+a/2)^tZ(u+a/2)+b^tu)},\qquad
 \Delta_5=\prod_{a^tb\ \mathrm{even}}\nu_{a,b},
\]
one has
\[
 \Delta_5(Z)
 =64(qrs)^{1/2}
 \prod_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}
                (1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
The product is a formal total-height expansion and an absolutely
convergent analytic expansion sufficiently near the standard cusp in
the type-II chamber.  Its continuation is the holomorphic
weight-five cusp form with Maass character.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Jacobi product theorem applies to \(\phi_{0,1}\)
\cite[Theorem 2.1 and Example 2.4]{GNII}.
The classical product identification, with the theta normalization
in the statement, is \cite[Theorem 4.1]{GN}.
The active-cone argument above gives the formal completion.
The six nonconstant theta factors have leading coefficients
\[
 2q^{1/8},\ 2q^{1/8},\
 2s^{1/8},\ 2s^{1/8},\
 2q^{1/8}s^{1/8}(r^{1/4}+r^{-1/4}),\
 2q^{1/8}s^{1/8}(r^{1/4}-r^{-1/4}).
\]
Together with the other four leading constants, they give
\(64q^{1/2}s^{1/2}(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2})\).
This fixes the coefficient \(64\) and its sign.
\end{proof}

The monic form is \(D_5=\Delta_5/64\).
If theta characteristics are instead defined with phase
\(b^t(u+a/2)\), the product of the ten constants is
\(-\Delta_5\).  The square and the divisor are unchanged.
The sign of the square-root section is fixed only after this phase
convention is specified.

For analytic convergence, the chamber inequalities are
\[
 y_2<0,\qquad y_1+y_2>0,\qquad y_3+y_2>0,
 \qquad y_j=\Imag z_j.
\]
The product theorem applies sufficiently far toward the cusp within
this chamber.  In particular it applies on a neighborhood of
\((y_1,y_2,y_3)=(T,-T/2,T)\) for large \(T\).
The frequently used ordering
\(0<|s|\ll|q|\ll|r|^{-1}\ll1\) selects a further subregion.
Its interpretation does not replace the support or convergence
hypotheses.

\subsection{The coefficient 93}

The leading coefficient of \(\Delta_5\) and the first interior
coefficient of its normalized product are different quantities.
Put
\[
 P(q,r,s)=\frac{D_5}{(qrs)^{1/2}}.
\]
Its constant coefficient in \(q,s\) is \(1-r^{-1}\).

To compute the coefficient of \(qs\), only the factors of
\(q\)-degree and \(s\)-degree at most one are needed.
Let \(A=r+10+r^{-1}\).  The boundary factors give
\((1-qA)(1-sA)\) to these degrees.  The mixed factors give
\[
 1-qs\bigl(10(r^2+r^{-2})-64(r+r^{-1})+108\bigr).
\]
Therefore
\[
 [qs]P=(1-r^{-1})
 \bigl(-9(r^2+r^{-2})+84(r+r^{-1})-6\bigr),
 \qquad [qrs]P=93.
\]

The factor \((1-qrs)^{-64}\) contributes \(64\) to this
coefficient.  The other factors contribute \(29\).

The same calculation is particularly transparent in root variables
\(x_1=qr\), \(x_2=sr\), \(x_3=r^{-1}\):
\[
 P=
 \prod_{i=1}^3(1-x_i)
 \prod_{i<j}(1-10x_ix_j)
 (1+64x_1x_2x_3)
 \pmod{(x_1^2,x_2^2,x_3^2)}.
\]
Thus
\[
 [x_ix_j]P=-9,\qquad
 [x_1x_2x_3]P=-1+30+64=93.
\]

The standard minimal Gritsenko--Nikulin presentation assigns nine
even imaginary simple generators to each primitive null pair.
At the first interior root
\(\beta=\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3\), the coefficient \(93\)
gives \(93\) odd imaginary simple generators.

The earlier even generators contribute \(29\) even vectors.
Two are multilinear brackets of the real generators.
The other \(3\cdot9\) bracket a null simple generator with the
omitted real generator.  The first real Serre relations have
height four.
Consequently the whole root space has parity dimensions
\[
 \dim\autcor_\beta^{\bar0}=29,\qquad
 \dim\autcor_\beta^{\bar1}=93,\qquad
 \operatorname{sdim}\autcor_\beta=-64.
\]

This uses the defining Lie presentation, not just the signed product
exponent.  Its construction is given in
Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}.

In Gritsenko--Nikulin's Fourier notation
\(e^{\pi i(nz_1+\ell z_2+mz_3)}\), the coefficient with
\((n,\ell,m)=(1,1,1)\) is \(64\)
\cite[(1.6)--(1.7)]{GN}.
In the present \(q,r,s\) notation that is
\([q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5\).
It is consistent with the coefficient \(93\) of \(P\).

\subsection{Fourier--Jacobi and separating factors}

Factoring all \(m=0\) terms gives
\[
 D_5=s^{1/2}b(q,r)
 \prod_{m>0,\ n\geq0,\ \ell\in\Z}
                (1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)},
\]
where
\[
 b(q,r)=q^{1/2}(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2})
 \prod_{a\geq1}(1-q^a)^{10}(1-rq^a)(1-r^{-1}q^a)
 =\eta(\tau)^9\vartheta(\tau,z).
\]
The odd Jacobi theta function \(\vartheta\) is normalized by this
product and has first term \(q^{1/8}(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2})\).
The squared boundary factor is
\[
 \mathcal B(q,r)=b(q,r)^2
 =q(r+r^{-1}-2)
   \prod_{a\geq1}(1-q^a)^{20}(1-rq^a)^2(1-r^{-1}q^a)^2.
\]
These are the factors needed in the normalized half and full
elliptic-index series.

The separating limit is
\[
 D_5=
 2\pi iz_2\,\eta(z_1)^{12}\eta(z_3)^{12}+O(z_2^3).
\]
It follows by dividing out \(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2}\), pairing the
\(\ell\) and \(-\ell\) factors, and using
\(\phi_{0,1}(\tau,0)=12\).
The quotient is even in \(z_2\), which gives the stated cubic
remainder.

Squaring yields
\[
 \Xi=D_5^2
 =(2\pi iz_2)^2\eta(z_1)^{24}\eta(z_3)^{24}+O(z_2^4).
\]
The product with leading coefficient one is \(\Xi\), the
\(\chi_{10}\) of \cite{OPand,OB}.
The theta square is \(\Delta_5^2=4096\,\Xi\).
Accordingly the primitive reduced scalar series is
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{OP}}^X=-\Xi^{-1}=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The expansion prescriptions and primitive-class hypotheses are those
of Chapter~\ref{ch:pentadic}.

\subsection{The two denominator exponentials}

\begin{lemma}[Two exponential normalizations]
\label{lem:appB-doubling}
For
\(\zeta=z_3f_2+z_2f_3+z_1f_{-2}\),
\[
 q^nr^\ell s^m=e^{-\pi i(\alpha(n,\ell,m),\zeta)_L}.
\]
The denominator written with
\(e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,\zeta)_L}\) is
\[
 \operatorname{den}(\autcor)(Z)=D_5(2Z)
 =64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The pairing is
\((\alpha(n,\ell,m),\zeta)_L
=-2(nz_1+\ell z_2+mz_3)\), proving the first formula.
The Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator theorem uses the doubled
exponential for each root and for
\(\rho=f_2-\tfrac12f_3+f_{-2}\)
\cite[\S3]{GN}.
Substitution \(Z\mapsto2Z\) makes precisely this change.
\end{proof}

In these coordinates the additive denominator is
\[
 \sum_{w\in W^{(2)}(M_{II})}\det(w)
 \left(e^{-2\pi i(w\rho,\zeta)_L}
 -\sum_a m(a)e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho+a),\zeta)_L}\right).
\]
For a primitive isotropic ray \(a\), its coefficients are related by
\[
 1-\sum_{t\geq1}m(ta)u^t
 =\prod_{t\geq1}(1-u^t)^{\tau(ta)}.
\]
The numbers \(m(a)\) on negative-norm rays and \(\tau(ta)\)
on null rays specify the imaginary simple-root data.
They enter the Lie presentation in addition to the real-root
Cartan matrix.

The determinant signs in this alternating sum belong to the linear
tube action.  On a chosen symplectic reflection lift,
the weight-five factor \(j(g,Z)^5\) must also be included.
Lemma~\ref{lem:view1-maass-multiplier-restriction} computes the
resulting relation between the tube character \(\varepsilon_5\)
and the Maass character \(\nu_5\).

\subsection{The eight diagonal-divisor forms}

For positive integers \(t,N\), let \(\Gamma_t(N)\) be the
Hecke congruence subgroup of the paramodular group:
\[
 \Gamma_t(N)=
 \left\{
 \begin{pmatrix}
 *&t*&*&*\\
 *&*&*&t^{-1}*\\
 N*&Nt*&*&*\\
 Nt*&Nt*&t*&*
 \end{pmatrix}\in\Sp_4(\Q):\text{each }*\in\Z
 \right\}.
\]
In particular \(\Gamma_1(1)=\Sp_4(\Z)\), and
\(\Gamma_1(N)=\Gamma_0^{(2)}(N)\).
The integer \(t\) is the polarization type and the index of the
weight-zero Jacobi input.
The integer \(N\) is the congruence level.
A diagonal-divisor form has precisely the
\(\Gamma_t(N)\)-translates of \(z_2=0\) as its zero divisor,
each with multiplicity one.

Cl\'ery--Gritsenko classify eight such forms, up to scalar
\cite[Theorem 1.4]{GC}.
The table uses their names and the order of the character or
multiplier on \(\Gamma_t(N)\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c}
 j&F_j&(t,N)&k_j&\operatorname{ord}(\nu_j)&c_j^{\mathrm{tot}}\\ \hline
 1&\Delta_5&(1,1)&5&2&10\\
 2&\Delta_2&(2,1)&2&4&4\\
 3&\Delta_1&(3,1)&1&6&2\\
 4&\Delta_{1/2}&(4,1)&\tfrac12&8&1\\
 5&\nabla_3&(1,2)&3&2&6\\
 6&\nabla_2&(1,3)&2&2&4\\
 7&\nabla_{3/2}&(1,4)&\tfrac32&4&3\\
 8&Q_1&(2,2)&1&4&2
\end{array}
\]
The final column denotes the total constant coefficient entering
the weight formula, so \(k_j=c_j^{\mathrm{tot}}/2\).
Its meaning at congruence level is specified below.
The monic convention for the first form is \(D_5\).
Our symbol \(\Delta_5\) restores the factor \(64\).

In this order the weights and total constants are
\[
 (5,2,1,\tfrac12,3,2,\tfrac32,1),\qquad
 (10,4,2,1,6,4,3,2).
\]
If the pairs are instead ordered
\[
 (1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(3,1),(1,3),(4,1),(1,4),(2,2),
\]
the same columns read
\[
 (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),\qquad
 (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
Keeping the parameter pair prevents an interchange of the
paramodular and congruence families.

\subsubsection{The first Fourier--Jacobi coefficients}
With monic leading terms, the eight Jacobi coefficients are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 F&b_F(\tau,z)&\text{Jacobi-parabolic multiplier}\\ \hline
 D_5&\eta(\tau)^9\vartheta(\tau,z)&v_\eta^{12}\times v_H\\
 \Delta_2&\eta(\tau)^3\vartheta(\tau,z)&v_\eta^6\times v_H\\
 \Delta_1&\eta(\tau)\vartheta(\tau,z)&v_\eta^4\times v_H\\
 \Delta_{1/2}&\vartheta(\tau,z)&v_\eta^3\times v_H\\
 \nabla_3&\eta(\tau)\eta(2\tau)^4\vartheta(\tau,z)&\chi_2^{(2)}\times v_H\\
 \nabla_2&\eta(3\tau)^3\vartheta(\tau,z)&\chi_2^{(3)}\times v_H\\
 \nabla_{3/2}&\dfrac{\eta(2\tau)\eta(4\tau)^2}{\eta(\tau)}
                    \vartheta(\tau,z)&\chi_4^{(4)}\times v_H\\[5pt]
 Q_1&\dfrac{\eta(2\tau)^2}{\eta(\tau)}
                    \vartheta(\tau,z)&\chi_4^{(2)}\times v_H
\end{array}
\]
These coefficients and their lifts are given in
\cite[(13)--(19), \S3]{GC}.
The symbol \(\chi_d^{(N)}\) denotes the order-\(d\) character
or multiplier determined by the displayed seed on \(\Gamma_0(N)\).
The first coefficient of the theta-normalized \(\Delta_5\) is
\(64b_{D_5}\).

For clarity, use Heisenberg elements
\[
 [\lambda,\mu;\kappa]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0&\mu\\
 \lambda&1&\mu&\kappa\\
 0&0&1&-\lambda\\
 0&0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \lambda,\mu,\kappa\in\Z.
\]
Their character on the extended index-\(\tfrac12\) theta
function \(\vartheta(\tau,z)e^{\pi i\sigma}\) is
\[
 v_H([\lambda,\mu;\kappa])
 =(-1)^{\lambda+\mu+\lambda\mu+\kappa}.
\]
Indeed \(z\) changes by \(\lambda\tau+\mu\), and \(\sigma\)
changes by
\(\lambda^2\tau+2\lambda z+\lambda\mu+\kappa\).
The odd theta transformation cancels the first two terms in the
latter exponential and supplies \((-1)^{\lambda+\mu}\).
This also specifies the convention for every \(v_H\) in the table.

\subsubsection{Products at paramodular level}
For \(t=1,2,3,4\), the index-\(t\) weak Jacobi inputs have
constant rows
\[
 r+10+r^{-1},\quad r+4+r^{-1},\quad
 r+2+r^{-1},\quad r+1+r^{-1},
\]
respectively.

Besides \(\phi_{0,1}\), they can be written as
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \phi_{0,4}(\tau,z)&=\frac{\vartheta(\tau,3z)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)},\\
 \phi_{0,3}(\tau,z)&=
       \left(\frac{\vartheta(\tau,2z)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}\right)^2,\\
 \phi_{0,2}(\tau,z)&=
 \frac{\vartheta(\tau,2z)^4}
      {\vartheta(\tau,z)^3\vartheta(\tau,3z)}
 +\frac{\vartheta(\tau,4z)\vartheta(\tau,z)}
      {\vartheta(\tau,2z)\vartheta(\tau,3z)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last expression follows by substituting the first two into
\[
 \phi_{0,2}\phi_{0,4}-\phi_{0,3}^2
 =\frac{\vartheta(\tau,4z)}{\vartheta(\tau,2z)}.
\]
The apparent poles cancel in these weak Jacobi forms
\cite[Lemma 2.5 and (2.10), (2.24)]{GNII}.
Their initial coefficients satisfy
\[
 \phi_{0,2}=r+4+r^{-1}
 +q\bigl(r^3+r^{-3}-8(r^2+r^{-2})-(r+r^{-1})+16\bigr)+O(q^2).
\]

Write
\(\phi_{0,t}=\sum f_t(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell\).
Their monic products are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_2&=q^{1/4}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
       \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^{2m})^{f_2(nm,\ell)},\\
 \Delta_1&=q^{1/6}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
       \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^{3m})^{f_3(nm,\ell)},\\
 \Delta_{1/2}&=q^{1/8}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
       \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^{4m})^{f_4(nm,\ell)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \((n,\ell,m)>0\) is the cusp ordering, with zero exponents
omitted.  These identities are
\cite[(2.11), (2.20)--(2.21)]{GNII}.

They extend to \(\Gamma_t^+\), generated by \(\Gamma_t\)
and the Fricke involution, with the specified multipliers.
The projective Fricke action is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}z_1&z_2\\z_2&z_3\end{pmatrix}
 \longmapsto
 \begin{pmatrix}tz_3&z_2\\z_2&z_1/t\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\subsubsection{The constant terms at all cusps}
Let \(\phi\in J_{0,t}^{!}(\Gamma_0(N))\) be holomorphic on
\(\mathbb H_1\times\C\) and meromorphic at the cusps.
Choose a scaling matrix \(M_{f/e}\) for each cusp \(f/e\),
with \(e\mid N\).
Put
\[
 h_e=\frac{N}{\gcd(e^2,N)},\qquad N_e=\frac Ne,\qquad
 \phi|_{0,t}M_{f/e}
 =\sum_{n\in h_e^{-1}\Z,\ \ell\in\Z}
             c_{f/e}(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell.
\]
The width is \(h_e\).

Assume at each cusp that
\[
 \frac{h_e}{N_e}c_{f/e}(u,\ell)\in\Z
 \qquad\text{whenever }4tu-\ell^2\leq0.
\]

Cl\'ery--Gritsenko's product theorem \cite[Theorem 3.1]{GC}
then gives a meromorphic modular form on
\(\Gamma_t(N)^+=\langle\Gamma_t(N),V_t\rangle\), where
\(V_t\) is the Fricke involution above.
Its weight is
\[
 k=\frac12c^{\mathrm{tot}},\qquad
 c^{\mathrm{tot}}
 =\sum_{f/e}\frac{h_e}{N_e}\,c_{f/e}(0,0).
\]
Its factors are
\[
 B_\phi=q^Ar^Bs^C
 \prod_{f/e}\prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}
 \left(1-(q^nr^\ell s^{tm})^{N_e}\right)^{
               (h_e/N_e)c_{f/e}(nm,\ell)}.
\]
For this possibly polar input, \((n,\ell,m)>0\) means
\(m>0\) with \(n,\ell\in\Z\), or \(m=0,n>0\), or
\(m=n=0,\ell<0\).  For a weak input the nonzero factors have
\(n\geq0\).

The cusp prefactors are
\[
 A=\frac1{24}\sum_{f/e,\ell}h_e c_{f/e}(0,\ell),\quad
 B=\frac12\sum_{f/e,\ell>0}\ell h_e c_{f/e}(0,\ell),\quad
 C=\frac14\sum_{f/e,\ell}\ell^2 h_e c_{f/e}(0,\ell).
\]
The singular coefficients at all cusps specify the divisor.
The complete cusp expansions also determine the multiplier and weight.

For the four congruence forms, the constant contributions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 F&\text{cusps}&c_{f/e}(0,0)&c^{\mathrm{tot}}\\ \hline
 \nabla_3&\infty,\ 0&2,\ 4&2+4=6\\
 \nabla_2&\infty,\ 0&1,\ 3&1+3=4\\
 \nabla_{3/2}&\infty,\ 1/2,\ 0&0,\ 2,\ 2&0+\tfrac12(2)+2=3\\
 Q_1&\infty,\ 0&0,\ 2&0+2=2
\end{array}
\]
These are the inputs of \cite[\S3]{GC}.
In particular \(\nabla_3\) has weight \(3\) although its
constant coefficient at infinity is \(2\).
The single infinity coefficient suffices when \(N=1\), but does
not give the congruence weight.

\subsubsection{Half-integral automorphy}
The orders \(8\) and \(4\) in the table are orders of multiplier
systems.  They do not specify the degree of a covering space.
For a half-integral weight, one can choose holomorphic square roots
of \(j(g,Z)=\det(CZ+D)\) and retain their multiplication cocycle
in the multiplier.  Equivalently, use the group
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma
 =\{(g,\varphi):g\in\Gamma,\ \varphi(Z)^2=j(g,Z)\},
\]
with product
\((g,\varphi)(h,\psi)=(gh,(\varphi\circ h)\psi)\).
On this cover the half-integral automorphy factor is single-valued.

This is an output group acting on Siegel space.
The metaplectic input group \(\Mp_2(\Z)\) acts on the Jacobi
theta components and must be specified separately.

\subsection{Denominator algebras and CHL parameters}

The first four paramodular forms have the
Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator presentations
\cite[\S5.1.1]{GNII}.
The other four forms also occur with specified root data in
Govindarajan's twisted-CHL constructions
\cite[\S4 and Table 3]{Gov11BKM}.
For CHL orbifold orders one, two and three, the dyon interpretation
is developed in \cite{CD09BKM}.

To relate the two parameter systems, write \(N_{\mathrm{orb}}\)
for the CHL orbifold order and \(M_{\mathrm{tw}}\) for the
twisting order.  For the entries with
\(N_{\mathrm{orb}}\leq M_{\mathrm{tw}}\), the catalog pair is
\[
 t=\gcd(N_{\mathrm{orb}},M_{\mathrm{tw}}),\qquad
 N=\frac{M_{\mathrm{tw}}}{t}.
\]
The dictionary is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 F&(N_{\mathrm{orb}},M_{\mathrm{tw}})
       &\text{number of real simple roots}\\ \hline
 \Delta_5&(1,1)&3\\
 \Delta_2&(2,2)&4\\
 \Delta_1&(3,3)&6\\
 \Delta_{1/2}&(4,4)&\text{countably infinite}\\
 \nabla_3&(1,2)&3\\
 \nabla_2&(1,3)&3\\
 \nabla_{3/2}&(1,4)&3\\
 Q_1&(2,4)&4
\end{array}
\]
The root lattice has rank three even when the set of real simple
roots is larger.  At fixed \(N_{\mathrm{orb}}\) the Weyl
vector and real-root system agree, while the imaginary
multiplicities vary with \(M_{\mathrm{tw}}\).

The forms at exchanged CHL parameters are related by a change of
cusp and can have different denominator chambers
\cite[(3.12), \S4]{Gov11BKM}.
The Weyl chamber, parity prescription and additive denominator must
therefore accompany the product.

The half-weight \(3/2\) associated with the cyclic order-four CHL
eta product is consistent with the preceding table.
For cyclic orders \(1,2,3,4,6\), let \(g_N\) be the eta products
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|c}
 N&g_N(\tau)&\operatorname{wt}(g_N)\\ \hline
 1&\eta(\tau)^{24}&12\\
 2&\eta(\tau)^8\eta(2\tau)^8&8\\
 3&\eta(\tau)^6\eta(3\tau)^6&6\\
 4&\eta(\tau)^4\eta(2\tau)^2\eta(4\tau)^4&5\\
 6&\eta(\tau)^2\eta(2\tau)^2\eta(3\tau)^2\eta(6\tau)^2&4
\end{array}
\]
Multiplication by the Jacobi factor \(\vartheta^2/\eta^6\)
subtracts two from the weight, as in the CHL lifting prescription
\cite[Table 1 and \S3.2]{GK10Composite}.
Half of the resulting seed weights is
\[
 5,\quad3,\quad2,\quad\frac32,\quad1.
\]
This eta-weight calculation does not identify all these parameters
with the pairs \((t,N)\), nor does it construct a compact Hall
source for any row.

\subsection{The weight and the geometric source}

For each form in the catalog, define
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(F_j)=\operatorname{wt}(F_j)\).
Then the precise common weight identity is
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(F_j)=\frac{c_j^{\mathrm{tot}}}{2}.
\]
For the Igusa input, \(c_1^{\mathrm{tot}}=f(0,0)=10\).

The number \(5\) is a coordinate determined by the automorphic
construction.
The categorical and Hodge quantities attached to \(S\times E\)
come from other constructions.
So do the rank \(3\) of the root-degree lattice,
\(24=e(S)\), and \(2=\chi(\mathcal O_S)\).
A normalized Heisenberg trace may introduce another comparison
invariant.  Its identification with a lattice rank requires the
comparison data stated in Chapter~\ref{ch:pentadic}.
The numerical equality \(3+2=5\) does not replace the product
weight formula.

The catalog supplies automorphic lines, sections and, with the
cited root presentations, denominator algebras.
The primitive reduced \(S\times E\) counting theorem supplies the
scalar \(-\Xi^{-1}\) for the first form.
Constructing a compact source with the appropriate coefficient
complexes, Hall operations, reduction, orientation and state is an
additional requirement.  The conditional Pfaffian criteria in
Appendix~\ref{app:obstruction-discharges} retain those data.
The comparison must preserve the complete boundary factor, the
character and the divisor multiplicities.
It must also preserve the exponential convention and the
normalized section.
