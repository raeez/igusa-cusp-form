\section{Pfaffian crossings and reflection characters}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-normal-form}

A simple zero of a Pfaffian changes sign under reflection of its
normal coordinate. To interpret that sign as the action on an
orientation line, one must specify a lift of the reflection and
require it to preserve the section. This appendix gives the local
skew model, compares its sign with the automorphic character, and
states the gluing needed for a global compact comparison.

\subsection{The three type-II walls}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-three-walls}

Use the root lattice
\[
 L=\mathbb Zf_2\oplus\mathbb Zf_3\oplus\mathbb Zf_{-2},
 \qquad(f_2,f_{-2})=-1,\quad f_3^2=2,
\]
with other pairings zero. Its type-II sublattice is
\[
 L_{\mathrm{II}}=
 \{mf_2+lf_3+nf_{-2}:m,n\in2\mathbb Z\}
 \simeq U(4)\oplus\langle2\rangle.
\]
It has index four in \(L\) and determinant \(-32\).
The three simple roots and their Gram matrix are
\[
 \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\quad
 \delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\quad \delta_3=f_3,
 \qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2&-2\\-2&2&-2\\-2&-2&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The eigenvalues \(4,4,-2\) give signature \((2,1)\).
For a root \(\delta\), reflection is
\(s_\delta(x)=x-(x,\delta)\delta\).
The Weyl vector is
\[
 \rho=\tfrac12(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
       =f_2-\tfrac12f_3+f_{-2},
 \qquad (\rho,\delta_i)=-1.
\]
These formulas use the same lattice and active cone as
Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}.

The walls bound an ideal hyperbolic triangle. Its reflection group
\(W=W^{(2)}(L_{\mathrm{II}})\) has presentation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appE-free-product}
 W=\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\mid s_i^2=1\rangle,
 \qquad s_i=s_{\delta_i}
\end{equation}
\cite[Proposition 2.2]{GN}. A product of distinct simple
reflections is parabolic, not hyperbolic. On their two-dimensional
root span its matrix is
\[
 s_is_j=\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix}
       =I+\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\\2&-2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad (s_is_j-I)^2=0.
\]
It has infinite order because its \(n\)-th power is
\(I+n(s_is_j-I)\).

The type-I normals
\(f_{-2}-f_2,f_2-f_3,f_{-2}-f_3\) have divisibility one in
\(L\); their reflections give the chamber symmetry group \(S_3\).
The \(\delta_i\) have divisibility two. The index six of the
reflection subgroup in the full norm-two reflection group is
independent of the index four of the lattices.
The denominator on \(L_{\mathrm{II}}\) is
\(64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\). Doubling changes the exponential
coordinates as in Proposition~\ref{prop:view1-GN-doubled-denominator};
it does not change these integral indices.

\subsection{One normal Pfaffian factor}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-rank-one}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-sign-computation}

Take a complex normal chart \(U=T_0\times\mathbb A^1_u\), or its
analytic analogue, with base reflection \(s(t,u)=(t,-u)\).
Let \(V=\mathcal O_U^2\) and put
\[
 D_u:V\longrightarrow V^\vee,
 \qquad D_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The complex \(K_D=[V\xrightarrow{D_u}V^\vee]\) lies in degrees
\([-1,0]\). Its Pfaffian line and section are
\[
 \mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf}}=\det V^\vee,
 \qquad \operatorname{pf}(D_u)=u,
 \qquad \operatorname{pf}(D_u)^2=\det D_u=u^2
\]
in the chosen frame. There is one hyperbolic Pfaffian factor;
the skew matrix has rank two away from the wall. The scalar complex
\([\mathcal O\xrightarrow{u}\mathcal O]\) has determinant section
\(u\), so it is a different determinant problem.

A compatible reflection lift is explicit. With
\(R=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\),
\[
 R^tD_uR=D_{-u},\qquad \det R=-1.
\]
Hence the induced comparison of Pfaffian frames has sign \(-1\).
An identity lift on the rank-two bundle would fail the first
identity. For a tangential skew factor, assume its Pfaffian has a
nonvanishing reference section \(\upsilon\) fixed by the chosen
comparison. The total local coefficient is then \(\upsilon u\).

\begin{proposition}[Sign of a section-compatible crossing]
\label{prop:appE-local-pfaffian-sign}
In this model, pullback by the base reflection changes the
Pfaffian expression by \(-1\) and preserves its square:
\[
 s^*(\upsilon u)=-\upsilon u,
 \qquad s^*((\upsilon u)^2)=(\upsilon u)^2.
\]
Relative to the specified reference frames, a lift carrying the
section to its pullback has residual sign
\(\epsilon_o^{\mathrm{loc}}(s)=(-1)^{N^{\mathrm{Pf}}}=-1\), where
\(N^{\mathrm{Pf}}=1\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Pfaffian of the displayed matrix is \(u\). Pullback replaces
\(u\) by \(-u\) and leaves \(\upsilon\) fixed. The transformation
law \(\operatorname{Pf}(R^tDR)=\det(R)\operatorname{Pf}(D)\)
identifies this change with the stated lift. Squaring removes the
sign.
\end{proof}

The functional identity is not, by itself, a construction of that
lift on a geometric orientation line. Nor does a real path through
\(u=0\) compute monodromy in the wall complement. The skew model,
tangential unit and section comparison are the hypotheses of the
local assertion.

\subsection{The automorphic character}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-maass-match}

Let \(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}\) be the homogeneous orthogonal
character of Definition~\ref{def:homogeneous-orthogonal-character}.
It retains both the weight-five line and its modular character.

\begin{lemma}[Values on the six reflections]
\label{lem:appE-maass-on-type-ii}
The values of \(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}\) are \(-1\) on the
three simple type-II reflections and \(+1\) on the three type-I
chamber reflections. Multiplying \(\Delta_5\) by a nonzero constant
changes none of these values.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The symplectic lifts in
Lemma~\ref{lem:view1-maass-multiplier-restriction} satisfy
\(\sigma(g_\beta)=-s_\beta\) and
\(j(g_\beta,Z)=-1\). Maass's formula
\cite[(1.5)]{GN} gives modular character \(+1\) on the type-II
lifts and \(-1\) on the type-I lifts. Homogeneity of degree
\(-5\) gives
\(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}(-I)=-1\), reversing the signs for
\(s_\beta\). These are the linear reflection signs of
\cite[Proposition 2.1]{GN}. A scalar cancels from every
transformation law.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local orientation comparison]
\label{thm:appE-local-orientation-match}
Suppose each simple type-II wall has the section-compatible skew
crossing above. Then, in those reference comparisons,
\[
 \epsilon_o^{\mathrm{loc}}(s_i)=-1
       =\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}(s_i),\qquad 1\le i\le3.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:appE-local-pfaffian-sign} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:appE-maass-on-type-ii} to each generator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From three local signs to an action}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-W2-extension}

The group \(W\) has a determinant character \(\det_W\), taking
value \(-1\) on every \(s_i\). Equation~\eqref{eq:appE-free-product}
shows directly that these assignments respect its relations.
The preceding lemma proves
\[
 \widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}|_W=\det_W.
\]
To compare a geometric orientation with this character, local lifts
must first form an action on the same root pair.

For chosen lifts \(\phi_w\) of \(w\in W\), their composition
defect is
\[
 c(w,v)=\phi_w\,w^*(\phi_v)\,\phi_{wv}^{-1}.
\]
When all lifts preserve the specified square map, this defect is
\(\mu_2\)-valued. On a connected base with constant coefficients
it is a group two-cocycle. More generally its coefficients are
locally constant signs on the relevant components, with the induced
group action. A one-cochain \(\eta\) with
\(\delta\eta=c\) replaces the lifts by coherent ones. This
cochain equation does not determine their character: different
coherent lifts can differ by a character.

Assume further that the coherent action has constant scalar values
in the reference comparisons and that it preserves the local
Pfaffian sections. Those values give a homomorphism
\(\epsilon_o:W\to\{\pm1\}\). Since its generator values are
\(-1\),
\[
 \epsilon_o=\det_W=\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}|_W.
\]
This is the conditional global conclusion. It uses the action,
reference comparisons and sections, rather than just an equality
of determinant squares.

\subsection{The global wall-atlas condition}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-O2}

\begin{definition}[Compatible wall atlas]
\label{def:appE-O2}
For each finite geometric stage \(R\), a compatible type-II wall
atlas consists of charts \(U_{w,i,R}\), for \(w\in W\) and
\(1\le i\le3\), with skew-complex comparisons to the normal model
above; Pfaffian-line and section isomorphisms on overlaps; and
coherent lifts of the Weyl action. The charts cover the specified
geometric locus and its required boundary pieces. Their comparisons
commute with the transition maps in \(R\).

Equivalently, when lifts are initially projective, include their
actual sign two-cocycle and a one-cochain trivializing it. That
trivialization must agree on components, boundaries and overlaps,
and preserve the local section comparisons.
\end{definition}

The condition is denoted \((O2_{\mathrm{atlas}})\).
Its local hypotheses can be expressed by the chart, wall, splitting,
tangential-unit and normal-rank residuals
\[
 \mathfrak o^{\mathrm{chart}}_{\delta,R},\quad
 \mathfrak o^{\mathrm{wall}}_{\delta,R},\quad
 \mathfrak o^{\mathrm{split}}_{\delta,R},\quad
 \mathfrak o^{\mathrm{unit}}_{\delta,R},\quad
 \mathfrak o^{\mathrm{rank}}_{\delta,R}.
\]
Their vanishing means that the corresponding geometric maps exist
with the stated comparisons. The transition residual
\(\mathfrak o^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\delta,R'R}\) additionally requires
commutativity with the stage maps. A local zero in one chart gives
neither a global covering nor a trivialization of the global cocycle.

Together with quotient orientation and coherent Weyl transport,
this atlas yields the character comparison above. The conditional
transport statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:G-O2-orbit-transport} uses
these hypotheses. Constructing them on the compact reduced Hall
locus remains a geometric problem.

\subsection{The two numerical appearances of \(4096\)}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-O2-4096}

The abstract set \(\Omega_{12}=\{\pm1\}^{12}\) has
\(2^{12}=4096\) elements. A geometric interpretation as a count of
wall configurations requires an actual bijection with this set,
including the identifications, coverage and stabilizer weights.
Definition~\ref{def:appE-O2} alone gives no such bijection.

Independently, the theta-leading coefficient is \(64\), so
\[
 \Xi=(64^{-1}\Delta_5)^2,\qquad
 Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}=-\Xi^{-1}=-4096\Delta_5^{-2}
\]
in the primitive curve-counting convention
\cite{OB,OPand}. Here \(4096=64^2\) is a normalization constant.
Equality with \(|\Omega_{12}|\) supplies no map from a geometric
orbit to \(\Omega_{12}\), and it does not determine an orientation
character. The separate hypotheses in
Theorem~\ref{thm:G-R1-R2} must be retained when either geometric
interpretation is claimed.

\subsection{Parity and the first timelike root}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-recognition}

A reflection character acts on a line. Primitive recognition compares
a Lie superalgebra and therefore needs the even and odd dimensions
as well as its brackets. Put
\(\beta=\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3\).

\begin{lemma}[The first timelike parity split]
\label{lem:appE-first-timelike-parity}
For the fixed Gritsenko--Nikulin presentation,
\[
 \operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\beta}=-64,
 \qquad
 (\dim\mathfrak g_{\beta,\bar0},\dim\mathfrak g_{\beta,\bar1})
       =(29,93).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Jacobi coefficient is \(f(1,1)=-64\)
\cite{EZ:1}. In the presentation, the even part at \(\beta\)
contains two independent Lie words in the three real generators and nine
brackets for each of the three real--null decompositions. Thus its
 dimension is \(2+3\cdot9=29\). The odd part consists of the
\(93\) imaginary simple generators read from the chamber
correction. No real Serre relation occurs in this multidegree.
This is the word calculation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bkm-delta123-presentation-count}, and
\(29-93=-64\).
\end{proof}

The integer \(93\) comes from the simple-generator correction, not
from assigning one parity to a product exponent. More explicitly,
with the complete boundary factors retained,
\[
 m(\beta)=-[qrs]\bigl((qrs)^{-1/2}64^{-1}\Delta_5\bigr)=-93.
\]
Witt's multigraded formula gives the two real words as
\(3!/3=2\); the null-ray multiplicity nine is the first coefficient
of the corresponding eta-nine correction \cite{GN,GNII}.

\begin{corollary}[Parity dimensions do not determine brackets]
\label{cor:appE-signed-not-recognition}
Even the full list of root parity dimensions does not recognize the
positive Borcherds algebra without an operation comparison.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Give the underlying positive graded super vector space of
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) the zero bracket. Every even and odd
root dimension is unchanged, but the real-root bracket
\([e_i,e_j]\ne0\) for \(i\ne j\) has been lost. The two graded
Lie superalgebras cannot be identified while preserving these
specified generators and their brackets. A single root component
is not itself the recipient for all those brackets.
\end{proof}

Higher degrees sharpen the comparison. Write
\(a_{ij}=\delta_i+\delta_j\). At \(2a_{ij}\), the signed root
multiplicity ten consists of the null simple multiplicity nine and
the one-dimensional affine contribution. At
\(a_{ij}+2\delta_k\), with distinct \(i,j,k\), the simple
correction is \(90\) and the signed multiplicity is \(108\).
The full parity pair is \((201,93)\), by
Proposition~\ref{prop:bkm-height-six-parities}. Thus the residual
\(108-90=18\) is a signed bracket contribution; it is not a parity
block. Also
\[
 s_{\delta_k}(\beta)=a_{ij}+3\delta_k,
\]
so the height-four degree is not that reflected timelike degree.
The real Serre exponents are three for a real--real pair, five for a
complementary real--null pair, and three for a real generator acting
on a timelike simple generator of degree \(\beta\).
The all-height parity recursion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bkm-all-height-parities} determines both target
parities in the fixed presentation. It does not construct a compact
source with those dimensions or operations.

\subsection{The remaining geometric comparisons}
\label{app:pfaffian-wall-open-obligations}

The local skew calculation supplies a Pfaffian section with a
specified reflection lift. The automorphic calculation independently
supplies the homogeneous character. A compact identification needs
a covering geometric wall atlas, compatible square-root and section
comparisons, quotient descent, and coherent Weyl transport at every
stage and under every transition. These are the hypotheses in
Theorems~\ref{thm:G-O1} and \ref{thm:G-O1-plus} and the wall-atlas
condition above.

A geometric primitive comparison must further preserve both
parities and the actual Lie and Hall operations. The scalar
normalization and the abstract twelve-sign count solve none of
those existence problems. They remain independent even after the
local reflection signs have been matched.
