\section{Mukai vectors and Gram degrees}
\label{appendix:mukai-gram-dictionary}

Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective K3 surface and $E$ a smooth
complex elliptic curve.  Consider a one-dimensional subscheme $Y\subset S\times E$ with
curve class $(\beta,d)$.  Here $\beta$ is its projected class on $S$,
identified with its Poincar\'e dual, and $d$ is its degree over $E$.  The two Mukai components of its ideal give
\[
\Pi_X(Q_Y,P_Y)=\left(\frac{\beta^2}{2},\chi(\mathcal O_Y),d-1\right).
\]
We derive the formula, give sheaf representatives of the three
simple Gram degrees, and describe the quadratic correction required
when charges are added.

\subsection{Surface vectors and pairings}
\label{subsec:mukai-vector-pairing}

\begin{definition}[Mukai vector]
\label{def:mukai-lattice-vector-appendixC}
Write $\omega_S$ for the point class with $\int_S\omega_S=1$.
The cohomological Mukai lattice is
\[
\widetilde H(S,\Z)=H^0(S,\Z)\oplus H^2(S,\Z)\oplus H^4(S,\Z),
\]
with coordinates $(r,D,s)$ denoting $r+D+s\omega_S$.
For $F\in D^b\Coh(S)$, set
\[
v_S(F)=\operatorname{ch}(F)\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(S)}
=(\rk F,c_1(F),\rk F+\operatorname{ch}_2(F)).
\]
Here $\operatorname{ch}_2(F)$ is integrated over $S$, and
$\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(S)}=1+\omega_S$.  Actual coherent vectors
lie in $\widetilde H_{\mathrm{alg}}(S,\Z)=\Z\oplus\NS(S)\oplus\Z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Mukai form]
\label{prop:mukai-pairing-appendixC}
The symmetric form
\[
\langle(r,D,s),(r',D',s')\rangle=D\cdot D'-rs'-r's
=-\int_S(r,-D,s)(r',D',s')
\]
is even unimodular of signature $(4,20)$ on the full lattice.
On the algebraic sublattice it has signature $(2,\rho(S))$, and
\[
\chi_S(F,G)=-\langle v_S(F),v_S(G)\rangle.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $H^0\oplus H^4$ part is a hyperbolic plane.  Adjoining it to
$H^2(S,\Z)\cong U^{\oplus3}\oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus2}$ proves
the assertion about the full lattice.  Adjoining it to $\NS(S)$,
whose signature is $(1,\rho-1)$, gives the algebraic signature.
Hirzebruch--Riemann--Roch gives the last identity, as in
Proposition~\ref{k3:prop:rr}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Symmetry and additional algebraic structures]
\label{rem:mukai-symmetric-both-parts}
The Mukai form is symmetric on both the algebraic and transcendental
parts.  Its real infinitesimal isometry algebra is
$\mathfrak{so}(4,20)$.  Its complexification is
$\mathfrak{so}_{24}(\C)$.  This statement concerns a quadratic vector
space.  A separate supergrading does not change that bilinear form,
and the form alone defines neither a Yangian nor a Hall algebra.

A comparison with a quantum group requires its multiplication,
coproduct and deformation parameter, together with a map respecting
those structures.  For example, the Hilbert-scheme construction of
\cite[\S2]{SchiffmannVasserot2013} uses the equivariant cohomology of
Hilbert schemes of $\mathbb A^2$, while the construction in
\cite[\S2]{KSCoHA} uses quiver representation stacks.  Neither
construction by itself supplies such a comparison for the full K3
Mukai lattice.
\end{remark}

The surface examples in Example~\ref{ex:mukai-vector-point-and-ideal}
include point ideals and divisor ideals, which have different vectors
and self-pairings.  They will supply the threefold representatives
below.

\subsection{Paired charges on the threefold}
\label{subsec:kunneth-k3-times-e-appendixC}

\begin{proposition}[K\"unneth decomposition and Euler form]
\label{prop:kunneth-k3-times-e-appendixC}
Let $X=S\times E$ and normalize $\omega_E$ by $\int_E\omega_E=1$.
Every even integral cohomology class has a unique expression
\[
v_X=P\otimes1_E+Q\otimes\omega_E,
\qquad Q,P\in\widetilde H(S,\Z).
\]
For a perfect complex $\mathcal F$ on $X$, the components of
$v_X(\mathcal F)=\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal F)
\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(X)}$ satisfy
\[
P=v_S(Li_t^*\mathcal F),\qquad
Q=v_S(Rp_{S*}\mathcal F).
\]
Here $i_t:S\hookrightarrow X$ is a fiber inclusion and
$p_S:X\to S$ is projection.  If $c=(Q,P)$ and $c'=(Q',P')$ are
charges of $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$, then
\[
\chi_X(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)=Q\cdot P'-P\cdot Q'.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cohomology of both factors is torsion free, and the odd
cohomology of $S$ vanishes.  The integral K\"unneth theorem therefore
gives the decomposition.  Derived restriction extracts the
$1_E$ coefficient.  Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch for $p_S$, with
$\operatorname{td}(E)=1$, extracts the $\omega_E$ coefficient.
In particular, both components of a coherent charge are integral
and algebraic.

For the Euler form, the duality involution sends
$P\otimes1_E+Q\otimes\omega_E$ to
$P^\vee\otimes1_E-Q^\vee\otimes\omega_E$.  Thus Riemann--Roch
and $\int_S v^\vee w=-\langle v,w\rangle$ give
\[
\chi_X(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
=\int_S(P^\vee Q'-Q^\vee P')=Q\cdot P'-P\cdot Q'.
\]
\end{proof}

There are also two useful symmetric forms on this rank-$48$ group:
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{\oplus}(c,c')&=Q\cdot Q'+P\cdot P',\\
b_{\mathrm{cross}}(c,c')&=Q\cdot P'+P\cdot Q'.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their signatures are $(8,40)$ and $(24,24)$, respectively.  The
second is the tensor of the Mukai form with the hyperbolic form on
$H^0(E,\Z)\oplus H^2(E,\Z)$.  Both are auxiliary to the alternating
threefold Euler form.

\begin{definition}[Gram degrees]
\label{def:gram-degree-projection-appendixC}
Order the formal charge lattice as
$\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}=\widetilde H(S,\Z)\oplus\widetilde H(S,\Z)$,
with $c=(Q,P)$.  Its Gram map is
\[
\Pi_X:\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mathrm{gram}}
=\Z^3,\qquad
\Pi_X(Q,P)=\left(\frac{Q^2}{2},Q\cdot P,\frac{P^2}{2}\right).
\]
We call $\Pi_X(c)$ the Gram degree of $c$.  It determines the
exponent of $q^nr^ls^m$ when $(n,l,m)=\Pi_X(c)$ in the chosen
Igusa cusp coordinates.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The ideal-sheaf dictionary}
\label{subsec:d6-d2-d0-dictionary-appendixC}

\begin{theorem}[Threefold ideal-sheaf charge]
\label{thm:mukai-vector-ideal-sheaf-threefold}
Let $Y\subset X$ be a one-dimensional closed subscheme.  Write its
fundamental curve class as
$[Y]=(\beta,d)\in H_2(S,\Z)\oplus H_2(E,\Z)$ and put
$n=\chi(\mathcal O_Y)$.  Identify $\beta$ with its Poincar\'e dual
in $H^2(S,\Z)$.  Then
\[
v_X(I_Y)=(1,0,1-d)\otimes1_E+(0,-\beta,-n)\otimes\omega_E.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first nonzero Chern character of $\mathcal O_Y$ is
\[
\operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal O_Y)
=\beta\otimes\omega_E+d\omega_S\otimes1_E.
\]
Since $c_1(X)=0$, Riemann--Roch gives
$\int_X\operatorname{ch}_3(\mathcal O_Y)=n$.
The ideal sequence and $\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(X)}
=(1+\omega_S)\otimes1_E$ now give the displayed formula.
Zero-dimensional embedded components contribute to $n$ and require
no change in the calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The Euler coordinate]
\label{rem:threefold-euler-characteristic-no-todd-correction}
The equality $\int_X\operatorname{ch}_3(\mathcal O_Y)=n$ follows
because the only possible extra term in degree six is
$\operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal O_Y)c_1(X)/2$, which vanishes.
The ideal sequence fixes the sign $-n$ in $Q_Y$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[D6--D2--D0 Gram dictionary]
\label{thm:d6-d2-d0-dictionary-appendixC}
Under the preceding hypotheses, put
$P_Y=(1,0,1-d)$ and $Q_Y=(0,-\beta,-n)$.  Then
\[
\Pi_X(Q_Y,P_Y)=\left(\frac{\beta^2}{2},n,d-1\right).
\]
If $S$ is elliptically fibered with section $s_1$ and fiber $F$,
the class $\beta_h=s_1+hF$, $h\geq0$, gives
$\Pi_X(Q_Y,P_Y)=(h-1,n,d-1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The three pairings are
\[
Q_Y^2=\beta^2,\qquad Q_Y\cdot P_Y=n,\qquad P_Y^2=2(d-1).
\]
For the elliptic class, adjunction gives $s_1^2=-2$.  Also,
$s_1\cdot F=1$ and $F^2=0$.  Hence $\beta_h^2=2h-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Algebraicity and primitivity]
\label{rem:algebraic-versus-primitive-d2-charges}
The projection of an effective curve cycle on $X$ to $S$ is an
effective algebraic cycle, possibly zero.  Thus
$\beta\in\NS(S)$ and $d\geq0$.  The formula holds whether or not
$\beta$ is primitive.  Here primitive means nonzero and indivisible
in the integral lattice, rather than merely indivisible among
classes represented by a chosen family of curves.  The class
$\beta_h$ is primitive because $\beta_h\cdot F=1$.

These numerical conditions do not determine the existence of a
subscheme for every prescribed triple $(\beta,d,n)$.  Enumerative
comparisons impose their own hypotheses, including the primitive
K3-class regime in \cite{OPand}.  The charge calculation does not
extend that regime to imprimitive classes.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Additive charges]
\label{rem:chart-data-not-additive-hall-grading}
An exact triangle adds Mukai vectors, so a Hall operation is graded
by $(Q,P)$ whenever its source category and coefficient theory have
been specified.  Gram degrees add with an additional cross term.
Consequently a sum over equal Gram degrees is not by itself a
regrading by a group homomorphism.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Polarization of the Gram map}
\label{subsec:gram-cocycle-appendixC}

\begin{lemma}[The cross term]
\label{lem:gram-additivity-defect-appendixC}
For $c=(Q,P)$ and $c'=(Q',P')$, define
\[
B(c,c')=(Q\cdot Q',\;Q\cdot P'+Q'\cdot P,\;P\cdot P').
\]
Then $B$ is symmetric, integral and bilinear, and
\[
\Pi_X(c+c')=\Pi_X(c)+\Pi_X(c')+B(c,c'),\qquad B(c,c)=2\Pi_X(c).
\]
With $(\delta f)(c,c')=f(c)+f(c')-f(c+c')$, one has
$B=-\delta\Pi_X$ and $\delta B=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand the two squares and the middle pairing in $\Pi_X(c+c')$.
Bilinearity gives the cocycle equation
\[
B(c,c')+B(c+c',c'')=B(c',c'')+B(c,c'+c'').
\]
The remaining identities follow by subtraction and by setting $c'=c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quadratic and linear corrections]
\label{rem:cleanly-stated-polarization-identity}
The equality $B(c,c)=2\Pi_X(c)$ also follows from
$\Pi_X(2c)=4\Pi_X(c)$.  Thus the group-cohomology class of $B$ is
zero, with a quadratic trivialization.  An additive homomorphism
$f$ has $\delta f=0$, so it cannot trivialize a nonzero $B$.
This is the obstruction to an additive linear correction.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An additive extension of Gram degrees}
\label{subsec:normal-ordered-gram-map-appendixC}

\begin{definition}[Normal-ordered charge group]
\label{def:normal-ordered-charge-lattice-appendixC}
Let $\widehat\Gamma_X$ be the set
$\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{gram}}$ with law
\[
(c,T)\star(c',T')=(c+c',T+T'+B(c,c')).
\]
Its identity is $(0,0)$ and its inverse operation is
$(c,T)^{-1}=(-c,-T+2\Pi_X(c))$.  The split section and the raw
placement are, respectively,
\[
s(c)=(c,\Pi_X(c)),\qquad i_0(c)=(c,0).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Additive Gram map]
\label{prop:normal-ordered-gram-homomorphism-appendixC}
The cocycle law makes $\widehat\Gamma_X$ an abelian group.  The map
\[
(c,T)\longmapsto(c,T-\Pi_X(c))
\]
is an isomorphism to the ordinary direct sum
$\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}\oplus\Gamma_{\mathrm{gram}}$.
In particular,
\[
\overline\Pi_X(c,T)=\Pi_X(c)-T
\]
is a homomorphism, $s$ is a homomorphism, and
$\overline\Pi_X\circ s=0$, whereas
$\overline\Pi_X\circ i_0=\Pi_X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Associativity is the cocycle equation, and symmetry of $B$ gives
commutativity.  For the displayed splitting, the identity
$\Pi_X(c+c')=\Pi_X(c)+\Pi_X(c')+B(c,c')$ cancels the cross term.
The other assertions follow immediately.  The raw placement fails
to be additive precisely where $B$ is nonzero.
\end{proof}

The threefold sheaves of
Proposition~\ref{prop:type-ii-compact-mukai-representatives} realize
the three simple Gram degrees $(1,1,0)$, $(0,1,1)$ and $(0,-1,0)$.
They are external sums of point ideals or point sheaves on $S$.
Their construction requires no special Picard lattice.  Their support dimensions differ,
and a common stability condition with the required Hall operations
remains a separate comparison problem.

\begin{remark}[Choice of lift]
\label{rem:resolution-polarization-defect-appendixC}
Products of raw lifts accumulate the cross term in the central
coordinate.  For example,
\[
i_0(c)\star i_0(c')=(c+c',B(c,c')).
\]
The additive map $\overline\Pi_X$ sends this product to
$\Pi_X(c)+\Pi_X(c')$.  The split lift $s(c+c')$ instead has additive
Gram degree zero.  These are different lifts of the same underlying
charge.  The lattice extension is associative, but lifting an
actual Hall product requires a rule compatible with its objects
and extension correspondences.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Effective support]
\label{rem:chart-data-not-hall-support-appendixC}
The formal lattice contains nonalgebraic vectors.  Even in its
algebraic sublattice, a class need not be represented by an object
in a prescribed heart or stability chamber.  Such support must be
specified before forming its Hall algebra, as in
Definition~\ref{def:algebraic-effective-hall-support}.  The
ideal-sheaf dictionary and the three explicit sheaves above supply
particular representatives, not a realization of every root class
with all the required operations.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Automorphic and protected interpretations}
\label{subsec:three-igusa-names-chart-data}

The automorphic form $\Delta_5$ is a section of its weight-five line
with character $\nu_{\Delta_5}$.  In the root-lattice normalization,
the Borcherds--Kac--Moody denominator is
\[
\operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})
=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]
The Gram dictionary explains the associated Fourier exponents.
It does not construct a compact protected Pfaffian section.  Such a
section requires the oriented geometric data of
Chapter~\ref{chap:dirac-igusa-pro-object}, including the
Weyl-transport condition~\ref{def:O1-plus-weyl-transport}, and the
finite-stage hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pfaffian-dirac-finite-stage}.

Likewise, symmetry of the Mukai lattice does not identify a Hall
algebra with its orthogonal isometry algebra.  Both comparisons
require maps respecting the relevant operations.  The lattice and
charge identities proved here remain valid independently of those
constructions.
