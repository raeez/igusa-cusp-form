\chapter{Elliptic states, joining and the inverse Igusa form}
\label{ch:zbps-realization}

The inverse Igusa form occurs in two geometric series.  Hilbert
schemes of a K3 surface give an elliptic-index series after its
boundary factors have been restored.  Reduced curve counts on
\(X=S\times E\), with \(S\) a K3 surface and \(E\) an elliptic
curve, give the reciprocal cusp form itself, with a specified
normalization.  To compare operations behind these formulas, one
needs the complexes on which they act.

We first construct elliptic states from the chiral de Rham sheaf.
Its actual Enriques coefficient has parity dimensions \((1|65)\),
although the corresponding BKM root space has dimensions
\((29|93)\).  Both have superdimension \(-64\).  Hilbert
correspondences then give a concrete joining operator, whose double
commutator with creators measures multiplication of surface classes.
These constructions make the comparison problem explicit.  They do
not identify a reduced threefold Hall action with a field action.

The second half of the chapter relates the primitive curve counts
to \(\Delta_5^{-2}\), determines the spin \(L\)-function of the
weight-ten eigenline, and specifies the additional trace and sewing
data needed for a compact realization.  A Pfaffian comparison
retains a line, a section and its symmetry action.  Squaring that
section and taking a numerical trace each forget information, by
different operations.

\section{Geometric fields before their index}
\label{sec:zbps-geometric-fields}

Let \(Y\) be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension \(d\).
On a coordinate chart its chiral de Rham sheaf
\(\Omega_Y^{\mathrm{ch}}\) has even fields \(\gamma^i,\beta_i\)
and odd fields \(c^i,b_i\), with singular products
\[
 \beta_i(z)\gamma^j(w)\sim\frac{\delta_i^j}{z-w},
 \qquad b_i(z)c^j(w)\sim\frac{\delta_i^j}{z-w}.
\]
Their conformal weights are \(0,1,0,1\).  The fields \(c^i,b_i\)
have fermionic charges \(1,-1\); the even fields have charge zero.
We write \(a_{(m)}b\) for the coefficient of
\((z-w)^{-m-1}\) in the field product, and \(\partial\) for
vertex translation.

For a coordinate change \(\widetilde\gamma=f(\gamma)\) with
inverse \(g\), put \(f_i^a=\partial_i f^a\) and
\(g_a^i=\partial_a g^i\).  The fields transform by
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde c^a&=f_i^a(\gamma)c^i,&
 \widetilde b_a&=:g_a^i(f(\gamma))b_i:,\\
 \widetilde\beta_a
 &=:\beta_i g_a^i(f(\gamma)):
   +:\partial_k(g_a^i\circ f)(\gamma)c^k b_i:.
\end{align*}
The colons denote the normal product in the indicated order.
The last summand is essential.  For example, when
\(\widetilde\gamma=\gamma^2\) on a punctured chart,
\[
 \widetilde\beta=:\beta(2\gamma)^{-1}:
                   -(2\gamma^2)^{-1}:cb:.
\]
The coordinate theorem proves both preservation of the singular
products and exact composition on overlaps.\footnote{
F.~Malikov, V.~Schechtman and A.~Vaintrob, \emph{Chiral de Rham
complex}, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9803041}{(3.17),
Theorems~3.7--3.8}.}

Choose a finite affine cover with affine intersections.  The
Thom--Whitney totalization of its cosimplicial sections is denoted
\[
 \mathbb V_Y=\operatorname{TW}
       \bigl(\check C^\bullet(Y,\Omega_Y^{\mathrm{ch}})\bigr).
\]
Concretely, a component is a polynomial differential form on a
simplex with values in sections on the corresponding intersection;
face and degeneracy maps impose the compatibility conditions.
For a form \(\omega\) and field \(v\), total parity is
\(\deg\omega+\epsilon(v)\), where \(\epsilon\) is intrinsic
fermionic parity.  Thus
\[
 (\omega\otimes v)_{(m)}(\eta\otimes w)
 =(-1)^{\epsilon(v)\deg\eta}\omega\eta\otimes v_{(m)}w.
\]
The differential is the simplex de Rham differential.  No
additional differential on the fields is included.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zbps-derived-field-seed}
The complex \(\mathbb V_Y\) is a dg vertex superalgebra computing
\(R\Gamma(Y,\Omega_Y^{\mathrm{ch}})\) in each conformal weight.
Its vacuum is the compatible constant unit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Tensoring a vertex superalgebra with a commutative dg algebra gives
the displayed product, and the Koszul sign makes the differential
a derivation.  Compatible cosimplicial components preserve the
vertex identities.  Integration on simplices compares this
complex with the Cech complex; the polynomial Poincare contraction
proves that comparison is a quasi-isomorphism.  At each weight,
the coordinate construction has a finite filtration by coherent
tensor bundles.  They are acyclic on the affine intersections.
Induction through the filtration identifies the Cech complex
with derived sections.
\end{proof}

Let \(L_0,J_0\) be the conformal-weight and charge operators,
with formal variables \(q,y\).  The associated-graded
supercharacter of the field sheaf is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-field-symbol}
 y^{-d/2}\bigotimes_{a\ge1}
 \left(\Lambda_{-yq^{a-1}}T_Y^*
 \otimes\Lambda_{-y^{-1}q^a}T_Y
 \otimes\operatorname{Sym}_{q^a}T_Y^*
 \otimes\operatorname{Sym}_{q^a}T_Y\right).
\end{equation}
The exterior signs encode intrinsic parity.  The coordinate
filtration need not split as a field sheaf.  Its Euler
characteristic nevertheless gives
\[
 \sum_k(-1)^k\operatorname{str}
 \left(q^{L_0}y^{J_0-d/2};H^k(Y,\Omega_Y^{\mathrm{ch}})\right)
 =\operatorname{Ell}(Y;q,y).
\]
This equality retains the product of the sheaf-degree sign and the
intrinsic-parity sign.  It does not recover either grading from
the resulting Laurent series.

\subsection{The first Enriques coefficient}

Let \(T\) be an Enriques surface and \(K=K_T\).  A trivialization
of \(K^{\otimes2}\) permits local volume coordinates whose
transition determinants are constant \(\pm1\).  The coordinate
change of \(J=\sum_i:c^i b_i:\) adds the vertex derivative of the
logarithm of that determinant, so \(J\) is global.  Its zero mode
is fermionic charge.  Set
\[
 \mathscr V=(\Omega_T^{\mathrm{ch}})_{L_0=1,J_0=2}.
\]
Since normalized charge is \(J_0-1\), this is the coefficient of
\(qy\).  The weight-zero, charge-two sheaf is \(K\).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zbps-enriques-projector}
There is a splitting of sheaves of complex vector spaces
\[
 \mathscr V=\partial K\oplus\ker J_{(1)},
 \qquad P=\tfrac12\partial J_{(1)},\qquad P^2=P.
\]
The maps need not be \(\mathcal O_T\)-linear.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The translation commutator is
\([J_{(1)},\partial]=J_{(0)}\).  For \(a\in K\), weight gives
\(J_{(1)}a=0\), while charge gives \(J_{(0)}a=2a\).
Thus \(J_{(1)}\partial a=2a\).  Translation is injective on
\(K\), with left inverse \(J_{(1)}/2\), proving the splitting.
Locally the two summands of
\(\partial(c^1c^2)=(\partial c^1)c^2+c^1\partial c^2\)
each map to \(c^1c^2\) under \(J_{(1)}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zbps-enriques-coefficient}
The dimensions of \(H^k(T,\mathscr V)\), for \(k=0,1,2\), are
\((0,65,1)\).  The summand \(\ker J_{(1)}\) has only
\(H^1\), of dimension \(65\).  On a K3 surface the corresponding
field coefficient has dimensions \((1,130,1)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Writing \(\Omega=\Omega_T^1\), the coefficient of
\eqref{eq:zbps-field-symbol} has internally even associated graded
\[
 \Omega\otimes\Omega\oplus K\otimes\Omega\oplus K\otimes T_T
 \cong\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega\oplus K\oplus\Omega\oplus T_T.
\]
Use \(\Omega\cong T_T\otimes K\) and \(K^2\cong\mathcal O_T\).
Pullback along the etale K3 cover is injective on global sections.
The vanishing of positive symmetric holomorphic tangent and
cotangent tensors on K3, together with Serre duality, leaves only
\(H^1\) in the three noncanonical summands.\footnote{
S.~Kobayashi, \emph{The first Chern class and holomorphic symmetric
tensor fields}, Corollary~8; I.~Dolgachev, \emph{A brief introduction
to Enriques surfaces}, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7744}{\S3}.}
Their dimensions are \(45,10,10\).  For instance,
\[
 \operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega)=3-4c_2(T),
 \qquad \operatorname{td}(T)=1+c_2(T)/12,
 \qquad \int_Tc_2(T)=12,
\]
so Riemann--Roch gives \(\chi(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega)=-45\).
The canonical summand has \(H^2(K)=\C\) and no other cohomology.

The finite filtration gives \(h^0=0\), \(h^1\le65\),
\(h^2\le1\), and Euler characteristic \(-64\).
The projector preserves \(H^2(K)\), so \(h^2=1\) and
\(h^1=65\).  On K3 the three degree-one dimensions are
\(90,20,20\), and the canonical summand contributes one dimension
in degrees zero and two.  The same splitting and filtration bounds
give \((1,130,1)\).
\end{proof}

Total parity therefore gives \((1|65)\) on Enriques.  The BKM
coefficient \((29|93)\) in Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm} has the same
superdimension, but a comparison would have to account for the
additional \(28\) dimensions of each parity.  The charge-current
projector acts on the geometric field sheaf; it supplies no
cancellation differential on the BKM algebra.

There is a natural extra differential on chiral de Rham fields.
Locally put \(Q=\sum_i:\beta_i c^i:\) and
\(G=\sum_i:b_i\partial\gamma^i:\).  Their modes satisfy
\[
 [Q_{(0)},G_{(1)}]_+=L_0.
\]
The operators in this formula are global, even where \(Q\) itself
changes by a total vertex derivative.\footnote{Malikov, Schechtman
and Vaintrob, loc. cit., Theorems~4.2--4.4.}
At weight \(m>0\), \(G_{(1)}/m\) contracts \(Q_{(0)}\).
With the total cochain signs it also contracts the sum of
\(Q_{(0)}\) and the sheaf-resolution differential.  Moreover,
\(Q_{(0)}\) raises charge by one.  This extra differential thus
changes both the positive-weight cohomology and its charge grading;
it cannot be inserted while preserving the state theory just
constructed.

\section{Cycle sectors and the chiral comparison}
\label{sec:zbps-cycle-states}

The twisted chiral de Rham construction associates a sheaf
\(\Omega_{Y^N}^{\mathrm{ch},g}\) to the fixed locus of each
\(g\in\mathfrak S_N\), with a compatible centralizer action.\footnote{
E.~Frenkel and M.~Szczesny, \emph{Chiral de Rham complex and
orbifolds}, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307181}{Theorems~4.2--4.3}.}
Writing \(C(g)\) for the centralizer of \(g\), define the
unshifted sector complex
\[
 \mathcal H_N(Y)=\bigoplus_{[g]\subset\mathfrak S_N}
 R\Gamma\bigl((Y^N)^g,\Omega_{Y^N}^{\mathrm{ch},g}\bigr)^{C(g)}.
\]
Only the sheaf-resolution differential is used.  Coefficients are
in \(\C\); finite-group invariants are exact.  For an \(n\)-cycle
\(\sigma_n\), set
\[
 \mathcal K_n(Y)=
 R\Gamma\bigl(Y,\Omega_{Y^n}^{\mathrm{ch},\sigma_n}\bigr)^{\mu_n}.
\]
Its local oscillators have indices in \(\tfrac1n\Z\), and the
cyclic invariants retain the centralizer projection.

Let \(s\) record cycle length.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zbps-cycle-decomposition}
There is a natural equivalence of supercomplexes
\[
 \prod_{N\ge0}s^N\mathcal H_N(Y)
 \simeq\widehat{\operatorname{Sym}}_{\mathrm{super}}
       \left(\bigoplus_{n\ge1}s^n\mathcal K_n(Y)\right).
\]
The product completion is coefficientwise in length and conformal
weight, and the symmetry uses total cochain and intrinsic parity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A permutation with \(a_n\) cycles of length \(n\) has fixed locus
\(Y^{\sum a_n}\) and centralizer
\(\prod_n(\mu_n^{a_n}\rtimes\mathfrak S_{a_n})\).
Disjoint coordinate sets give external products of the twisted
sheaves.  The finite coherent filtrations justify coefficientwise
Kunneth.  Taking cyclic invariants first gives
\(\bigotimes_n\mathcal K_n(Y)^{\otimes a_n}\); the remaining
symmetric groups give the super symmetric powers.  Each length
has finitely many partitions, proving the stated completion.
\end{proof}

In the symmetric-power model, use coinvariants and identify them
with invariants by averaging.  Concatenation and assignment of
cycles to two groups define a commutative, cocommutative dg super
Hopf algebra.  Explicitly,
\[
 \Delta(v_1\cdots v_a)=
 \sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,a\}}(-1)^{\epsilon(I)}v_I\otimes v_{I^c}.
\]
Subwords keep their order; \(\epsilon(I)\) is the total Koszul
permutation sign.  The empty cycle configuration is the unit and
the counit component.  Single-cycle vectors are primitive and
have antipode \(-1\).  In invariant coordinates multiplication
is the normalized shuffle.  These conventions fix the factorials.
For multiplication \(M_v\) and the derivation \(D_\lambda\)
associated to a homogeneous continuous single-cycle functional,
\[
 [D_\lambda,M_v]_{\mathrm{super}}=\lambda(v).
\]
The identity is the graded Leibniz rule.  The Hopf identities follow
by assigning cycles to three groups in either order.

The seed fields give another action.  Let
\(V_Y=H^0(Y,\Omega_Y^{\mathrm{ch}})\).  Its mode Lie superalgebra is
\[
 \mathfrak m(V_Y)=
 \frac{V_Y\otimes\C[t,t^{-1}]}
 {\operatorname{im}(\partial\otimes1+1\otimes\partial_t)},
 \qquad
 [a[m],b[n]]=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom mj(a_{(j)}b)[m+n-j].
\]
Here \(a[m]\) is the class of \(a\otimes t^m\).
The diagonal field \(a^\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^N a^{(i)}\) is invariant
under permutations and acts on every twisted sector.  Distinct
factors have no singular products, so this is a mode Lie action
\(\rho\), with central vacuum mode \(1[-1]\) acting by \(N\).  Under the
cycle decomposition it acts on each cycle in turn; hence
\[
 [\rho(A),M_v]_{\mathrm{super}}=M_{\rho(A)v}.
\]
The diagonal map is not a unital vertex map: its vacuum has
multiplicity \(N\), and regular products have cross terms.
Neither this length-preserving mode action nor cycle
concatenation is an extension correspondence for sheaves on
\(S\times E\).

\subsection{An obstruction at weight one}

Take \(Y=S\) to be K3 and write
\[
 C_{H,N}=R\Gamma(S^{[N]},\Omega_{S^{[N]}}^{\mathrm{ch}}),
 \qquad C_{O,N}=\mathcal H_N(S).
\]
Retain sheaf degree \(k\), conformal weight \(w\), charge \(J\)
and intrinsic parity \(\epsilon\).  Total parity is
\(k+\epsilon\), and normalized charge at length \(N\) is
\(J-N\).  An untwisted field monomial satisfies
\(\epsilon\equiv J\pmod2\): the odd generators have charges
\(\pm1\), and the even generators have charge zero.
This holds sheafwise and therefore in cohomology.

The age of a permutation is the sum of the numbers
\(\theta\in(0,1)\) in its normal eigenvalues \(e^{2\pi i\theta}\),
with multiplicity.  On a surface product it is \(N-c(g)\), where
\(c(g)\) counts cycles.  Age contributes to the twisted charge;
the constant ground section has intrinsic parity zero.

In the transposition sector, let \(\tau\) be the constant ground
section.  It has \((k,w,J,\epsilon)=(0,0,1,0)\); the charge one
is its age.  The global invariant field
\(G^\Delta=G^{(1)}+G^{(2)}\) gives
\(v=(G^\Delta)_{(0)}\tau\).  The difference coordinates have
half-integral modes.  Directly,
\[
 v=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^2
      \gamma^{a,-}_{-1/2}b^-_{a,-1/2}\tau,
 \qquad
 2\beta^-_{1,1/2}c^{1,-}_{1/2}v=\tau.
\]
The rightmost detector acts first.  Its two contractions use
\([\beta_r,\gamma_t]=\delta_{r+t,0}\) and
\(\{b_r,c_t\}=\delta_{r+t,0}\).  The coefficient \(1/2\)
is the derivative of the half-integral coordinate mode.
Globality follows from the invariant field action before this
local calculation is used.  The detector proves nonvanishing.

\begin{theorem}[Unshifted chiral comparison]
\label{thm:zbps-unshifted-mckay}
There is no cochain-degree-zero quasi-isomorphism, in either
direction or by a zigzag, between \(C_{H,2}\) and \(C_{O,2}\)
that preserves weight, charge and total parity.  The same
obstruction occurs at every even cycle length.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The class \(v\) lies in \(H^0(C_{O,2})\) with
\((w,J,\epsilon)=(1,0,1)\).  It cannot be a boundary in a
sheaf-resolution model concentrated in nonnegative degrees.
The corresponding Hilbert component is zero by the untwisted
charge-parity relation.  A map preserving \(k\) and total parity
also preserves \(\epsilon\).  Its cone therefore retains this
class, in degree zero for a map from Hilbert to orbifold and in
degree \(-1\) for the reverse direction.

For an \(N\)-cycle, let \(\tau_N\) be the constant ground section.
The global descendant \(v_N=(\sum_{i=1}^N G^{(i)})_{(0)}\tau_N\)
has the Fourier-coordinate expression
\[
 v_N=\sum_{a=1}^2\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}\frac rN
 \gamma^{a,r}_{-r/N}b_{a,r;-(1-r/N)}\tau_N.
\]
The positive detector
\((N/r)\beta_{a,r;r/N}c^{a,r}_{1-r/N}\) sends it to \(\tau_N\).
Each term has weight one and centralizer eigenvalue one.
The ground age is \(N-1\), so the class has
\((k,w,J,\epsilon)=(0,1,N-2,1)\).
For even \(N\), its charge is even and its intrinsic parity odd,
again excluding a Hilbert counterpart.  Homogeneous resolution
changes and contractible additions preserve this cohomology
mismatch.  A zigzag would identify the two homogeneous cohomology
groups, and coefficientwise completion leaves them unchanged.
\end{proof}

This argument does not obstruct the corresponding odd-length
component.  It also specifies a different possible comparison.
Let \(C[a]^k=C^{k+a}\), and let \(\Pi\) reverse intrinsic parity.
For a permutation \(g\), its surface age is
\(a(g)=N-\ell(g)\), where \(\ell(g)\) counts cycles.  Define
\[
 C_{O,N}^{\mathrm{age}}=
 \bigoplus_{[g]}\Pi^{a(g)}
 R\Gamma\bigl((S^N)^g,\Omega_{S^N}^{\mathrm{ch},g}\bigr)^{C(g)}
 [-a(g)].
\]
Both sheaf degree and intrinsic parity increase by the age, so
total parity and the elliptic supertrace are unchanged.
The transposition class moves to \((k,w,J,\epsilon)=(1,1,0,0)\).
The mismatch used in the theorem disappears.  Constructing
quasi-isomorphisms with \(C_{H,N}\), together with supported field,
creation and joining maps and their coherent compatibilities,
remains a separate question.

\section{Hilbert correspondences and joining}
\label{sec:zbps-geometric-joining}

Let \(Y\) be K3 or Enriques, put \(X_N=Y^{[N]}\), with
\(X_0=\mathrm{pt}\), and use rational
cohomology with its complex orientation.  Write \(A=H^*(Y;\Q)\).
Its odd part is zero, and \(c_1(T_Y)=0\) rationally.  The punctual
incidence variety for \(N\ge0\) and \(r>0\) is
\[
 \mathfrak Q_{N,r}=\{(Z',x,Z):Z\subset Z',\ \ell(Z)=N,
 \ \ell(Z')=N+r,\ \operatorname{Supp}(I_Z/I_{Z'})=\{x\}\}
\]
lies in \(X_{N+r}\times Y\times X_N\).  Its fundamental cycle
and projections \(\pi_+,s_Y,\pi_-\) define
\[
 C_r(a)v=(\pi_+)_*
       \bigl([\mathfrak Q_{N,r}]\cup s_Y^*a\cup\pi_-^*v\bigr).
\]
Cap product and Poincare duality are understood.  The pushforward
is proper.  If \(\dagger\) is the ordinary Poincare adjoint, set
\(A_r(a)=(-1)^{r-1}C_r(a)^\dagger\).  Then
\[
 [A_r(a),C_t(b)]=r\delta_{rt}\int_Yab,
 \qquad [C_r(a),C_t(b)]=[A_r(a),A_t(b)]=0.
\]
The Nakajima--Grojnowski theorem identifies the polynomial space
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}\left(\bigoplus_{r\ge1}s^r A\right)
 \cong\bigoplus_{N\ge0}H^*(X_N;\Q)
\]
by sending the generator \(p_r(a)\) to \(C_r(a)|0\rangle\),
where \(|0\rangle=1_{X_0}\).
Creation has degree \(\deg a+2(r-1)\) and Hodge shift
\((r-1,r-1)\).\footnote{See \cite{NakajimaHilbert1997} and
M.~Lehn--C.~Sorger, \emph{The cup product of the Hilbert scheme
for K3 surfaces}, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0012166}
{Theorems~3.5--3.6}.  Their trace on the shifted surface algebra
is \(-\int_Y\); the annihilator above uses \(+r\int_Yab\).}
Over distinct points the iterated length-one correspondence
orders the points in \(N!\) ways.  Hence
\[
 \frac1{N!}C_1(1)^N|0\rangle=1_{X_N}.
\]

These correspondences have a simple Hall antecedent.  A point of
\(X_N\) is a cyclic quotient \(\mathcal O_Y\twoheadrightarrow Q\).
Constructible functions on zero-dimensional sheaves act on those
quotients by
\[
 (h\cdot f)(\mathcal O_Y\twoheadrightarrow Q)
 =\int_{U\subset Q}h(U)
 f(\mathcal O_Y\twoheadrightarrow Q/U)\,d\chi.
\]
Associativity integrates over the same flags
\(0\subset U\subset W\subset Q\) in either order.  The zero
subobject gives the unit.  For \(Z\subset Z'\), the extension is
\[
 0\longrightarrow I_Z/I_{Z'}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{Z'}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal O_Z\longrightarrow0.
\]
The framing generates the middle object.  Euler pushforward proves
this constructible-function action; critical cohomology requires
its own pullback and pushforward maps.  Passing to \(Y\times E\)
also admits nilpotent normal operators, so the surface category
is not extension-closed in the threefold category.

\subsection{The surface diagonal}

Choose a basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_b\) of \(H^2(Y;\Q)\), where \(b=b_2(Y)\),
and let \(\omega\) be the point class with \(\int_Y\omega=1\).
Writing \(Q_{ij}=\int_Yu_iu_j\),
\[
 A=\Q1\oplus\bigoplus_i\Q u_i\oplus\Q\omega,
 \qquad u_iu_j=Q_{ij}\omega,
 \qquad u_i\omega=\omega^2=0.
\]
Multiplication \(m\) and its adjoint diagonal \(\Delta\) satisfy
\begin{align*}
 \Delta1&=1\otimes\omega+\omega\otimes1
              +\sum_{i,j}Q^{ij}u_i\otimes u_j,\\
 \Delta u_i&=u_i\otimes\omega+\omega\otimes u_i,
 &\Delta\omega&=\omega\otimes\omega,
\end{align*}
where \((Q^{ij})=Q^{-1}\).  Testing against basis pairs proves
these formulas.  Consequently
\[
 e_A=m\Delta1=(b+2)\omega=c_2(T_Y),\qquad m\Delta(a)=e_Aa.
\]
The coefficient is \(12\) for Enriques and \(24\) for K3.
A split followed by a join inserts this Euler class.

The full permutation product explains this insertion.  Label each
cycle of \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_N\) by an element of \(A\).
For a joint orbit \(O\) of \(\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\), let
\(c_\sigma(O)\) count its \(\sigma\)-cycles, and set
\[
 g_O=\frac{|O|+2-c_\sigma(O)-c_\tau(O)-c_{\sigma\tau}(O)}2.
\]
Riemann--Hurwitz identifies it with the nonnegative genus of the
transitive three-branch covering.  If \(a_O,b_O\) are the products
of the input labels on that orbit, its output labels are
\[
 \Delta_{c_{\sigma\tau}(O)}(a_Ob_Oe_A^{g_O}).
\]
Here \(\Delta_k\) is the iterated diagonal, with
\(\Delta_1=\operatorname{id}\).
Tensoring over orbits gives the Frobenius permutation product.
Its associativity follows from the projection formula and
\(m\Delta(a)=e_Aa\), which identify the decompositions of each
connected covering surface.  For \(s_2=(12)\) and \(\rho=(123)\),
\[
 (as_2)(bs_2)=\Delta(ab)\,\mathrm{id},\qquad
 (a\rho)(b\rho)=abe_A\rho^2,
 \qquad (a\rho)(b\rho^{-1})=\Delta_3(ab)\,\mathrm{id}.
\]
Genus at least two gives zero because \(e_A^2=0\).

For the Hilbert product, multiply this law by
\[
 (-1)^{(|\sigma|+|\tau|-|\sigma\tau|)/2},
 \qquad |\sigma|=N-c(\sigma).
\]
Permutation parity makes the exponent integral; telescoping proves
the two-cocycle identity.  The invariant algebra with this sign
is \(H^*(X_N;\Q)\), since \(K_Y\) is numerically trivial.\footnote{
Lehn--Sorger, loc. cit., \S2 and Theorem~3.2.}
A cycle-labelled permutation summed over all conjugates with
repetitions corresponds to the product of its creators on the
vacuum.  The identity sector then has coefficient \(N!\).
Over \(\C\), the factor \(i^{|\sigma|}\) removes the sign twist;
over \(\Q\), the sign remains explicit.

\subsection{The second-order operator}

For a homogeneous basis \(a_\alpha\) of \(A\), write
\(a_\alpha a_\beta=m_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}a_\gamma\) and
\(\Delta a_\gamma=\Delta_\gamma^{\alpha\beta}
 a_\alpha\otimes a_\beta\).  Let \(\partial_{n,\alpha}\)
differentiate \(p_n(a_\alpha)\).  Define
\begin{align*}
 J&=\frac12\sum_{i,j\ge1}ij\,m_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
 p_{i+j}(a_\gamma)\partial_{i,\alpha}\partial_{j,\beta},\\
 S&=\frac12\sum_{i,j\ge1}(i+j)\Delta_\gamma^{\alpha\beta}
 p_i(a_\alpha)p_j(a_\beta)\partial_{i+j,\gamma},
 \qquad D_H=J-S.
\end{align*}
All sums are finite at fixed length.  Joining two cycles gives
\(ij\) endpoint choices.  Splitting a cycle gives \(i+j\)
choices, counted twice by the ordered sum.  Thus the transposition
sum acts by \(J+S\) in the ordinary orbifold product and by
\(D_H\) with the Hilbert sign.  Geometrically \(D_H\) is cup
multiplication by \(-c_1(\mathcal O_Y^{[N]})\); the canonical-class
term vanishes.\footnote{Lehn--Sorger, Proposition~3.9.}
For \(N\ge2\), the transposition class is
\(C_2(1)C_1(1)^{N-2}|0\rangle/(2(N-2)!)\), with the denominator
given by its centralizer order.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zbps-joining}
If \(M_i(a)\) multiplies by \(p_i(a)\), then
\[
 [[D_H,M_i(a)],M_j(b)]=ijM_{i+j}(ab),\qquad
 [[[D_H,M_i(a)],M_j(b)],M_k(c)]=0.
\]
The operator preserves length and raises cohomological degree by
two and normalized Hodge charge by one.  Moreover,
\[
 D_H^2p_2(a)=-p_2(e_Aa),\qquad
 D_H^{2N+1}|_{H^*(X_N)}=0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first-order splitting term has zero double commutator with
multiplication.  Differentiating both derivative slots of \(J\)
gives \(ijM_{i+j}(ab)\), which commutes with every creator.
Joining increases the age degree by two; splitting inserts a
degree-four diagonal and decreases the age degree by two.
Both have Hodge increase \((1,1)\).  A label of Hodge degree
\((r,s)\) on a length-\(i\) cycle has normalized charge
\(r+i-1-i=r-1\), so the increase is one.
At length two, splitting and joining gives \(-m\Delta(a)\),
proving the square formula.  The degree range \(0,\ldots,4N\)
proves the nilpotence bound.
\end{proof}

In particular \(D_H^2p_2(1)\ne0\).  This joining operator is
even and second order; it is not a square-zero differential.
Its double commutator gives an operation that the scalar
elliptic genus alone does not specify.

\subsection{Preparing the elliptic matrix coefficient}
\label{subsec:zbps-elliptic-transport}

Let \(\mathcal E_{q,y}(X_N)\) be the tensor class in
\eqref{eq:zbps-field-symbol}, and put
\[
 \varepsilon_N=\operatorname{td}(T_{X_N})
                 \operatorname{ch}(\mathcal E_{q,y}(X_N)),
 \qquad R=\Q(y)[[q]].
\]
Riemann--Roch gives
\(\int_{X_N}\varepsilon_N=\operatorname{Ell}(X_N;q,y)\).
These are even, generally inhomogeneous cohomology classes.
Their degree-zero parts \(r_N\) have constant \(q\) coefficient
\(y^{-N}(1-y)^{2N}\), a unit over \(\Q(y)\).  Since
\(\nu_N=\varepsilon_N-r_N\) has positive degree,
\[
 \varepsilon_N^{-1}=r_N^{-1}
       \sum_{j=0}^{2N}(-r_N^{-1}\nu_N)^j.
\]
Write \(U_N\) for multiplication by \(\varepsilon_N\).

A cohomological kernel from \(X_M\) to \(X_N\) is a class on
\(X_N\times X_M\).  It acts by pullback, cup product and
pushforward; convolution uses the same operations on the triple
product.  Transport it by
\[
 \widetilde K=\pi_N^*\varepsilon_N\cup K
                         \cup\pi_M^*\varepsilon_M^{-1}.
\]
The middle classes cancel in every convolution, so
\[
 \widetilde{L\circ K}=\widetilde L\circ\widetilde K,
 \qquad T_{\widetilde K}=U_NT_KU_M^{-1},
 \qquad\widetilde{[\Delta_{X_N}]}=[\Delta_{X_N}].
\]
The transported creators and annihilators obey the same Heisenberg
relations.  Cup multiplication commutes with \(U_N\), so \(D_H\)
is unchanged.  Telescoping and the vacuum normalization give
\[
 \frac1{N!}\widetilde C_1(1)^N|0\rangle=\varepsilon_N.
\]
With \(\langle\mathcal I|\) denoting integration in each length,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-prepared-elliptic-state}
 \left\langle\mathcal I\middle|
       \exp(s\widetilde C_1(1))\middle|0\right\rangle
 =\sum_{N\ge0}s^N\operatorname{Ell}(Y^{[N]};q,y).
\end{equation}
This is a specified matrix coefficient.  Conjugation leaves
ordinary traces unchanged, and \(q\) here is a coefficient
parameter.  The inverse used above is an inverse in cohomology,
not a tensor inverse of a coefficient complex.

The twisted-sector index theorem and the elliptic McKay theorem
identify this scalar with the index of the cycle sectors.\footnote{
Frenkel--Szczesny, Theorem~6.1, with the intrinsic-parity signs in
\eqref{eq:zbps-field-symbol}; L.~Borisov--A.~Libgober,
\emph{McKay correspondence for elliptic genera},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206241}{Corollary~6.7}.}
If \(\operatorname{Ell}(Y;q,y)=\sum c(m,\ell)q^my^\ell\), then
\[
 \sum_{N\ge0}s^N\operatorname{Ell}(Y^{[N]};q,y)
 =\prod_{n>0,\,m\ge0,\,\ell}
       (1-s^nq^my^\ell)^{-c(nm,\ell)}.
\]
For Enriques and K3 the seeds are \(\phi_{0,1}\) and
\(2\phi_{0,1}\).  With the boundary factor \(b(q,y)\) of
Section~\ref{sec:view1-boundary-factors}, the two series are
\[
 F_T=\frac{s^{1/2}b(q,y)}{D_5(q,y,s)},\qquad
 F_S=\frac{s\,b(q,y)^2}{D_5(q,y,s)^2},\qquad F_T^2=F_S.
\]
The boundary factors cancel before specialization at \(y=1\).
The unshifted obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:zbps-unshifted-mckay}
shows why this scalar equality cannot be read as a comparison of
all the underlying gradings and operations.

There is also a domain issue at \(y=1\).  At \(q=0\), set
\(t=y-1\), \(z=y+y^{-1}-2=t^2/(1+t)\), and
\(a=1+z/12\).  On the surface,
\[
 \varepsilon_Y(0,y)=z\,1+a\,c_2(T_Y).
\]
Writing \(e=\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Y)\), its multiplication matrix
on \((1,\omega)\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}z&0\\ae&z\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The transported length-one annihilator therefore has value
\[
 \widetilde A_1(1)(1)=\int_Y\varepsilon_Y^{-1}=-ae\,z^{-2},
\]
a fourth-order pole at \(t=0\), although the index
\(\int_Y\varepsilon_Y=ae\) is regular.  The image lattice
\(\varepsilon_Y H^*(Y;\Q)[[t]]\) is abstractly free, while its
embedded special image has rank one.  Its cokernel is
\[
 \Q[[t]]/(t^4)\oplus
       \bigl(\Q[[t]]/(t^2)\bigr)^{b_2(Y)}.
\]
Indeed the outer matrix has Smith form \(\operatorname{diag}(1,t^4)\),
and multiplication on \(H^2(Y)\) is a unit times \(t^2\).
The derived special fibre of the cokernel consequently has
\(b_2(Y)+1\) dimensions in each of degrees \(-1,0\).
The later degeneration chapters develop this distinction between
the lattice, its embedded fibre and the derived cokernel.

Finally, any invertible even classes \(\alpha_N\), with
\(\alpha_0=1\), give the same conjugation construction with
matrix coefficient \(\sum_Ns^N\int_{X_N}\alpha_N\).
For \(N>0\), taking \(\alpha_N=1+c_N[\mathrm{pt}]\) realizes
an arbitrary coefficient \(c_N\in R\).  A prescribed scalar
series therefore does not determine a critical Hall action on
independently defined field complexes.

\section{Primitive curve counts on the threefold}
\label{sec:zbps-scalar-shadow}

Return to \(X=S\times E\).  Let \(\beta_h\) be a nonzero
primitive K3 class with \(\beta_h^2=2h-2\), and let \(d\) be
the degree along \(E\).  Translation by \(E\) and the K3
symplectic reduction enter the reduced theory.  Its stable-pair
and ideal-sheaf moduli are \(P_n(X,(\beta_h,d))\) and
\(I_n(X,(\beta_h,d))\).  For pairs, \(n=\chi(F)\); for an
ideal sheaf of a curve \(C\), \(n=\chi(\mathcal O_C)\).
The obstruction theories and virtual classes live on these moduli.
The target remains a complex threefold.

\begin{convention}[Rational representatives and variables]
\label{conv:op-chamber}
Let \(R_{h,d}(v)\) be the rational function whose Laurent
expansion at \(v=0\) is the reduced DT/PT series in class
\((\beta_h,d)\).  Use
\[
 (T_{\mathrm{OP}},Q_{\mathrm{OP}},P_{\mathrm{OP}})=(s,q,r),
 \qquad v=-r,
\]
and define the rational representative of the full primitive
series by
\[
 Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}(r,q,s)=
       \sum_{h,d\ge0}R_{h,d}(-r)q^{h-1}s^{d-1}.
\]
The DT/PT expansion uses \(r=0\); the Borcherds product chamber
uses the expansion at \(r=\infty\), coefficientwise in \(q,s\).
The GW substitution is \(r=e^{iu}\), hence \(v=-e^{iu}\),
followed by Laurent expansion at \(u=0\).
\end{convention}

Oberdieck proves equality of reduced DT and PT series for nonzero
K3 class and rationality of the PT series.  The degree-zero DT
correction is trivial because \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S\times E)=0\)
\cite[Theorem~3]{OberdieckReducedPT}.  The primitive PT/GW
comparison is \cite[Proposition~5]{OPand}.  The K3 reduction and
primitive-class convention also agree with the reduced K3 theory
of \cite{MaulikPandharipande}.

\begin{theorem}[The primitive inverse Igusa series]
\label{thm:zbps-level-n-shadow}
With Convention~\ref{conv:op-chamber}, the reduced numerical
invariants satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-op-dt}
 Z^X_{\mathrm{DT}}=Z^X_{\mathrm{PT}}=Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}
 =-(\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
 =-4096\,\Delta_5(Z)^{-2}.
\end{equation}
Here the equality of DT/PT series means equality of their rational
representatives.  Define the unscaled scalar by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-equality}
 Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}^{K3\times E}:=-4096^{-1}Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}
 =\bigl(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}\bigr)^{-1}=\Delta_5^{-2}.
\end{equation}
This definition does not supply an operator trace.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The reduced GW series is
\[
 Z(u,q,s)=\sum_{g,h,d\ge0}N_{g,h,d}u^{2g-2}q^{h-1}s^{d-1}.
\]
Oberdieck--Pixton identify it with the Laurent expansion of
\(-1/\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}\) under \(r=e^{iu}\)
\cite[Theorem~1]{OB}.  Their product is
\[
 \chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}(r,q,s)
 =rqs\prod_{(h,k,d)>0}(1-r^kq^hs^d)^{c(4hd-k^2)},
 \qquad c(4hd-k^2)=2f(hd,k).
\]
Positivity means \(h,d\ge0\) and \(h+d>0\), or \(h=d=0\)
and \(k<0\).  The primitive PT/GW comparison, including
\(v=-e^{iu}\), and the reduced DT/PT theorem identify the
rational representatives.  In the theta convention of this book,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-op-monic}
 \chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2
 =2^{-12}\Delta_{10}^{\theta}=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
 \qquad \Delta_{10}^{\theta}=\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2.
\end{equation}
This is the product normalization of Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic},
with the classical Igusa identification
\cite{Igusa1962,Igusa1964II}.  Inverting proves
\eqref{eq:zbps-op-dt} and the rescaling gives
\eqref{eq:zbps-equality}.
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns the primitive K3-class branch.  It makes no
assertion for an arbitrary imprimitive class.  Changing stability
chamber in a proposed compact Hall realization requires the
correspondence comparison of
Definition~\ref{def:chamber-compatible-hall-descent}; the rational
variable substitution alone does not transport a Hall bracket.

\subsection{Coefficients, roots and normalization}
\label{subsec:zbps-coefficients}

The seed has expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-phi01-leading}
 \phi_{0,1}=y^{-1}+10+y
 +q\bigl(10y^{-2}-64y^{-1}+108-64y+10y^2\bigr)+O(q^2).
\end{equation}
Its coefficients have several uses with different objects behind
them.  They are Euler superdimensions of the elliptic coefficient
bundles.  At the root
\(\alpha(n,\ell,m)=2nf_2-\ell f_3+2mf_{-2}\), the value
\(f(nm,\ell)\) is the signed root multiplicity in the BKM
denominator.  The K3 genus has coefficients \(2f\),
which produce the squared automorphic product.  These equalities
are equalities of indices; they do not identify the ordinary
parity dimensions or construct bracket maps.

In the product coordinates of Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-bgn-product}
 \Delta_5(Z)=64(qrs)^{1/2}
       \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\end{equation}
The chamber includes the factor \(1-r^{-1}\) at \(n=m=0\).
For \(P=D_5/(qrs)^{1/2}\), one has
\[
 [qs]P=(1-r^{-1})
       \bigl(-9(r^2+r^{-2})+84(r+r^{-1})-6\bigr),
 \qquad [qrs]P=93.
\]
Indeed the pure \(q\) and pure \(s\) factors contribute
\((1-qA)(1-sA)\), where \(A=r+10+r^{-1}\), and the mixed
factor contributes
\(-qs(10(r^2+r^{-2})-64(r+r^{-1})+108)\).
Their product gives the displayed expression.
The isolated factor \((1-qrs)^{-64}\) supplies \(64\); the
remaining factors supply \(29\).  The coefficient \(93\) is
therefore different from the root superdimension \(-64\).

Squaring gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-delta5-squared-leading}
 \Delta_5^2=4096qrs
       \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{2f(nm,\ell)}.
\end{equation}
Thus, with \((p,q,\widetilde q)=(r,q,s)\) in the OP notation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-leading-coeff-check}
 \chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
 \qquad [pq\widetilde q]\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=1,
 \qquad [(qrs)^{1/2}]D_5=1.
\end{equation}
The leading theta coefficient is \(64\), and its square is
\(4096\).  The minus sign in \eqref{eq:zbps-op-dt} is the OP
convention.  None is an orientation character.

\begin{remark}[The sign data]
\label{rem:zbps-constants-not-orientation}
The homogeneous orthogonal character
\(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}\) of
Definition~\ref{def:homogeneous-orthogonal-character} belongs to
the weight-five automorphic line.  A geometric character
\(\epsilon_o\) requires an actual equivariant root line and
coherent wall transport.  Its local sign must be computed from
the action on that root, as in Chapter~\ref{ch:pfaffian-dirac}.
The formal set \(\Omega_{12}=\{\pm1\}^{12}\) also has
cardinality \(4096\), but a geometric sign orbit identified
with that set is additional data.  Its cardinality cannot be
inferred from the theta coefficient.
\end{remark}

At a generic smooth point of the diagonal divisor on Siegel space,
write \(\Delta_5=u a\) in a local frame, with \(a\) a unit.
Then \(\Delta_5^{-2}=u^{-2}a^{-2}\) has a double pole.
This divisor calculation leaves the geometric mirror-discriminant
comparison of Chapter~\ref{ch:view4-mirror} open.
The coefficient of \(q^{-1}s^{-1}\), expanded at \(r=0\), is
\[
 [q^{-1}s^{-1}]\Delta_5^{-2}
 =\frac{r}{4096(1-r)^2}
 =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac n{4096}r^n.
\]
A fixed Euler degree extracts \([r^n]\); it is not the complete
series.  Evaluation at a Siegel point outside the polar divisor
is yet another operation.  The target Euler characteristic is
\(\chi(\mathcal O_X)=\chi(\mathcal O_S)\chi(\mathcal O_E)=0\),
which is distinct from the Euler degree of a curve or stable pair.

\section{The weight-ten Hecke eigenline}
\label{sec:spin-l-factor}

The arithmetic of \(\Delta_5^2\) is independent of its scalar
normalization.  Put
\[
 g(\tau)=\Delta(\tau)E_6(\tau),\qquad
 \Delta=q\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{24},\qquad
 E_6=1-504\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_5(n)q^n.
\]
Here \(\sigma_5(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^5\).  The space
\(S_{18}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z))\) is one-dimensional, so \(g\)
is its normalized Hecke eigenform.  The Saito--Kurokawa
correspondence identifies its lift with the one-dimensional
weight-ten Siegel cusp space.  Thus its eigenline is
\(\C\Delta_5^2=\C\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}\)
\cite[Theorem~2 and its corollary]{ZagierSK}.

\begin{proposition}[The full Hecke operators]
\label{prop:zbps-sk-hecke}
Let \(a_p\) be the coefficient of \(q^p\) in \(g\), and use
Andrianov's full operators \(T(p)\) and \(T(p^2)\), normalized
by \cite[Theorem~3.1.1]{Andr74}.  On the weight-ten eigenline,
\[
 \lambda(p)=a_p+p^8+p^9,\qquad
 \lambda(p^2)=a_p^2+a_p(p^8+p^9)+p^{18}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrr}
 p&a_p&\lambda(p)&\lambda(p^2)\\\hline
 2&-528&240&135424\\
 3&-4284&21960&293343849
 \end{array}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a positive even Siegel weight \(K\), the lift of a
weight-\(2K-2\) eigenform has local polynomial
\[
 (1-p^{K-2}t)(1-p^{K-1}t)
          (1-a_pt+p^{2K-3}t^2).
\]
Andrianov's polynomial for the full operators is
\[
 1-\lambda(p)t+
 \bigl(\lambda(p)^2-\lambda(p^2)-p^{2K-4}\bigr)t^2
 -\lambda(p)p^{2K-3}t^3+p^{4K-6}t^4.
\]
Set \(x=p^{K-2}\), \(y=p^{K-1}\), so \(xy=p^{2K-3}\).
The linear and quadratic coefficients of the factored polynomial
are \(-(a_p+x+y)\) and \(a_p(x+y)+2xy\).  Therefore
\[
 \lambda(p^2)=(a_p+x+y)^2-x^2-a_p(x+y)-2xy
             =a_p^2+a_p(x+y)+y^2.
\]
Taking \(K=10\) gives the formulas.  Expanding the defining
product for \(\Delta\) and the divisor series for \(E_6\) gives
\(g=q-528q^2-4284q^3+\cdots\), from which the table follows.
\end{proof}

The notation \(T(p^2)\) here denotes the full Hecke operator;
it must not be replaced by a single double-coset generator with
the same informal degree.  The elliptic bound
\(|a_p|\le2p^{17/2}\) shows \(\lambda(p)/p^9\to1\).
The Satake factor \(p^9\), normalized by \(p^{17/2}\), has
absolute value \(p^{1/2}\), expressing the non-tempered nature
of this lift.

\begin{remark}[The Maass subspace]
\label{rem:zbps-sk-hecke-level0}
For positive even \(K\), the full-level lift takes
\(S_{2K-2}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z))\) to the Maass subspace in weight
\(K\), with Hecke eigenvalues prescribed by the local polynomial
above.  At \(K=10\), both the source
and the entire Siegel cusp space have dimension one
\cite[pp.~375, 379--381]{ZagierSK}.  The product normalization
\eqref{eq:zbps-op-monic} changes its generator but leaves all
Hecke eigenvalues unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[The spin factorization and its completion]
\label{thm:zbps-andrianov-factor}
For \(F=\Delta_{10}^{\theta}=\Delta_5^2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-spinor-factor}
 L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,F)=\zeta(s-8)\zeta(s-9)L(s,g),
 \qquad g=\Delta E_6.
\end{equation}
The local polynomial and completed function are
\begin{align*}
 Q_{p,F}(t)&=1-\lambda(p)t+
 (\lambda(p)^2-\lambda(p^2)-p^{16})t^2
 -\lambda(p)p^{17}t^3+p^{34}t^4,\\
 \Psi_F(s)&=(2\pi)^{-2s}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s-8)
                         L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,F).
\end{align*}
The completion is meromorphic and satisfies
\(\Psi_F(18-s)=\Psi_F(s)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Saito--Kurokawa local factor just computed gives
\eqref{eq:zbps-spinor-factor} in a right half-plane and hence by
meromorphic continuation \cite[(10)--(11), Theorem~2]{ZagierSK}.
Andrianov's Theorem~3.1.1 supplies the completion and functional
equation \cite{Andr74}.  These are separate uses of the two
results: the general genus-two functional equation does not by
itself imply the Saito--Kurokawa factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The pole at \(s=10\)]
\label{prop:zbps-spin-pole-residue}
The possible pole at \(s=9\) in \eqref{eq:zbps-spinor-factor}
is cancelled by \(L(9,g)=0\).  At \(s=10\) there is a simple
pole with positive residue
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-residue}
 \operatorname*{Res}_{s=10}L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,F)
 =\zeta(2)L(10,g)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}L(10,g)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Mellin transform
\[
 \Lambda(g,s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(s,g)
             =\int_0^\infty g(iy)y^{s-1}\,dy
\]
continues to an entire function.  Modularity of weight eighteen
gives \(g(i/y)=-y^{18}g(iy)\).  Splitting the integral at one
and substituting \(y\mapsto1/y\) gives
\[
 \Lambda(g,s)=\int_1^\infty
        g(iy)\bigl(y^{s-1}-y^{17-s}\bigr)\,dy,
 \qquad\Lambda(g,s)=-\Lambda(g,18-s).
\]
Thus \(L(9,g)=0\).  At \(s=10\) the factor in parentheses is
\(y^9-y^7>0\) for \(y>1\).

The product for \(\Delta(iy)\) is positive.  Modularity of
\(E_6\) gives \(E_6(i)=0\), and its Fourier series gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dy}E_6(iy)=1008\pi
       \sum_{n\ge1}n\sigma_5(n)e^{-2\pi ny}>0.
\]
Hence \(g(iy)>0\) for \(y>1\), proving
\(\Lambda(g,10)>0\) and \(L(10,g)>0\).
The pole of \(\zeta(s-9)\) has residue one; multiplying the
remaining factors gives \eqref{eq:zbps-residue}.
\end{proof}

The rational-period theorem supplies a nonzero even-critical
period \(\Omega^+(g)\), defined up to \(\Q^\times\), such that
\[
 \frac{L(10,g)}{(2\pi i)^{10}\Omega^+(g)}\in\Q.
\]
Here the superscript specifies the even critical integers, rather
than the parity of the index in
\(r_n(g)=\int_0^\infty g(iy)y^n\,dy\).
The rational structures come from Eichler--Shimura--Manin theory
\cite{Manin72,KZ}, with the period convention of \cite{Del79}.
The positivity proof is additional to that rationality statement.
Neither argument uses a geometric Pfaffian or a compact Hall
realization.

\section{Trace domains and sewing}
\label{sec:scalar-trace-not-gravity}

A scalar series becomes a trace only after a state space, an
endomorphism and a trace functional have been specified.  At a
finite geometric truncation \(R\), suppose one has charge-graded
perfect complexes \(V_{R,\gamma}\), where \(\gamma=(h,d,n)\),
and even degree-zero chain endomorphisms \(U_{R,\gamma}\).
For a bounded finite-dimensional model their normalized trace is
\[
 t_{R,\gamma}=\lambda_{R,\gamma}
       \sum_{k,\epsilon}(-1)^{k+\epsilon}
       \operatorname{tr}
       (U_{R,\gamma}\mid V_{R,\gamma}^{k,\epsilon}).
\]
The intrinsic parity \(\epsilon\) is included only when that
grading is part of the state theory.  Cyclicity of the finite
supertrace makes chain-homotopic endomorphisms have the same value.
The normalization \(\lambda_{R,\gamma}\) is essential: the
coefficient \(n/4096\) above need not be an integer superdimension.
An inverse limit requires coefficientwise stabilization or a
trace-convergence theorem; a formal limit alone does not supply it.

If the construction starts from a boundary dg-category
\(\mathcal C_R\) and a chosen object \(b_R\), then
\(A_{b,R}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C_R}(b_R)\) is an
associative algebra by composition.  For a chosen coefficient
line \(\ell_R\), a cyclic functional
\(A_{b,R}\to\ell_R[-3]\), a cyclic pairing on its Hochschild
chains, and a pairing on a chiral centre are differently typed
data.  Passing between them requires the relevant comparison.
An object \(b_R\) also need not generate the boundary category.
A proposed compact realization must specify its trace domain
and prove compatibility with the state and all sewing maps.

\subsection{A finite-trace obstruction on a continuous carrier}

The domain matters even for a positive heat operator.  Write
\(M_f\) for multiplication by \(f\), and let
\(\mathcal H=L^2((0,\infty),dP)\) and
\(C_t=M_{e^{-tP^2}}\), with \(t>0\).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zbps-no-full-cyclic-extension}
There is no two-sided ideal \(\mathcal J\subset B(\mathcal H)\)
containing \(C_t\) and carrying a complex-linear functional
\(\tau:\mathcal J\to\C\) with
\(\tau(AB)=\tau(BA)\) for \(A\in\mathcal J\),
\(B\in B(\mathcal H)\), which agrees with the ordinary trace
on finite-rank operators.
No continuity or positivity assumption on the functional is
needed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(q=M_{\mathbf1_{[1,2]}}\).  Multiplication by
\(\mathbf1_{[1,2]}e^{tP^2}\) is bounded, so \(q\in\mathcal J\).
The range \(q\mathcal H\) is infinite-dimensional.  Choose a
unilateral shift \(V\) on it, extended by zero on its orthogonal
complement.  Then
\[
 V^*V=q,\qquad VV^*=q-p_1,
\]
where \(p_1\) is a rank-one projection.  As
\(V=qV\in\mathcal J\), cyclicity and linearity give
\(\tau(q)=\tau(q-p_1)=\tau(q)-1\), a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The commutative multiplier algebra has the positive semifinite
weight \(M_f\mapsto\int_0^\infty f(P)\,dP\) for \(f\ge0\),
finite on \(C_t\).  The proposition rules out a finite cyclic extension
to the specified full operator ideal.  It is not an obstruction
to every trace domain, and it says nothing about the Igusa
coefficients without an actual identification of their operator
carrier with this one.

\subsection{Marked kernels retain finite amplitudes}
\label{subsec:zbps-marked-kernels}

Choose a nonnegative measurable \(\rho\) such that
\(e^{-tP^2}\) and \(\rho(P)e^{-tP^2}\) lie in \(\mathcal H\)
for every \(t>0\).  Put
\[
 v_0(t)=e^{-tP^2},\qquad v_1(t)=\rho(P)e^{-tP^2},\qquad
 K_{ij}(t,s)=|v_i(t)\rangle\langle v_j(s)|.
\]
These rank-one kernels have the sewing law
\[
 K_{ij}(t,s)K_{k\ell}(u,v)
 =\langle v_j(s),v_k(u)\rangle K_{i\ell}(t,v).
\]
It follows directly by applying both sides to a vector.
The middle overlap is part of the product.  In particular,
\[
 \langle v_0(t),v_1(s)\rangle
   =\int_0^\infty\rho(P)e^{-(t+s)P^2}\,dP,
 \qquad
 \langle v_1(t),v_1(s)\rangle
   =\int_0^\infty\rho(P)^2e^{-(t+s)P^2}\,dP.
\]
One and two marked boundaries insert \(\rho\) and \(\rho^2\),
respectively.  Each \(K_{ii}(t,t)\) is positive; when
\(v_i(t)\ne0\), division by \(\|v_i(t)\|^2\) gives a positive
contraction.  For \(a>0\), \(C_a v_i(t)=v_i(a+t)\), so the heat action is compatible
with these marked states.  This construction preserves finite
matrix coefficients without assigning a finite trace to \(C_t\)
in the forbidden ideal.

\subsection{A closed scalar does not fix a cap}
\label{subsec:zbps-frobenius-sewing}

A finite example separates normalization from sewing.  On
\(A=\C e_1\oplus\C e_2\), take
\[
 e_ie_j=\delta_{ij}e_i,\qquad 1=e_1+e_2,
 \qquad \varepsilon(e_1)=w,\quad\varepsilon(e_2)=1-w,
 \qquad 0<w<1.
\]
This is a positive Frobenius algebra for its coordinatewise real
structure.  If \(p_1=w,p_2=1-w\), its coproduct and cup are
\[
 \Delta(e_i)=p_i^{-1}e_i\otimes e_i,
 \qquad \operatorname{coev}(1)=\sum_i p_i^{-1}e_i\otimes e_i.
\]
The inverse weights are forced by the snake identity.
A connected surface of genus \(g\) with \(q\) outgoing circles
sends \(e_i\) through multiplication, handles and coproducts with
the corresponding factor \(p_i^{1-g-q}\); in particular its
closed value is
\[
 Z_g=\sum_i p_i^{1-g}.
\]
Both \(w=1/2\) and \(w=1/3\) give \(Z_0=1\) and \(Z_1=2\),
but their genus-two values are \(4\) and \(9/2\).
Thus even a unit, a normalized sphere and the same torus value
do not determine all sewing data.  This example does not compare
two theories with the same complete family of closed operations.

\subsection{When a supertrace is positive}
\label{subsec:zbps-positive-supertrace}

Let a graded Hilbert space carry an odd self-adjoint operator
\(D\) and an even nonnegative self-adjoint Hamiltonian \(H\)
strongly commuting with \(D\).  Fix a domain of \(\beta>0\) on
which \(e^{-\beta H}\) is trace class.  Each finite-dimensional
\(H\)-eigenspace decomposes into \(\ker D\) and paired even/odd
states on its complement.  Their contributions cancel, giving
\[
 \operatorname{STr}(e^{-\beta H})
 =\operatorname{STr}(e^{-\beta H}|_{\ker D}).
\]
If the physical kernel is entirely even, this becomes an ordinary
positive trace and hence a Laplace transform of a positive
spectral measure.  Without that parity hypothesis the remaining
measure is signed.  A protected index therefore supplies neither
positivity nor an ordinary thermal interpretation merely by being
written as a supertrace.

\section{The compact realization question}
\label{sec:k3-e-threefold-realization}

The geometric states, Hilbert correspondences and reduced curve
counts constructed above have different coefficient theories.
A compact realization must compare those theories by maps
preserving their operations.  In particular, the prepared state
in \eqref{eq:zbps-prepared-elliptic-state}, the empty cut unit,
the local field vacuum and a scalar augmentation are distinct
data.  Equality of their eventual scalar series cannot identify
them.

\begin{scope}[The physical interpretation]
\label{scope:zbps-no-3d-gravity}
The primitive inverse Igusa identity is a theorem about reduced
numerical invariants.  A protected D-brane interpretation asks for
\[
 \Omega_X(h,d,n)\stackrel{\mathrm{phys}}{=}
                 \mathbf{DT}^X_{n,(\beta_h,d)}
\]
in a specified physical chamber.  This is additional to the
DT/PT/GW theorem.  Dyon counting provides a physical setting for
inverse automorphic products \cite{DVV}; the automorphic
construction itself is the singular theta lift \cite{B}.
Neither result constructs the state and sewing maps of a
three-dimensional gravitational sector obtained from
\(\mathrm{AdS}_3\times S^3\times K3\).  A metric path integral
or a boundary operator algebra requires those additional data.
\end{scope}

\subsection{The Hall and state comparison}
\label{sec:hall-borcherds-residual}

Fix a reduced oriented Hall coefficient theory on the desired
threefold moduli, including its extension flags, supported
pullbacks and pushforwards, relative unit and a geometric charge
map.  Denote its completed associative algebra by
\(\widehat{\mathcal H}^{\mathrm{red}}_X\).  This notation
presupposes the construction of those operations; it does not
assert that every reduction admits them.

Let \(\mathfrak n_+\) be the positive part of
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\).  At the ordinary coefficient level
the target is \(\widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+)}\), with a specified
positive-height completion.  For a chiral comparison on \(E\),
one must instead supply a geometric associative Ran algebra
\(F_X\) and the corresponding current target
\(\mathcal A_{+,E}\), with their Ran convolution tensor and
empty unit.  A current envelope and an ordinary enveloping
algebra have different domains.

\begin{conjecture}[Compact Hall and state comparison]
\label{conj:zbps-hall-borcherds-residual}
For a suitable reduced coefficient theory and compatible charge
dictionary, construct algebra and supported current comparisons
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-hall-to-borch}
 \Theta_{\mathrm{Hall}}:
 \widehat{\mathcal H}^{\mathrm{red}}_X
 \longrightarrow\widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+)},
 \qquad
 \Theta_E:F_X\longrightarrow\mathcal A_{+,E}.
\end{equation}
Their primitive maps must preserve the defining relations,
invariant pairing and its radical.  Their state maps must
intertwine the extension operations with independently defined
geometric fields and joining correspondences, including the
supported collision operations and vacuum insertions.  For the
Hilbert/orbifold field comparison, use the age and parity data
specified after Theorem~\ref{thm:zbps-unshifted-mckay}.
\end{conjecture}

The charge dictionary is part of the problem.  The Enriques
seed has half the K3 elliptic genus, but dividing an index by two
does not construct a functor from K3 threefold states to Enriques
states.  Nor does matching the coefficient \(-64\) construct
the extra differentials or primitive brackets.  The ordinary
cycle Hopf algebra has zero primitive commutator; the nonzero
cohomological joining commutator is an even second-order
operation.  A Hall comparison must identify the actual operation
complexes before comparing these expressions.

The gravity-line extension of this problem, as formulated in
\cite{ChiralBarCobarVolII}, further asks for a boundary action
and bulk sewing.  A Drinfeld double requires a compatible Hopf
pairing, integral form and completion.  Current enveloping along
\(E\) requires its own coefficient and support functor.  An
open--closed comparison must preserve the operations of the
two-coloured Swiss-cheese chiral-topological operad, including
its boundary and bulk compositions.  None is a formal consequence
of an isomorphism of graded vector spaces.  In particular, an
ambient fusion or reconstruction object cannot be identified
with a unitary spectral presentation without a comparison of
those structures.

Once source and target operation complexes are fixed, their
higher comparison equations are concrete.  If \(b_A,b_B\) are
their bar coderivations and the components below arity \(n\)
have been constructed, put
\[
 R_n=(b_BF_{<n}-F_{<n}b_A)_n.
\]
The next equation is
\(\partial_{\mathrm{Hom}}F_n=-R_n\).
For a contraction
\(\partial_{\mathrm{Hom}}h+h\partial_{\mathrm{Hom}}=1-P\),
the choice \(F_n=-hR_n\) solves it when \(PR_n=0\).
The normal-coordinate construction of Chapter~5 solves its own
coordinate equations; it does not compute these Hall-supported
residuals.  This locates the next comparison without assuming
its answer.

\subsection{A Pfaffian and its inverse square}
\label{subsec:zbps-pfaffian-comparison}

Suppose an independently constructed family over a base \(B\)
has a Pfaffian line \(\mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf}}\), section
\(p_X\), and squaring map
\[
 \sigma:\mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\otimes2}
                  \xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr L_{\det}.
\]
For a rank-two skew block
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&u\\-u&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
the Pfaffian is \(u\) and the determinant is \(u^2\).
The square therefore forgets the sign of its root.  A comparison
with a modular form also needs a parameter map
\(\kappa:B\to\mathcal A_2\) and an isomorphism
\[
 \iota_{\mathrm{aut}}:\mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf}}
       \xrightarrow{\sim}\kappa^*(\mathcal L^5\otimes\nu_{\Delta_5}).
\]
Here \(\mathcal L\) is the Hodge line on the Siegel modular
stack and \(\nu_{\Delta_5}\) is its multiplier character.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional square-root consequence]
\label{thm:zbps-square-root-consequence}
Assume the complete geometric hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-pfaffian-dirac}: the reduced orientation and
wall data, the finite oriented block comparisons in every active
degree with both parities, compatible effective lines and sections,
and the stated parameter, convergence and extension conditions.
Then, as meromorphic sections over \(B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-square-root}
 \iota_{\mathrm{aut}}(p_X)^{-2}
 =\kappa^*\Delta_5^{-2}
 =\kappa^*(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}})^{-1}
 =\kappa^*Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}^{K3\times E}.
\end{equation}
If a protected state theory independently satisfies the sewing
identity \(Z_X=-4096\,\iota_{\mathrm{aut}}(p_X)^{-2}\), then
its scalar equals \(-4096\,\kappa^*\Delta_5^{-2}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Pfaffian comparison gives
\(\iota_{\mathrm{aut}}(p_X)=\kappa^*\Delta_5\).
Squaring and inverting gives the first two equalities;
Theorem~\ref{thm:zbps-level-n-shadow} gives the last.
The additional sewing identity yields the state-theoretic
conclusion.  It is not implied by taking the reciprocal of a
section.
\end{proof}

The expression \(\operatorname{Pf}_{\mathrm{prot}}
(\mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}})\) abbreviates \(p_X\) only when
this geometric system has been constructed.  A proposed system
may be organized as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-dirac-igusa-pro-object}
\begin{split}
 \mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}}=\bigl\{\bigl(&
 \mathcal A^{E_3}_{X,R},F^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{X,R},\Gamma_{X,R},\Pi_X,
 \Phi_R,o_R,H_R,P_R^\Pi,C_{X,R},\Theta_{\mathrm{Kos},R},\\
 &\mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf},R},p_{X,R},\epsilon_{o,R},\mathrm{Rec}_R
 \bigr)\bigr\}_R.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The entries name, respectively, the chosen factorization
refinement and hybrid Ran carrier, charge indices and map,
coefficient complexes, orientation, extension operations,
primitive pieces, source coalgebra and Koszul comparison,
Pfaffian line and section, symmetry action and recognition map.
This denotes the pro-system, with its specified transition maps.
Each entry has its own construction and compatibility conditions.
In particular, a finite root window is not automatically a
proper geometric truncation, and a formal height limit need not
be an effective family.

The conditions \((D0),(O1),(O1)^+,(O2)\) express distinct
geometric requirements.  A degeneration system must compare
reduced coefficients, orientations, their specialization and
integration.  The reduced orientation must descend through
stabilizers, with coherent symmetry lifts.  A wall model must
compare the actual root action and its local Pfaffian divisor.
The finite Pfaffian condition \((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\) further
requires oriented block factorizations, units, all active parity
dimensions and the leading coefficient \(64x^\rho\).
These conditions control the conditional theorem; they are not
existence results for the compact family.

If \(\epsilon_o\) denotes the resulting root-line character,
the reflection comparison asks for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-orientation-equality}
 \epsilon_o|_{W^{(2)}(\Lambda_{II}^{2,1})}
 =\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}|_{W^{(2)}(\Lambda_{II}^{2,1})}.
\end{equation}
The rank-one wall calculation determines a sign only after the
actual root lift, the section transformation and the local frame
comparison have been specified.  A ratio of scalar formulas in
unrelated frames does not determine that character.  Global
modular descent also requires the full multiplier and boundary
normalization, beyond the reflection subgroup.

An inverse-square scalar is invariant under \(p_X\mapsto-p_X\).
Likewise, the identity has supertrace one on spaces of dimensions
\((1|0)\) and \((2|1)\).  These examples exhibit information
lost by the two operations.  A virtual determinant constructed
from Euler superdimensions sees the product exponents, but does
not supply brackets, Hopf-pairing radicals, a Pfaffian line or a
root of its symmetry action.  The automorphic product, its BKM
denominator and a geometric Pfaffian meet only after the stated
comparisons.  In the Gritsenko--Nikulin coordinates the algebraic
denominator is \(64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\); the change of argument
is part of that lattice convention.

\begin{remark}[Five numerical invariants]
\label{rem:zbps-kappa-stratum}
Let \(r_{\mathrm{Gram}}\) be the rank of the three-coordinate
Gram lattice.  Define \(\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}=\chi(\mathcal O_X)\),
let \(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}\) denote the same
alternating Hodge sum in the indicated convention, and put
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)\) and
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}=\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zbps-kappa-tuple}
 (\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}},
 r_{\mathrm{Gram}},\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}})
 =(0,0,3,5,24).
\end{equation}
The two zeroes come from \(1-1+1-1\).  The modular weight is
\(f(0,0)/2=5\) \cite{Borcherds95,Gritsenko99}, while
\(\chi(\mathcal O_S)=2\).  Thus an additive formula
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}+\chi(\mathcal O_S)\)
would assert \(5=2\).  A genus-one Heisenberg bar coefficient
instead requires a chosen complex, trace and normalized class;
rescaling its trace changes it without changing \(r_{\mathrm{Gram}}\).
An identification with the Gram rank requires its own map.
\end{remark}

\section{Further structures}
\label{sec:zbps-open-problems}

An associative source comparison must keep the carrier and its
tensor product fixed.  If \(C_X\) is a chiral coalgebra, a centre
comparison goes through a specified cobar algebra or comodule
category.  A filtered quasi-isomorphism of underlying complexes
\(A_b\simeq C_X\) does not compare their multiplications,
cooperations or cyclic functionals.  Applying chiral Hochschild
or boundary-realization constructions
\cite{ChiralBarCobarVolI,ChiralBarCobarVolII,ChiralBarCobarVolIV}
therefore requires an associative comparison preserving those
operations, completion, support and transitions.  Lie--Com
chiral Koszul duality cannot provide it by a change of notation.

A local mixed holomorphic--topological theory raises another
comparison problem.  A model on
\(\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\times\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}\) specifies both
topological and holomorphic directions.  Its coefficient and operator complexes
must descend, and the quantum master equation and anomaly class
must be defined in the actual global deformation complex.
Kunneth gives
\[
 H^1(S\times E,\mathcal O_{S\times E})\cong\C.
\]
This nonzero group neither identifies the full obstruction complex
nor proves that an obstruction class is nonzero.  A quartic
invariant such as \(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ad}}(x^4)\)
likewise specifies an anomaly question only after its BV descent
and form degrees are fixed.  Formal local Hamiltonian data alone
do not produce the compact sewing identity.

For a gravitational boundary algebra, the Brown--Henneaux relation
\(c=3\ell/(2G_N)\) fixes a Virasoro parameter.  With the usual
normalization its bracket is
\[
 [T_\lambda T]=\partial T+2\lambda T+\frac c{12}\lambda^3.
\]
A comparison with a K3 Heisenberg construction must identify the
actual bracket, state and trace normalization.  The Borcherds
weight \(5\) does not determine that central charge.  Constructing
an acting algebra with determinant section \(\Delta_5^2\),
proving that its partition function is the reciprocal section,
and relating it to \(p_X\) remain separate requirements.

\subsection{Mathieu and umbral structures}
\label{subsec:zbps-moonshine}

The K3 elliptic genus \(2\phi_{0,1}\) has an \(N=4\)
superconformal-character decomposition \cite{EOT}.  After the
massless terms are separated, its mock-modular series has form
\[
 \Sigma(\tau)=-2q^{-1/8}+\sum_{n\ge1}A_nq^{n-1/8}.
\]
The Mathieu twining functions of \cite{GHV} determine class
functions, and the module theorem proves that the massive
coefficients are actual \(M_{24}\)-characters
\cite{GannonUmbral}.  This character theorem does not supply a
simultaneous \(M_{24}\)-action on every geometric K3 state model.

Umbral moonshine begins with the six-group construction of
\cite{CDH} and extends to the twenty-three Niemeier lattices with
nonempty root systems.\footnote{M.~Cheng, J.~Duncan and J.~Harvey,
\emph{Umbral moonshine and the Niemeier lattices},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5793}{\S1}.}
The \(A_1^{24}\) case gives Mathieu moonshine.
A twining construction changes the coefficient data and uses
the powers of the group element. For the Mathieu inputs, the full
lifts are meromorphic Borcherds products \cite{WangWilliamsMathieu}.
Chapter~\ref{ch:open-obstructions} gives their lattices, weights
and the odd-divisor obstruction to particular square roots.
These results use the complete twining and cusp data; the untwined
coefficients \(f(n,\ell)\) alone do not define the lift.
An equivariant denominator algebra or a geometric Hall action
requires further constructions.

A compact comparison must identify the coefficient and state
theories on their actual supports, then compare the oriented
modular lines and sewing functionals.  The inverse Igusa series
fixes the scalar that these maps must recover.
