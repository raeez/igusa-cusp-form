\chapter{The remaining geometric comparisons}
\label{ch:open-obstructions}

\section{From the denominator to a compact realization}
\label{sec:open-obstructions-and-subsequent-constructions}

The automorphic product and the Borcherds superalgebra give two
constructions of the same normalized denominator. The compact
realization problem asks for geometric objects and maps behind that
identity. Its source is a system of moduli and extension
correspondences on \(X=S\times E\); its target includes the root
spaces with both parities, their brackets, a modular line and a
state carrying the required operators. Equality of scalar products
cannot supply these structures.

The established reference identities are
\[
 D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
 \Xi=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})(Z)=D_5(2Z).
\]
Their product, theta and singular-lift descriptions are developed
in Chapters~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic}, \ref{ch:view2-bkm} and
\ref{ch:singular-theta-lift} from \cite{Borcherds95,GN}.
The geometric Pfaffian would be a section in an independently
constructed line. Identifying it with \(\Delta_5\) requires the
line map, its character and the normalized section comparison.

Several genuine geometric constructions already lie between the
compact problem and these targets. The spherical triple has a
cyclic formal deformation model and compatible formal flags.
Ordinary critical Hall multiplication has its orientation and
support hypotheses. Hilbert schemes carry elliptic coefficient
bundles, geometric incidence operators and scalar indices.
The spectral section has explicit critical coefficients and
categorical realizations. Each supplies a particular object or map;
none alone identifies the complete reduced compact Hall system
with the denominator algebra.

The finite geometric datum of
Section~\ref{subsec:eight-finite-constructions} is therefore kept
as a system
\[
 \{\mathfrak D^{\mathrm{DI}}_{X,R}\}_R.
\]
Its fourteen components include the fields, hybrid supports,
charges and projection, critical coefficients and roots, Hall
operations and primitives, coalgebra and associative comparison,
Pfaffian line and section, Weyl action and recognition map.
The morphism conditions \textup{(M1)}--\textup{(M8)} specify how
these components commute. This notation denotes a pro-object
once its maps exist; it does not assert an effective infinite
moduli object or an ordinary componentwise inverse limit.

\subsection{The four orientation and degeneration conditions}
\label{subsec:four-named-obstructions}

The names \((D0),(O1),(O1)^+,(O2)\) refer to four construction
conditions. They are not four classes in one cohomology group.
The finite Pfaffian comparison \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is additional.
Appendix~\ref{app:obstruction-discharges} gives their precise
recipients and proves the consequences when the data exist.

\subsubsection{D0 specialization}
\label{subsubsec:obstruction-D0}

The D0 problem asks for specialization of reduced deformation
complexes, oriented critical coefficients, flag operations and
Pfaffian sections to zero-dimensional objects on the threefold.
The first requirement is an actual family and its charge map. The stability and moduli constructions of \cite{LiuProductStability,PiyaratneTodaModuli3folds} motivate the problem; a compact retained Hall slice still needs its own boundedness proof.
For sheaves on a fixed polarized \(X\), flatness preserves the
Hilbert polynomial. A nonzero curve class cannot become a pure
D0 sheaf in such a family. A comparison involving those two
charges must change the geometric or categorical recipient and
construct its specialization functor.

The ambient shifted symplectic structure \cite{PTVV2013} does
not remove scalar automorphisms or their dual obstruction
directions. A reduced two-term complex needs the actual reduction
and its amplitude proof. Cosection localization
\cite{KiemLi2013} applies to its specified obstruction theory;
it does not automatically produce a sheaf of reduced vanishing
cycles. Surjectivity in nonzero curve class cannot simply be
continued to the D0 class.

A D0 specialization system must retain object, extension, mixed,
wrapped and higher flag loci, the coefficient specialization maps,
root pairs and their cross determinants, support conditions and
stage transitions. The critical-sheaf and orientation theorems
\cite{BBDJS2015,JoyceUpmeier} enter after those hypotheses are
met. Theorem~\ref{thm:G-D0} constructs the resulting pro-system
from the supplied maps; effectivity remains an additional limit
statement. A K3 Hilbert-scheme index tests its scalar consequence
but does not construct this threefold specialization.

\subsubsection{Orientation on the translation quotient}
\label{subsubsec:obstruction-O1}

For a retained prequotient locus \(B\), root the actual reduced
determinant line \(\mathscr D\) on \([B/E]\):
\[
 (o,\rho:o^{\otimes2}\simeq\mathscr D).
\]
The obstruction is its Kummer class in \(H^2([B/E],\mu_2)\).
When it vanishes, the isomorphism classes of root pairs form an
\(H^1([B/E],\mu_2)\)-torsor. Neither rational
Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch nor translation invariance supplies
the required root with its square map.

There are two separate descent issues. A universal object on a
scalar gerbe has weight one and need not descend; its endomorphism
complex has weight zero and can descend with its natural maps.
A root on the quotient stack can carry a nontrivial stabilizer
character, while coarse descent requires trivial stabilizer action.
Removing common scalars also need not make all remaining inertia
finite.

The finite-stabilizer calculations in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:G-Klein-four} and
\ref{lem:G-two-primary-stabilizer} identify exact equations after
the obstruction has been reduced to their classifying-space edge.
For \(E[2]\), all three cyclic restrictions detect a quadratic
class. For the two-primary group \((\mathbb Z/2^a)^2\),
\(a\ge2\), the mixed class \(x_1x_2\) is invisible on cyclic
subgroups. In either case, the character of the chosen root is
additional degree-one data. Coherent primitives and flag
comparisons are needed to glue these local choices.

\subsubsection{Weyl transport of the root}
\label{subsubsec:obstruction-O1plus}

The type-II Coxeter subgroup
\[
 W=\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\mid s_i^2=1\rangle
\]
acts on the abstract root datum. Its geometric action requires
correspondences or functors on the actual compact recipient and
lifts to the root pairs. The lifts have an action-groupoid sign
defect \(c_o\). A chosen primitive for that defect, compatible
with quotient, flag and stage maps, makes the action honest.
Vanishing of an abstract class without these choices does not
supply the transported orientation.

If the residual generator actions are constant signs and the
section-compatible skew walls give \(-1\), then
Theorem~\ref{thm:G-O1-plus} identifies the character with
\(\det_W\). This is the restriction of the homogeneous
orthogonal character of \(\Delta_5\). The chamber-symmetry
extension and the symplectic automorphy factor are separate.
The abelianization \(W_{\mathrm{ab}}=(\mathbb Z/2)^3\)
classifies constant sign characters; it does not classify the
geometric root gerbe.

\subsubsection{Skew wall geometry}
\label{subsubsec:obstruction-O2}

A simple Pfaffian wall has the rank-two skew normal block
\[
 D_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \operatorname{pf}(D_u)=u,
 \qquad \det D_u=u^2.
\]
The complex is placed in degrees \([-1,0]\), with Pfaffian line
\(\det V^\vee\). A reflection lift and an invariant tangential
unit then give the local sign \(-1\). The scalar complex with
differential \(u\) has determinant \(u\) and is a different
object. Appendix~\ref{app:pfaffian-wall-normal-form} proves the
skew calculation and its precise character consequence.

The global condition requires actual wall objects, full charge
matches, reduced charts, overlaps and their cocycles, Weyl and
stabilizer maps, boundary coverage and compatible transitions.
Any assertion that only type-II walls contribute must also exclude
other geometric components. One normal chart does not provide
this atlas.

The abstract set of twelve signs has \(2^{12}=4096\) elements.
The squared theta-leading coefficient is also \(64^2=4096\).
A geometric wall count needs a bijection or a weighted integration
calculation on the actual atlas. Neither of those numerical
equalities supplies it. In particular,
\(L_{\mathrm{II}}/2L_{\mathrm{II}}\) has eight elements.

With all four conditions and \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) supplied,
Theorem~\ref{thm:G-four-obstruction-criterion} yields the geometric
Pfaffian and its \(W\)-character. Primitive recognition still
requires \textup{(S1)}--\textup{(S10)}. The trace still requires a
state and cyclic comparison. The hypothesis-deletion statements
of Proposition~\ref{prop:ch6-hypothesis-deletion-residues} retain
these distinctions.

\section{Mathieu products and the equivariant question}
\label{subsec:moonshine-adjacency}

The K3 elliptic genus is \(2\phi_{0,1}\). Its expansion in
small \(N=4\) superconformal characters led Eguchi, Ooguri and
Tachikawa to Mathieu moonshine \cite{EOT}. Gannon proves existence
of the graded \(M_{24}\)-module with the prescribed character
multiplicities \cite{GannonUmbral}. The mock modular form and its
nonholomorphic completion are functions associated with that
module; the completion is not itself a graded module. Its massive
level grading also differs from a full Jacobi or root grading.

Umbral moonshine has twenty-three cases, indexed by Niemeier
lattices with nonempty root systems \(X\), with groups
\[
 G^X=\operatorname{Aut}(N^X)/W^X.
\]
The \(A_1^{24}\) case is Mathieu moonshine. Duncan, Griffin and
Ono prove module existence for the other twenty-two cases
\cite{DuncanGriffinOnoUmbral}, following the Niemeier formulation
of \cite{CDHNiemeier}, which extends the six-group construction
of \cite{CDH}. The Leech lattice has no roots and lies outside
this list. These module-existence theorems do not construct a
Lie bracket, a vertex algebra or a compact Hall action. The K3
interpretations of \cite{GHV,ChengHarrison} address additional
geometric and conformal-field-theoretic structure.

\subsection{The full multiplicative lifts}

Use the full twined genus normalization
\[
 \varphi_g(\tau,z)
   =\sum_{n\ge0,\,l\in\mathbb Z}c_g(4n-l^2)q^n r^l,
 \qquad \varphi_{1A}=2\phi_{0,1}.
\]
The scalar functions are known for all twenty-six conjugacy
classes; in particular the order-seven and order-fifteen genera
are covered by \cite{GHVAllTwining}. Their multiplicative product
uses all powers of \(g\):
\[
 \Phi_g(Z)=qrs
 \prod_{(n,l,m)>0}
 \exp\left(-\sum_{a\ge1}
   \frac{c_{g^a}(4nm-l^2)}{a}(q^nr^ls^m)^a\right).
\]
Here positivity is the cusp convention of
Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic}. At the identity,
\[
 \Phi_{1A}=\Xi=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
The half-genus convention divides the seed by two; it must not be
mixed with this full-genus product.

Wang--Williams prove that every \(\Phi_g\) is a meromorphic
Borcherds product on
\[
 L_g=U(N_g)\oplus U\oplus A_1,
 \qquad \operatorname{sign}(L_g)=(3,2),
\]
where \(N_g\) is the level of the twining datum
\cite[Theorem~1.3]{WangWilliamsMathieu}. The input is the complete
tuple \((\varphi_{g^d})_{d\mid N_g}\), with its cusp data.
For cycle shape \(\prod_t t^{b_t}\), the weight is
\[
 k_g=\frac12\sum_t b_t-2.
\]
Thus a single constant coefficient at the infinity cusp of
\(\varphi_g\) does not determine the answer by the untwined
level-one rule. The lattice, the power tuple and all relevant
cusps are part of the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[The order-seven and order-fifteen products]
\label{conj:order-seven-fifteen-twined-denominators}
The four classes \(7A,7B,15A,15B\) give two scalar products:
\[
 \Phi_{7A}=\Phi_{7B},\qquad \Phi_{15A}=\Phi_{15B}.
\]
Their levels and weights are respectively \((7,1)\) and
\((15,0)\). Neither scalar product has a meromorphic square root
on \(\mathbb H_2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The paired classes have identical twining functions and identical
power tuples for this construction. Their cycle shapes are
\(1^3 7^3\) and \(1\cdot3\cdot5\cdot15\); the weight formula
gives \(6/2-2=1\) and \(4/2-2=0\).

The obstruction to a root can be read from the actual divisor.
In \(U(N)\oplus A_1\), choose \((e,f)=N\), \(h^2=2\),
with the other pairings zero. Then
\[
 Q(ue+wh+vf)=Nuv+w^2.
\]
The primitive dual-lattice vectors
\[
 v_7=\frac e7+\frac h2-\frac f7,
 \qquad
 v_{15}=\frac e{15}+\frac h2-\frac{2f}{15}
\]
have norms
\[
 Q(v_7)=\frac3{28},\qquad Q(v_{15})=\frac7{60}.
\]
The corresponding principal-part coefficients in the vector-valued
inputs are \(-1\) \cite[Appendix~A]{WangWilliamsMathieu}.
The largest negative index magnitude there is \(1/4\), while
\(4Q(v_7)>1/4\) and \(4Q(v_{15})>1/4\). Consequently no
positive multiple \(a v_g\), \(a\ge2\), contributes to the
divisor multiplicity. The Borcherds multiplicity formula therefore
gives a simple pole along each indicated generic hyperplane.
A meromorphic square has even divisor order, so a meromorphic
square root cannot exist. This is also the square-root
classification in \cite[Theorem~1.3(2)]{WangWilliamsMathieu}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the obstruction excludes]
\label{rem:order-seven-fifteen-cross-checks}
A multiplier or an unramified level cover does not change the odd
local divisor order. A branched cover could change it, but would
change the recipient. Thus a construction requiring these full
products to be squares of meromorphic Pfaffian denominators on
\(\mathbb H_2\) is obstructed. The full products themselves
exist. Their weights \(1\) and \(0\) are not a failure of
modularity, and the cyclotomic degrees \(\varphi(7)=6\) and
\(\varphi(15)=8\) are not such an obstruction either.
\end{remark}

The four conjugacy-class labels still matter for representation
theory even though this scalar construction identifies each pair.
They have six and four orbits, respectively, in the twenty-four
point permutation representation. The geometric K3 symmetry
criterion requires at least five orbits: the order-seven classes
are realized by geometric symplectic automorphisms, while the
order-fifteen classes fail this criterion
\cite[\S2.1]{GaberdielVolpatoOrbifoldK3}. This has a different
scope from existence of their scalar twining functions.
Neither equality of paired functions nor a finite
Fourier comparison constructs a geometric action.

\subsection{The remaining equivariant algebra}

The open comparison is an equivariant lift with actual
\(M_{24}\)-representations in the required Jacobi and root
degrees, compatible brackets or Hall operations, and the full
power-trace formula above. An action on an independently defined
state must produce the twining operations rather than be assigned
after the scalar functions are known. If a denominator algebra is
claimed, its simple generators, both parities, relations and
invariant form must be constructed in the relevant lattice and
completion.

The untwined half-genus algebra \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\),
the full products \(\Phi_g\), and possible umbral algebraic
realizations are therefore separate targets. The theorem rules
out the stated meromorphic square-root requirement for orders
seven and fifteen; it does not rule out every equivariant
algebraic construction. Nor does module existence for the umbral
groups construct twenty-three Borcherds superalgebras or a map
from the Mathieu classes to Niemeier root systems.

The selected low-level frame of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bkm-kappa-universal} retains its exact hypotheses.
Its list is not a classification of all twined Borcherds products.
Likewise the Monster and Fake Monster denominator constructions
provide comparison examples, not compact realizations on
\(K3\times E\) or \(K3\times K3\times E\) merely from their
lattice ranks. The root lattice \(\mathrm{II}_{25,1}\) and the
orthogonal automorphic lattice \(\mathrm{II}_{26,2}\) have
different roles in the Fake Monster example.

\section{States, fusion and acting algebras}
\label{subsec:subsequent-constructions}

\subsection{The Hall--Borcherds state comparison}
\label{subsubsec:open-hall-borcherds}

The trace target is \(\Delta_5^{-2}\) in the cusp normalization
used here. It is already a well-defined scalar meromorphic
function. To derive it as a compact protected trace one needs an
independently constructed state, operators, cyclic structure and
normalization. Appendix~\ref{app:obstruction-discharges} gives a
sufficient state comparison and derives the product from its
oscillators. A degree \(-3\) pairing on a centre alone is not
that trace construction.

The algebraic and categorical recipients must be typed. If
\(C_X\) is a counital conilpotent coalgebra, its cobar
\(\Omega C_X\) is an associative algebra. A boundary endomorphism
algebra can be compared with that cobar, and its bar with \(C_X\).
Ordinary Hochschild cochains, an internal factorization centre,
cyclic chains and endomorphisms of the state are different objects.
Any proposed comparison must preserve the coefficient tensor,
unit, support and completion as well as the indicated operations
\cite{ChiralBarCobarVolI,ChiralBarCobarVolII}.

Scalar nonuniqueness has a simple exact witness. The underlying
graded super vector space of \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) can be
given the zero bracket without changing its root superdeterminant,
as in Lemma~\ref{lem:signed-dimensions-miss}. An even-odd pair
with zero differential also has zero superdimension but nonzero
cohomology. Thus even the full scalar product does not determine
the bracket or the state complex. An acyclic summand, by contrast,
would not by itself distinguish quasi-isomorphism types.

A Hall--Borcherds operator comparison additionally requires the
positive Hall map, its actual tensor and cooperation, an opposite
half and appropriate pairing when a double is claimed, and the
current or factorization maps on \(E\). The invariant Lie form
on primitives is not a full Hopf pairing on their ordinary
nonabelian enveloping algebra. The geometric realization and
normalization of a cyclic trace are further equations, not
consequences of those names.

\subsection{A compact chain-fusion comparison}
\label{subsubsec:open-chain-fusion}

Fix a Calabi--Yau category \(\mathcal C_X\), an admissible chart
\((\Sigma_{d-1},C)\) and a specified open factorization category.
A proposed chain-fusion comparison asks for a boundary condition
\(b(X,\Sigma_{d-1},C)\) and a map
\[
 \Phi_d^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)}(\mathcal C_X)
       \longrightarrow
 R\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}
       \bigl(b(X,\Sigma_{d-1},C)\bigr),
\]
with a definition of the source functor \(\Phi_d\), its tensor,
and its clutching, cyclic and Calabi--Yau compatibilities.
An equivalence requires an inverse on the claimed domain.
A boundary object does not automatically generate that domain;
both supported actions and the relevant generation or Morita
hypotheses must be proved.

For \(X=K3\times E\), the proposed chiral specialization has
reference target
\[
 U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{heis}_{\mathrm{Muk}})
       \otimes U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak g^{\mathrm{BPS}}_{K3}).
\]
The recipient must specify whether these are
Beilinson--Drinfeld current envelopes or corresponding objects on
\(\operatorname{Ran}(E)\), and which tensor is used.
This target notation does not define
\(\operatorname{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{K3,E}\).
The map requires the compact field complex, local and wrapped
supports, coefficient comparisons and factorization operations.
If an \(E_3\)- or BV model is used, its quantum master equation,
anomaly, framing and compact-support descent must hold on those
same complexes. The K3 surface, the geometric elliptic curve,
the equivariant elliptic parameter and the world-sheet curve
retain their distinct roles until compared by actual maps.

The outstanding boundary object is
\(b(K3\times E,\Sigma_2,E)\) in this specified category.
Local affine or toric models do not construct it on the compact
threefold. Nor is a divisor on the geometric curve \(E\)
automatically an Igusa cusp in Siegel space; that identification
would require its own parameter map. Other compact non-toric
threefolds, including Borcea--Voisin and Schoen examples, require
their own boundary constructions.

The finite first-root comparisons do not settle this problem.
The height bound of a root diagram, a geometric HN bound and a
formal precision are different indices. A comparison at one
finite stage does not give an equivalence at every stage, and
Francis--Gaitsgory Lie--commutative duality does not supply the
required associative-to-associative comparison.

\subsection{A gravity-line action}
\label{subsubsec:open-gravity-line}

A gravity-line realization would consist of an acting algebra, a
module and cyclic sewing operations, with a determinant section
identified with \(\Delta_5^2\) and a protected state trace
identified with its reciprocal. The determinant has weight ten;
its primitive square root has weight five. These are automorphic
weights, not by themselves a central charge or a count of metric
degrees of freedom. If a pentagon sewing diagram is used, its
five composites, homotopies and determinant comparison must be
constructed before assigning that scalar section to it.

Several numerical invariants coexist in this problem:
\[
 \bigl(\chi(\mathcal O_{S\times E}),
       \sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(S\times E),
       \operatorname{rk}\mathfrak h_{\Delta_5},
       \operatorname{wt}\Delta_5,
       \operatorname{rk}\widetilde H^*(S,\mathbb Z)\bigr)
       =(0,0,3,5,24).
\]
The first two entries are zero because the elliptic curve has
\(\chi(\mathcal O_E)=0\). The third is the rank of the
reference Cartan; calling it a geometric Heisenberg rank requires
the Hall comparison. The fourth is \(f(0,0)/2=10/2\), and
the last is the Mukai rank, equal to \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)\).
This tuple consists of different invariants. The numerical equality
\(3+2=5\) is not a construction relating their categories or
lines, and \(\chi(\mathcal O_S)=2\) differs from
\(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)=24\).

A gravitational path-integral interpretation further needs a
specified physical theory and the applicable asymptotic symmetry,
modularity, vacuum and saddle hypotheses. The examples and
constraints of \cite{HM} do not derive such a theory from the
Igusa product. Likewise a prepared state is additional structure;
normalizing a trace or adjoining a scalar vacuum does not solve
its cyclic and operator compatibility equations.

\section{The first geometric maps still required}

The compact construction begins with the global reduced oriented
coefficient pull--push on extension flags. It must preserve the
common translation torsor, cross determinants, support conditions
and full higher coherences. A compatible coproduct then needs its
own coefficient maps and tensor. The charge-two obstruction for
ordinary-Koszul cooperation on the specified polynomial shuffle
carrier does not rule out every Hall recipient, but it prevents
using that carrier without changing the obstructed requirement.
A meromorphic tensor, localization or different coefficient
category is a new construction to be compared.

Primitive recognition requires actual representatives and their
product, coproduct, unit, counit, bracket and invariant-form
matrices. The relation kernel, radical quotient, generation and
PBW maps must be proved on finite word spaces containing every
intermediate operation. In the first interior degree the target
is \(29\mid93\), not merely superdimension \(-64\).
The rank-28 differential works through height five and fails at
height six in its unchanged presentation. A different derived
presentation must retain its own comparison and cannot silently
inherit the obstructed one.

Compatible finite comparisons must exhaust the operations and
respect their genuine transition maps. Levelwise isomorphisms are
sufficient for a pro-isomorphism, but not necessary in general.
Cohomology commutes with a countable derived inverse limit only
with control of the relevant \(\varprojlim{}^1\) term.
Root-line effectivity, primitive kernels and PBW completions each
have their own comparison requirements.

The period construction of Chapter~\ref{ch:view4-mirror} gives a
classical family of lattice-polarized K3 surfaces on a suitable
marked or level cover of the Siegel threefold. The remaining
compact mirror map must still connect
the chosen threefold source, its Hodge or determinant line and its
geometric section to that period family. The even cohomology of
\(S\times E\) cannot recover a varying elliptic period, and a
rank-five polarized weight-three quotient is obstructed by its
alternating polarization. These exclude the specified routes,
not every possible geometric comparison.

The resulting problem remains to construct the compact source,
its operations, modular line and cyclic state together, with maps
to the established formal, algebraic and automorphic objects.
The classical period model, full Mathieu products, local critical
models and finite operator calculations sharpen that problem.
They leave the original geometric realization to be supplied by
those operation-preserving maps.
