\chapter{Pfaffians, orientation characters and primitive recognition}
\label{ch:pfaffian-dirac}

The automorphic form and the BKM denominator are already related by
an explicit theorem:
\[
 \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})(Z)
 =64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]
The compact \(K3\times E\) problem asks for more.  It asks for a
geometric Pfaffian section, a coherent action on its orientation,
and a realization of the BKM bracket on the geometric primitive
spaces.  These constructions have different sources and require
different comparison maps.  This chapter states the consequences
of supplying them, while keeping the missing compact constructions
visible.

The real simple generators have the usual Kac--Moody relations
\cite{KAC:1}.  The generalized construction of \cite{BorcherdsGKM88}
admits imaginary simple roots; the parity and multiplicity data for
this denominator are those of Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}.
The normalization and doubled modular
variable in the displayed formula are those of the type-II lattice in
Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}.  The Pfaffian comparison uses
\(\Delta_5\) itself, after a specified parameter map and an
isomorphism of lines.  Its reflection character is compared with
the homogeneous orthogonal character
\(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}\), not directly with the symplectic
Maass character \(\nu_{\Delta_5}\) of a lift \cite{MAASS:1}.

\section{The geometric data and their comparisons}
\label{sec:ch6-data}

\subsection{A system of finite geometric objects}
\label{subsec:ch6-pro-object}

Fix a cofiltered system of finite geometric moduli problems indexed
by \(R\), with their actual transition maps.  When constructed, a
Dirac--Igusa system records the following objects and maps:
\[
 \left(
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal A_{X,R},\ F^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{X,R},\
 \Gamma_{X,R},\ \Pi_X,\ \Phi_R,\ o_R,\ H_R,\ P_R^\Pi,\\
 C_{X,R},\ \Theta_{\mathrm{Kos},R},\
 \mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf},R},\ \operatorname{pf}_{X,R},\
 \epsilon_{o,R},\ \mathrm{Rec}_R
 \end{gathered}
 \right)_R.
\]
Here \(F^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) is the specified formal or geometric
coefficient object, \(\Gamma\) its charge group, \(\Pi_X\) the
specified normal-ordered charge homomorphism, and \(\Phi\) its critical
coefficient realization.  The symbol
\(o\) denotes its orientation, \(H\) the algebra carrying the supplied Hall
operations, and \(P^\Pi\) its descended primitive Lie algebra.
The coalgebra \(C\) and map \(\Theta_{\mathrm{Kos}}\) belong to a
separately specified bar--cobar comparison.  The remaining entries
are the Pfaffian line and section, an orientation character when
extractable from its action, and the primitive recognition map.
An enhanced factorization algebra structure on \(\mathcal A\),
including any claimed \(E_3\) structure, is further data with its
operations and coefficient category; the list does not construct it.

If all these data and their transitions are given, they define a
pro-object \(\mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}}\) in their common category
of structured objects.  A pro-object is a diagram, rather than an
assertion that a geometric inverse limit exists.  For ordinary
pro-objects the formula is
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Pro}(\mathcal C)}
 ((X_i),(Y_j))
 =\varprojlim_j\varinjlim_i\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(X_i,Y_j);
\]
in a derived setting the corresponding mapping spaces and homotopy
limits are used.  Cofinal reindexing preserves the pro-object.
Passing from it to an effective geometric line, a completed algebra
or an analytic operator requires the relevant comparison in each
case.  A finite root set is not a finite moduli space, and a
Harder--Narasimhan bound is not automatically a compatible root-height
quotient.

\begin{remark}[Three objects attached to \(\Delta_5\)]
\label{rem:ch6-three-delta-names}
The automorphic section \(\mathcal D_X=\Delta_5\), the denominator
of \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\), and a putative compact Pfaffian
section have different definitions.  The first two are identified
by the classical automorphic and BKM theorems
\cite{Borcherds95,GN,GNPartI,GNII}; the third needs the geometric
line and section comparison below.  Writing the same scalar
expansion does not identify their bases, coefficient systems or
operations.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reduction, orientation, transport and walls}
\label{subsec:ch6-obstructions}

We retain the names \((D0),(O1),(O1)^+,(O2)\) for four geometric
requirements.  They refer to data and equations, rather than four
universal scalar obstruction classes.

The \((D0)\) datum is a specified degeneration from the compact
problem to the chosen endpoint, together with finite object,
extension and complete-flag moduli, their derived enhancements and
transitions.  The reduced complexes, coefficient specialization,
orientation and Pfaffian comparisons must be supplied on that
family.  A semiregularity functional on \(\operatorname{Ext}^2\)
can help construct a reduced obstruction theory; it does not alone
give a nondegenerate reduced shifted symplectic form or a
vanishing-cycle specialization functor.  Similarly, a reduced
virtual-dimension shift does not by itself identify orientation
lines by tensoring with a parity symbol.

The \((O1)\) datum is the strong reduced orientation of
Definition~\ref{def:O1-strong-reduced-orientation}.  Its extension
formula contains the cross determinant \(L(F,G)\), the computed
reduction excess and the complete-flag coherence.  It descends
through the common elliptic quotient as a root pair.  Stabilizer
characters are allowed on the quotient stack.  If the chosen theory
also asks for a coarse or inertia-trivial orientation, their
vanishing is an additional condition.  Its global Kummer class
cannot in general be recovered solely from pointwise stabilizer
restrictions or a chosen representative of a Borel filtration term.

The \((O1)^+\) datum consists of the geometric Weyl transports
and coherent root lifts of Definition~\ref{def:O1-plus-weyl-transport}.
It includes a trivialization of the sign cocycle on the actual
action groupoid, the involutive relations and transport of all
extension and quotient identifications.  An orbit groupoid has the
cohomology of its stabilizer; it is not automatically the
classifying space of the whole Weyl group.  Extracting a scalar
character further needs the reference fibre identifications of
Definition~\ref{def:orientation-character-epsilon-o}.

The \((O2)\) datum is a complete type-II wall atlas with compatible
normal skew blocks
\[
 D_{u_i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&u_i\\-u_i&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad\operatorname{Pf}(D_{u_i})=u_i.
\]
Each stipulated base reflection sends \(u_i\) to \(-u_i\).
Its reference comparison preserves the tangential section and unit;
the actual root lift carries the Pfaffian section to its pullback.  These charts
must cover the relevant components and boundary strata, and their
orientation, coefficient and integration comparisons must agree on
overlaps.  A generic rank-one calculation alone supplies neither
coverage nor those equations.  In particular the middle wall
\(\delta_2\leftrightarrow(0,1,1)\) needs a geometric object in
that degree.  If the chosen charge comparison assigns it positive
geometric elliptic degree, the support obstruction of
Section~\ref{subsec:hybrid-ran-carrier} requires a wrapped source object.
The Gram label alone does not establish that geometric degree.

The finite geometric Pfaffian datum \((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\) of
Definition~\ref{def:finite-geometric-pfaffian-datum} is additional.
It supplies the skew realization, actual section, oriented block
factorization, every residual unit, the parameter map and the line
comparison.  Neither a list of ranks nor an orientation alone
supplies that section.

\begin{proposition}[What remains when a comparison is absent]
\label{prop:ch6-hypothesis-deletion-residues}
A supplied orientation without Weyl lifts remains a root pair;
there is then no specified \(W\)-action on it.  Coherent lifts
without wall charts give an action but no computed simple-wall
signs.  These orientation data without a Pfaffian realization do
not specify a section.  A Pfaffian section without a parameter and
line comparison is not yet a section of the automorphic line.
Finally, a section identity without primitive representatives and
their relations does not identify the source Lie algebra.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each conclusion names the object or map supplied by the corresponding
datum.  The missing constructions are not defined by the remaining
ones.  For example the two signs of a square root have the same
square, while the abelian and Heisenberg algebras on the same
three-dimensional graded space have the same scalar skew blocks.
Thus squaring forgets a root lift and graded block dimensions
forget a bracket.  These examples establish the stated limitations
of those particular data; they do not assert independence of every
geometric hypothesis on a compact family.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Limits of an independence argument]
\label{prop:ch6-obstruction-independence}
The preceding requirements have different mathematical roles, but
some depend on the existence of others.  In particular a root
transport includes root objects, and a geometric Pfaffian datum
includes its line and square map.  Treating the requirement spaces
as independent Cartesian factors does not prove that their
vanishing conditions are geometrically independent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A transport \(o_U\to g^*o_V\) is not an object of a category
independent of \(o_U,o_V\); its source and target are those root
objects.  Likewise the square equation for a Pfaffian section
already involves its line.  A product of formal predicates in
which these objects can be varied independently therefore need not
parametrize any geometric data.  A genuine nonimplication would
need a counterexample satisfying the other geometric hypotheses
in the same category.  The concrete examples above prove only the
specified scalar and algebraic failures.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The scalar branch convention}
\label{subsec:ch6-op-scalar-branch}

Set \(D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5\) and
\(\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2\), with the
complete chamber factors of Chapter~\ref{ch:view1-automorphic}.
The OP scalar branch is
\[
 Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}=-(\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
 =-4096\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The sign belongs to the signed chamber convention for the reduced
count \cite{OB}.  The number \(4096=64^2\) is the squared theta
normalization.  Both statements concern the scalar invariant.
They do not calculate the action on a geometric orientation line.
An identification with a protected trace requires the state and
sewing comparison of the theory in question.

\section{The conditional Pfaffian section identity}
\label{sec:ch6-pfaffian-dirac}

\begin{theorem}[Pfaffian--Dirac comparison]
\label{thm:ch6-pfaffian-dirac}
Suppose the compact system supplies \((D0),(O1),(O1)^+,(O2)\) and
\((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\) on the specified cofinal geometric system.
Assume its oriented block factorizations cover all active target
degrees and preserve both parities, with scalar normalization
\(64x^\rho\).  Suppose also that its compatible Pfaffian lines and
sections are effective on a base \(B\), with parameter map
\(\kappa:B\to\mathcal A_2\) and line comparison
\[
 \iota_{\mathrm{aut}}:\mathscr L_{\mathrm{Pf},X}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}\kappa^*(\mathcal L^5\otimes\nu_{\Delta_5}).
\]
Assume the formal block product converges on a nonempty cusp domain
and the compared sections extend holomorphically to a connected
reduced analytic base, with that domain meeting every irreducible component.
Then
\[
 \iota_{\mathrm{aut}}(\operatorname{pf}_X)=\kappa^*\Delta_5.
\]
The formal equality already holds in the specified height completion;
the analytic conclusion uses the additional convergence and extension
hypotheses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At each finite height the oriented skew-block comparison gives
\[
 64q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
 \prod_{\gamma\in A_R}(1-x^\gamma)^{\operatorname{sdim}V_\gamma}
\]
by Proposition~\ref{prop:pfaffian-product-finite-stage}.  In a fixed
height jet only finitely many active factors contribute.  Therefore
the products have a unique limit in the positive-height completion.
No assertion about general geometric inverse limits is used in this
coefficient stabilization.

The classical Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin product identifies this
formal series, with its boundary factors and normalization, with the
cusp expansion of \(\Delta_5\).  The actual line comparison puts
both sides in the same line over \(B\).  Effectivity supplies its
geometric section; convergence makes the two expansions equal on
the stated open domain.  Their difference is a holomorphic section
vanishing there, so the identity theorem on each reduced irreducible
analytic component gives the result.  Alternatively, when both
sections descend to the Siegel modular stack, the usual expansion
principle gives the same equality \cite{Igusa1962,Igusa1964II}.
A real-root character alone
would not supply that modular descent.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The role of the geometric hypotheses]
\label{rem:ch6-why-four}
The proof uses the actual block comparison to obtain the product,
positive height to take its formal limit, and the parameter and
line comparison to interpret its analytic continuation.  The
orientation and wall conditions control the geometric choices and
reflection action entering those comparisons.  Some of these
conditions may already be included in a stronger formulation of
\((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\); no assertion of logical minimality is
needed.  The theorem is conditional because those compact geometric
constructions are not consequences of the target product.
\end{remark}

If a protected state theory independently satisfies
\[
 Z_X=-4096\,
 \iota_{\mathrm{aut}}(\operatorname{pf}_X)^{-2},
\]
then the theorem yields
\(Z_X=-4096\,\kappa^*\Delta_5^{-2}\).
Under the rescaling \(Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}=-4096^{-1}Z_X\), this is
\(Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}=\kappa^*\Delta_5^{-2}\).
These are consequences of the additional sewing identity, rather
than constructions of a compact microscopic state space.

\section{The orientation character}
\label{sec:ch6-orientation-character}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional orientation character identity]
\label{thm:ch6-orientation-character}
Suppose \((O1)^+\) gives coherent orientation transport and the
reference identifications needed to extract \(\epsilon_o\).
Suppose \((O2)\) gives a compatible rank-one Pfaffian crossing on
each of the three simple type-II walls.  Then
\[
 \epsilon_o=\det_W
 =\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}|_W.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the reference frames, pullback sends the section
\(\upsilon_i u_i\) to \(-\upsilon_i u_i\), since the unit is
invariant.  Compatibility of the actual root lift with that section
therefore forces its residual sign to be \(-1\).  This gives all
three generator values.
Coherent transport and the reference identifications make these
values a character.  They equal the determinant character on the
generators, and the homogeneous automorphic character has the same
values by Lemma~\ref{lem:bkm-maass-restriction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The OP sign and the reflection sign]
\label{rmk:ch6-op-not-orientation-source}
The normal factor has Pfaffian \(u\) and determinant \(u^2\).
Squaring erases its reflection sign.  The OP branch sign cannot
recover it.  Likewise a real path joining \(u>0\) to \(u<0\)
would cross the deleted wall; the comparison here is the specified
reflection lift, not parallel transport along such a path.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Imaginary root divisors]
\label{rmk:ch6-imaginary-roots}
Imaginary simple roots have no associated simple Weyl reflections.
Any geometric monodromy around a divisor bearing such a label needs
the punctured analytic family and locally constant coefficient
system of Definition~\ref{def:local-divisor-monodromy-datum}.
The oriented coefficient is twisted by its orientation sign torsor,
not by tensoring a constructible sheaf with a coherent line.
Neither the target multiplicity nor the Maass multiplier establishes
this local constancy or defines the meridian action.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Chamber symmetry]
\label{rmk:ch6-S3-extension}
The homogeneous automorphic character is trivial on the type-I
chamber group \(S_3\).  The abstract extension of \(\det_W\) to
\(W\rtimes S_3\) compatible with it is therefore
\((w,\sigma)\mapsto\det_W(w)\).  The other abstract extension is
its product with \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\).  Realizing either
on the compact geometry still requires chamber transports and the
semidirect relations of
Proposition~\ref{prop:semidirect-hall-lift-construction-criterion}.
\end{remark}

\section{Primitive recognition}
\label{sec:ch6-primitive-recognition}

Let \(P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{desc}}\) be an actually constructed descended
primitive Lie superalgebra.  When a coefficient realization gives
\[
 P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{desc}}
 =H^0\Prim_{\mathrm{prot}}
   (\overline\Pi_{X,*}^{\Theta}\mathcal F_X),
\]
the equality includes that realization, its differential, the
primitive operation and charge descent.  Let \(B_X\) be a specified
even invariant Lie form, and put
\[
 R_X=\operatorname{Rad}B_X,\qquad
 P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{red}}=P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{desc}}/R_X.
\]
Invariance makes \(R_X\) a Lie ideal.  Its generated ideal in the
ordinary enveloping algebra is a Hopf ideal.  The Lie form itself
is not required to be adjoint to the primitive Hopf coproduct.

\begin{theorem}[Recognition from the compact representatives]
\label{thm:ch6-primitive-recognition}
Suppose \(P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{red}}\) satisfies the ten hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-recognition}: a constructed source and
descent; the Cartan and root grading; representatives of every real
and imaginary simple generator with both parities; the
Borcherds--Kac relations; the invariant-form comparison; the exact
presentation kernel; generation; the filtered enveloping comparison;
a specified continuous completion when used; and the full parity
dimensions with zero spaces in non-root degrees.
Then there is an isomorphism of graded Lie superalgebras
\[
 \mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\xrightarrow{\sim}
 P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{red}}
\]
preserving the invariant forms.  It induces a filtered Hopf
isomorphism of their ordinary enveloping algebras.  If the maps and
inverses extend continuously to the specified completions, the
completed objects are also identified.  The superdenominator is
\(64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathfrak F\) be the free Lie superalgebra on the chosen
Cartan basis and simple symbols, and \(J_{\mathrm{BK}}\) the ideal
of the presentation in Definition~\ref{def:bkm-relations}.
The geometric representatives define
\(\pi:\mathfrak F\to P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{red}}\).
The relations give \(J_{\mathrm{BK}}\subset\ker\pi\), the
no-extra-relations hypothesis gives equality, and generation gives
surjectivity.  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak F/J_{\mathrm{BK}}
 =\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\simeq P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{red}}.
\]
The form comparison and functoriality of the ordinary enveloping
algebra give the remaining algebraic assertions.  The target
invariant form is already nondegenerate; no further target radical
is added to the presentation ideal.

Compatible finite partial-operation comparisons determine this map
only when they cover all the relevant inputs, outputs and relations.
For genuine inverse systems of isomorphisms, the inverses are also
compatible and give the inverse limit isomorphism.  Passing a radical
quotient through a limit requires exactness of its kernel tower;
finite-dimensional towers give that exactness under the hypotheses
of Proposition~\ref{prop:strict-ml-completion-primitive-recognition}.
None of these linear statements equips a two-sided root product
with a continuous bracket.  The completion hypothesis specifies
that bracket and its domain.  Finally the denominator follows from
the known target presentation and its full graded dimensions.
\end{proof}

The associative Ran comparison is another assertion.  In the
coefficient category of Section~\ref{subsec:koszul-source}, let
\[
 V_E=\mathsf{Den}_{\Delta_5,E},\qquad
 \mathcal A_{\Delta,E}=U_\star(\iota_*V_E).
\]
The first object is the BD current envelope; the second is its
associative Ran envelope.  A source datum supplies an augmented
algebra \(A_{X,R}\), a coalgebra map
\(b_{X,R}:C_{X,R}\to BA_{X,R}\), and an associative comparison
\(\vartheta_R:A_{X,R}\to T_R\), with their acyclic cones and
operation compatibilities.  Its map is
\[
 \Theta_{\mathrm{Kos},R}
 =\vartheta_R\circ\varepsilon_{A_{X,R}}\circ\Omega b_{X,R}:
 \Omega C_{X,R}\longrightarrow T_R.
\]
Here \(B,\Omega\) use the specified Ran tensor \(\star\) and empty
unit.  If these maps are equivalences on the admitted bar--cobar
domain, preserve the transitions, and pass to the specified limit
with target \(\mathcal A_{\Delta,E}\), they give
\[
 \Omega C_X\simeq\mathcal A_{\Delta,E}.
\]
A direct statement about the BD chiral algebra \(V_E\) requires
the corresponding chiral comparison functor.  Primitive recognition
alone supplies neither comparison.  A word-cut coalgebra also needs
a map preserving the full collision operations before it can be
identified with this Ran coalgebra.

\begin{remark}[Identical dimensions with a different bracket]
\label{rmk:ch6-zero-bracket-counterexample}
Give the target graded super-vector space the zero bracket.
Its two parity dimensions in every degree, and hence its scalar
superproduct, remain unchanged.  But
\([e_i,f_i]=h_i\) fails for each nonzero real Cartan vector.
The assignments of generators do not factor through the BKM
presentation.  A zero form would moreover have the entire space
as radical.  This example proves that parity dimensions and a
scalar denominator do not recognize a Lie algebra.  Adding an
arbitrary nondegenerate form still does not supply the required
invariant form and bracket comparison.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Algebraic recognition and geometric orientation]
\label{rmk:ch6-recognition-without-orientation}
The algebraic recognition theorem needs the stated source algebra,
representatives and relations.  It can be applied to an algebra
constructed by any method satisfying those hypotheses.  In the
compact geometric construction considered here, orientation and
coefficient descent are among the requirements for producing that
source and its operations.  A Frobenius identity does not by itself
construct the coproduct, prove a radical coideal condition, or
identify the compact primitive algebra.
\end{remark}

\section{Compatibility with chiral Koszul duality and Calabi--Yau quantum groups}
\label{sec:ch6-chiral-compatibility}

The three theorems sit inside chiral Koszul duality and
Calabi--Yau quantum groups as the View~\textcircled{2} /
View~\textcircled{3} face of the Calabi--Yau-to-chiral comparison.  Three
compatibilities are required.

\subsection{Primitive-to-shadow comparison}
\label{subsec:ch6-primitive-to-shadow}

The primitive-to-shadow comparison is the cross-level identity
\textcircled{\scriptsize 2}\(\leftrightarrow\)\textcircled{\scriptsize 3}
applied to the primitive-to-shadow part of the chain.  The Vol II primitive
datum on \((X,D,\tau)\) is the bicoloured nine-tuple
\[
\bigl(\mathcal C\big|_{(X,D,\tau)},\,
\mathcal D^{\mathrm{cl}}\big|_X;\,
b,\,A_b,\,F^{\mathrm{cl}};\,
\sigma_{\mathrm{half}},\,\operatorname{tr}^{\mathrm{cl}}_X;\,
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal C}\bigr).
\]
The chart of vacuum \(b\) is the \(\alpha\)-datum; the chart
algebra \(A_b=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal C}(b)\) is the open primitive
algebra; the closed-colour vacuum factorization algebra
\(F^{\mathrm{cl}}\) gives the closed-side trace
\(\operatorname{tr}^{\mathrm{cl}}_X\) which evaluates at the type-II
cusp \(\mathfrak c_\infty\) to the weight-\(5\) Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin
section \(\mathcal D_X=\Delta_5\) (View \textcircled{\scriptsize 1}).  The
comparison
\(A_b=\Phi^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)}_d(\mathcal C_X)\) is the chain-fusion datum of
chiral Koszul duality and Calabi--Yau quantum groups at level
\(2\): under that datum the Calabi--Yau-side chiral algebra
\(A_X\) is compared with the open-side chart algebra \(A_b\) on the same
curve \(C\) with boundary vacuum induced by
\((\mathcal C_X,\Sigma_{d-1},C)\).  In the present setting, the
comparison concerns the chiral algebra on \(E\) attached to the Hall
source of \(K3\times E\); conditional on the chiral Koszul source
datum, the finite Koszul comparison datum, and strict Koszul
Mittag--Leffler exactness, the chiral cobar
\(\Omega_E^{\mathrm{ch}}C_X\simeq\mathsf{Den}_{\Delta_5,E}\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-primitive-recognition} is the source-side chiral
expression at level \(2\), and its constant-current Lie superalgebra is
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) (View \textcircled{\scriptsize 2}).  The
protected Pfaffian \(\operatorname{Pf}_{\mathrm{prot}}(\mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}})\)
is the pre-trace Pfaffian section.  After squaring and applying the
retained level-\(\mathsf Z\) datum, Vol~I Theorem~H
(chiral-Hochschild concentration on the Koszul locus) and Vol~II's
cyclic-Hochschild Beilinson stratification \textnormal{(i)+(ii)}
identify the derived-centre trace pairing of degree
\(-\dim_\C(K3\times E)\) whose scalar is \(\Delta_5^{-2}\)
(Appendix~\ref{app:obstruction-discharges},
Theorem~\ref{thm:G-vol2-discharge}).  The level-\(\mathsf A\)
gravity-line operator algebra acting on the boundary is the open
Hall--Borcherds residual of Vol~II and is not constructed by the
level-\(\mathsf Z\) trace criterion.

\subsection{Vol III chain fusion at level \(2\)}
\label{subsec:ch6-vol3-chain-fusion}

The Vol III \(\kappa\)-stratification supplies the
\(\kappa\)-tuple
\((\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}},\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}})=(0,0,3,5,24)\) at \(K3\times E\); the row
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=5\) is the cusp-form weight realized by
\(\Delta_5\), and the row \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}=24\) is
\(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3)=24\).  The additive expression
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}+\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{fiber}})\)
has no invariant meaning: the Hodge reading gives \(0+2=2\) at \(N=1\),
and the Heisenberg reading gives the accidental equality \(3+2=5\) only
on that row.  The CHL frame has Borcherds weights
\((5,3,2,\tfrac32,1)\), fixed by \(c_N(0)/2\) with
\(c_N(0)=(10,6,4,3,2)\) per Jatkar--Sen and Govindarajan--Krishna
\cite{JS06Dyon,GK09CHL,GK10Composite},
not by a fixed additive
formula.  Thus \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\) is the Euler characteristic
of the topological fibre and not of \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{fiber}}\); for
K3, \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3)=24\) but
\(\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})=2\).  Under
the Vol III chain-fusion datum at level \(2\), the chiral algebra
\(A_X\) on the CY face is compared with the chart algebra
\(A_b\) on the Vol II open face.  In the \(K3\times E\) specialization
this comparison becomes
\(A_X=A_b=\Omega_E^{\mathrm{ch}}C_X\simeq\mathsf{Den}_{\Delta_5,E}\) only
under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-primitive-recognition}.

\begin{remark}[Coefficient projection is not a Hall comparison]
\label{rmk:ch6-coefficient-projection}
A Hall--Borcherds comparison map cannot be defined by coefficient
projection alone, and a coefficient projection respecting charge
addition because both products use the same cone is not a recognition
theorem.  A coefficient projection is not a Hall product/bracket matrix
comparison, and does not prove Borcherds--Serre rows, radical descent,
no-extra-relations, or PBW.  The recognition criterion (S4)--(S10) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-primitive-recognition} is the mathematical
statement:
the source-side bracket matrices, Hopf radical, kernel equality, and
PBW associated-graded comparison are independent data, none of which is
inferable from a coefficient projection.
\end{remark}

\subsection{BCOV / mixed holomorphic-topological strings}
\label{subsec:ch6-bcov}

The mixed holomorphic-topological strings companion provides the local
model \(\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\times\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}\) with brane
completion as one realization of the factorization-algebra datum
\(\Phi_d^{\mathrm{FA}}\); the global obstruction is the holomorphic
de Rham obstruction class.  The level-\(n\) scalar shadow
\(Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}^{K3\times E}=\Delta_5^{-2}\) on the closed
\(K3\times E\mapsto\mathrm{point}\) cobordism is the BPS partition
function (Chapter~\ref{ch:zbps-realization}).  A three-dimensional
gravity-line interpretation with internal compact factor \(K3\times E\)
requires the Hall--Borcherds residual of Vol II and is not claimed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-pfaffian-dirac}.  The identity
\(\operatorname{Pf}_{\mathrm{prot}}(\mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}})=\mathcal D_X=\Delta_5\)
is a protected Pfaffian identity before trace; its inverse square is
the level-\(n\) protected scalar trace.  The Pfaffian and orientation
parts of the operator-level datum \(\mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}}\) are
defined on \(K3\times E\)
relative to the retained data of
Appendix~\ref{app:obstruction-discharges}; the primitive Lie-superalgebra
part is the compact-source recognition problem.

\section{Finite progress and the remaining compact constructions}
\label{sec:ch6-residuals}

The target degree calculations give concrete comparison problems.
At height three, the target has real simple dimensions \(1|0\),
isotropic dimensions \(10|0\), and interior dimensions \(29|93\).
The isotropic space consists of the real bracket and nine even
imaginary simple directions.  The interior space admits a
rank-28 differential in the positive height-three quotient, leaving
cohomology \(1|65\).  That differential extends through height
five and fails at height six for the unchanged Borcherds relations,
by Theorem~\ref{thm:bkm-serre-nonextension}.  Thus a proposed
geometric differential must be compared with the relations on its
actual domain, rather than inferred from the signed dimension
\(-64\).

The first enlarged degree set has 35 degrees, including zero
codomains of relations.  With
\(a_{ij}=\delta_i+\delta_j\),
\(C_{k,s}=a_{ij}+s\delta_k\), and
\(D_k=\delta_k+2a_{ij}\), it is
\[
 \begin{split}
 W_{\mathrm{rel}}^+={}&W_3
 \cup\{3\delta_i+\delta_j:i\ne j\}
 \cup\{2a_{ij}:i<j\}\\
 &\cup\{C_{k,s}:2\leq s\leq5\}
 \cup\{2\delta_{123}\}.
 \end{split}
\]
The three missing nonzero proper subdegrees are \(D_k\), giving
38 degrees after adjoining them.  Their full target dimensions are
known:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 2a_{ij}:10|0,\quad C_{k,2}:201|93,\quad
 C_{k,3}:29|93,\quad C_{k,4}:10|0,\quad C_{k,5}:0|0,\\
 D_k:510|1023,\qquad 2\delta_{123}:10136|6120.
 \end{gathered}
\]
These refine the signed coefficients
\(10,108,-64,10,0,-513,4016\).  Their derivation from the full
presentation is given in Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}; the
compact-source representatives and operation comparisons remain
separate.

For the degree-enlargement rule
\[
 T_i(\beta)=\beta+(1-(\delta_i,\beta))\delta_i
 \quad\text{when }(\delta_i,\beta)<0,
\]
the first enlargement of the specified ten degrees in that
38-degree set adds 21 new output degrees.  Eighteen have nonzero
signed coefficient.  Their nonzero proper subdegrees contribute
126 incidences across 30 new degrees, giving \(38+21+30=89\).
These are the finite counts proved in
Section~\ref{subsec:primitive-recognition}, using elliptic
reduction of every Jacobi coefficient.  Assigning zero to a
coefficient beyond a truncated Fourier expansion would change
those counts.

This rule is the real Serre output degree on an imaginary simple
generator.  On a composite root it merely specifies a degree;
it need not annihilate the whole root space.  Under this rule and
nonzero proper-subdegree closure, the affine families
\(A_N\Rightarrow B_N\Rightarrow A_{N+2}\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:recognition-affine-staircase} force an
unbounded set.  Finite partial-operation diagrams remain useful:
they specify exactly which inputs, codomains and relations they
contain.  The infinite enlargement argument is not an obstruction
to all finite comparison calculations, and a finite collection of
target dimensions is not a compact recognition theorem.

The orientation calculations likewise produce definite local
results.  Formal coordinate changes admit the all-arity functors
and full Artin determinant roots of
Section~\ref{subsec:cosection-twisted-orientation}.  Complete flags
retain their cross determinants and common trace excess.  Finite
stabilizer root problems reduce to actual character square roots,
while general gerbes can have additional mixed cohomology classes.
The first missing compact orientation construction is a single
reduced critical coefficient system whose root comparisons agree
on all these flags, quotient overlaps and transitions.

For the wall count, the only unconditional combinatorial statement
is \(|\{\pm1\}^{12}|=4096\).  To identify this set with the
geometric configurations of a half-Hilbert wall atlas requires a
bijection, coverage and the correct stabilizer weights.  If those
hypotheses are supplied, the count agrees numerically with
\(64^2\).  That equality of integers does not identify the wall
integration map with the OP scalar normalization.

The original compact comparison consequently still requires the
reduced coefficient realization and Hall operations, the geometric
Pfaffian family, the state and sewing map, and the modular parameter
and line comparison.  The ordinary connected cocommutative Hopf
recognition theorem applies only when that recipient and its
hypotheses are constructed.  Localized, spectral or braided Hall
operations use other tensors and coefficient domains; their
positive results do not automatically furnish this ordinary
compact structure.

The three-dimensional gravitational interpretation and the
Hall--Borcherds operator comparison need their actual state spaces
and sewing operations.  Numerical levels do not construct them.
For example the cusp-form weight is five, while the K3 elliptic
genus has weight zero, index one and value 24 at the trivial
elliptic variable \cite{Gritsenko99}.  A notation that groups categorical, Hodge,
Heisenberg, BKM and fibre values into a tuple such as
\((0,0,3,5,24)\) must define the five entries separately.  The
numerical equality \(5=3+2\) supplies no comparison among their
categories or traces.  The finite constructions above provide
specific local maps and falsifiers; the global compact section
and state statements remain conditional on the stated geometric
constructions.
