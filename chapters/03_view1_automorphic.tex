\chapter{The automorphic denominator}
\label{ch:view1-automorphic}

\section{The section to be realized}
\label{sec:view1-pentadic-level}

A geometric realization of the Igusa denominator must recover a section
of a specified line, with its character and boundary behavior.  These
data can be determined before constructing the geometric source.  The
Jacobi coefficients give a convergent product.  Its theta normalization
then fixes a weight-five section, and its square gives the monic
weight-ten form in the primitive curve-counting theorem.

The three constructions involved have different inputs.  The
automorphic product starts from a weak Jacobi form.  The
Borcherds--Kac--Moody denominator starts from a Lie superalgebra with
specified generators and relations.  A compact Pfaffian would start
from a family of complexes with a pairing, reduction and orientation.
This chapter identifies the first section and its relation to the
second denominator.  Its boundary calculations also give necessary
conditions on the third construction.

\section{Siegel space and the coefficient cone}
\label{sec:view1-siegel-and-chamber}

For the classical theory of Siegel modular forms, see
\cite[Chapter I]{FREITAG:1} and \cite[\S\S2--3]{VDGEER:1}.
Write
\[
 \mathbb H_2=\{Z=Z^t\in M_2(\C):\Imag Z>0\},\qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}z_1&z_2\\z_2&z_3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The action of
\(g=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
\in\Sp_4(\Z)\) is
\(gZ=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}\).  Put \(j(g,Z)=\det(CZ+D)\).
A modular form of integral weight \(k\) and character \(\nu\) satisfies
\[
 F(gZ)=\nu(g)j(g,Z)^kF(Z).
\]
Thus it is a section of \(\mathcal L^k\otimes\nu\) on the quotient
stack \([\Sp_4(\Z)\backslash\mathbb H_2]\), where \(\mathcal L\)
is the determinant Hodge line and \(\nu\) denotes the character line.
All line-valued identities below use this quotient.

At the standard rational cusp \(\mathfrak c_\infty\), set
\[
 q=e^{2\pi iz_1},\qquad r=e^{2\pi iz_2},\qquad
 s=e^{2\pi iz_3}.
\]
The three elementary root monomials are
\[
 x_1=qr,\qquad x_2=sr,\qquad x_3=r^{-1}.
\]
They correspond to the charges
\((1,1,0)\), \((0,1,1)\), \((0,-1,0)\), in the order
\((n,\ell,m)\).

Their nonnegative span is the monoid
\[
 C_+=\{(n,\ell,m)\in\Z^3:n\geq0,\ m\geq0,\quad
                                  n+m-\ell\geq0\}.
\]
Indeed
\[
 q^nr^\ell s^m=x_1^n x_2^m x_3^{n+m-\ell}.
\]
The total root height and its completion are therefore
\[
 h(n,\ell,m)=2(n+m)-\ell,\qquad
 \widehat{\Z[C_+]}=\Z[[x_1,x_2,x_3]].
\]
At height \(h\), this monoid has \(\binom{h+2}{2}\) elements.
Consequently multiplication and products of factors of increasing
positive height are defined coefficientwise.

The usual ordering set for the Borcherds product is
\[
\begin{split}
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}={}&
 \{(n,\ell,m):m>0,\ n\geq0,\ \ell\in\Z\}\\
 &{}\cup\{(n,\ell,0):n>0,\ \ell\in\Z\}
 \cup\{(0,\ell,0):\ell<0\}.
\end{split}
\]
This larger semigroup records the conventional order of the indices.
The coefficient cone \(C_+\) supplies the completion.  In particular,
the entire ordering semigroup does not have finite decomposition
sets: \((2,0,0)=(1,a,0)+(1,-a,0)\) for every integer \(a\).
The Jacobi support proved below places every nontrivial product factor
in \(C_+\setminus\{0\}\).

In analytic coordinates \(y_j=\Imag z_j\), the corresponding chamber is
\[
 -y_2>0,\qquad y_1+y_2>0,\qquad y_3+y_2>0.
\]
These inequalities imply \(\Imag Z>0\) and \(|x_i|<1\).
A useful ray is \((y_1,y_2,y_3)=(T,-T/2,T)\), on which
\(|x_i|=e^{-\pi T}\).  The product theorem will give absolute
convergence sufficiently far along this chamber.  The more restrictive
ordering \(0<|s|\ll|q|\ll|r|^{-1}\ll1\) is a possible cusp
subregion.  Formal identities use total height and require no limit
of complex variables.

\section{The Jacobi input}
\label{sec:view1-jacobi-input}

For the Jacobi variables \((\tau,z)\in\mathbb H_1\times\C\), use
\(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\) and \(r=e^{2\pi iz}\).
The weight-zero, index-one weak Jacobi form is
\[
 \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)
 =4\sum_{a=2}^4\frac{\theta_a(\tau,z)^2}{\theta_a(\tau,0)^2}
 =\sum_{n\geq0,\ \ell\in\Z}f(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell.
\]
Here
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \theta_2(\tau,z)&=\sum_{a\in\Z}q^{(a+1/2)^2/2}r^{a+1/2},\\
 \theta_3(\tau,z)&=\sum_{a\in\Z}q^{a^2/2}r^a,\qquad
 \theta_4(\tau,z)=\sum_{a\in\Z}(-1)^a q^{a^2/2}r^a.
\end{aligned}
\]
The denominators are nonzero on \(\mathbb H_1\).

The theta transformation formulas give the modular law
\[
 \phi_{0,1}\!\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac z{c\tau+d}\right)
 =e^{2\pi icz^2/(c\tau+d)}\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)
\]
for \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
\in\SL_2(\Z)\), and the elliptic law
\[
 \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z+a\tau+b)
 =q^{-a^2}r^{-2a}\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z),
 \qquad a,b\in\Z.
\]
Comparing coefficients gives
\[
 f(n,\ell)=f(n+a\ell+a^2,\ell+2a).
\]

Together with \(z\mapsto-z\), this implies that \(f(n,\ell)\)
depends only on \(4n-\ell^2\).  Reduction of \(\ell\) to \(0\) or
\(1\), followed by weak holomorphy, gives
\[
 f(n,\ell)=0\quad\text{if }4n-\ell^2<-1.
\]
The normalization is
\(\phi_{0,1}=r^{-1}+10+r+O(q)\).
It generates the one-dimensional space \(J_{0,1}^{\mathrm{weak}}\)
\cite{EZ:1}.  For a K3 surface \(S\), the elliptic genus has constant
term \(2r^{-1}+20+2r\), so
\(\operatorname{Ell}(S)=2\phi_{0,1}\).
This is the normalization used in the Hilbert-scheme formulas of
Chapter~\ref{ch:pentadic} \cite{DMVV}.

Expansion of the three theta quotients gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:view1-phi01-coefficients}
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
 &\ell=-3&-2&-1&0&1&2&3\\ \hline
 n=0&0&0&1&10&1&0&0\\
 n=1&0&10&-64&108&-64&10&0\\
 n=2&1&108&-513&808&-513&108&1
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:borcherds-product-expansion} gives the coefficient
calculation.  The constant coefficient \(10\) determines the weight.
The coefficient at discriminant \(-1\) determines the simple
reflective divisor \cite{Gritsenko2018Reflective}.
The negative coefficients will be signed root
multiplicities.

Define the active set by
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}
 =\{(n,\ell,m)\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}:f(nm,\ell)\ne0\}.
\]
For \(n+m>0\), its elements satisfy
\(\ell^2\leq4nm+1\).  If \(\ell>n+m\), integrality would give
\(\ell^2\geq(n+m+1)^2>4nm+1\), a contradiction.
For \(n=m=0\), the only active index is \((0,-1,0)\).
It follows that
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}\subset C_+\setminus\{0\}.
\]
This proves the local finiteness needed for the formal product and
for each of its inverses.

\section{The theta normalization}
\label{sec:view1-theta-normalization}

The theta phase matters for a square root.  Choose representatives
\(a,b\in\{0,1\}^2\), and define the genus-two theta constants by
\[
 \nu_{a,b}(Z)=
 \sum_{u\in\Z^2}
 \exp\!\left(\pi i\bigl((u+a/2)^tZ(u+a/2)+b^tu\bigr)\right).
\]
Use the ten characteristics with \(a^tb\) even, and put
\[
 \Delta_5(Z)=\prod_{a^tb\ \mathrm{even}}\nu_{a,b}(Z).
\]
This is the convention of Gritsenko--Nikulin
\cite[(1.2)]{GN}.

In the convention with phase
\(b^t(u+a/2)\), each constant acquires
\(e^{\pi i a^tb/2}\).  The product of those phases is \(-1\),
coming from \(a=b=(1,1)\).  Specifying the series removes this
otherwise hidden sign.

\begin{proposition}[Theta-constant leading coefficient]
\label{prop:view1-theta-constant-leading-coefficient}
In the chosen convention,
\[
 [q^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5
 =64(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2}),\qquad
 [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5=64.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The four constants with \(a=(0,0)\) start with \(1\).
The two with \(a=(1,0)\) start with \(2q^{1/8}\), and the two
with \(a=(0,1)\) start with \(2s^{1/8}\).
For \(a=(1,1)\), the two even characteristics give
\[
 2q^{1/8}s^{1/8}(r^{1/4}+r^{-1/4}),\qquad
 2q^{1/8}s^{1/8}(r^{1/4}-r^{-1/4}).
\]
Their product retains both elliptic monomials.  Multiplication by
the other eight leading terms gives the stated coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Monic Borcherds form]
\label{def:view1-monic-Borcherds-Gritsenko-form}
Set \(D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5\).
Its coefficient at \(q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}\) is \(1\).
The half powers mean the exponentials
\(e^{\pi iz_1}\), \(e^{\pi iz_2}\), \(e^{\pi iz_3}\) on
\(\mathbb H_2\).
\end{definition}

The factor \(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2}\) will give the separating divisor.
Calling the form monic fixes one coefficient.  It does not discard
the other term in that coefficient of \(q^{1/2}s^{1/2}\).

\section{The Borcherds product}
\label{sec:view1-borcherds-gritsenko-nikulin-section}
\label{ssec:view1-product-expansion}

The Jacobi form has integral coefficients, weight zero and index one.
The Borcherds product theorem in this case gives a global modular form
from precisely these data \cite[Theorem 4.1]{GN},
\cite[Theorem 2.1 and Example 2.4]{GNII}.

\begin{theorem}[Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin product]
\label{thm:view1-borcherds-gritsenko-nikulin-lift}
The monic form has the expansion
\[
 D_5(Z)=(qrs)^{1/2}
 \prod_{\gamma=(n,\ell,m)\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}}
             (1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
After division by \((qrs)^{1/2}\), this is an element of
\(1+(x_1,x_2,x_3)\Z[[x_1,x_2,x_3]]\).
Analytically, the product converges absolutely sufficiently near
\(\mathfrak c_\infty\) in the specified chamber.
It continues to a holomorphic Siegel cusp form of weight five,
with a character of order two on \(\Sp_4(\Z)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The active-cone calculation proves the formal assertion.
The analytic product theorem supplies the continuation, its weight
\(f(0,0)/2=5\), and its rational quadratic divisors.
Gritsenko--Nikulin identify this product with their theta product
divided by \(64\) \cite[Theorem 4.1]{GN}.
Their later convention writes the monic form itself as
\(\Delta_5\) \cite[(2.16)]{GNII}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:view1-theta-constant-leading-coefficient}
fixes the conversion to the convention used here.
\end{proof}

For example, convergence holds for sufficiently large \(T\) on the
ray \((T,-T/2,T)\), and on a neighborhood of this ray far enough
toward the cusp.  On smaller compact subsets the convergence is
normal.  The general convergence statement and its continuation
follow from the same product theorem.  A formal height completion
alone would not supply analytic convergence.

\begin{corollary}[Theta-normalized section]
\label{cor:view1-theta-normalized-section}
With zero exponents omitted or retained, respectively,
\[
 \Delta_5
 =64(qrs)^{1/2}
       \prod_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}}(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}
 =64(qrs)^{1/2}
       \prod_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
\end{corollary}

The composite index \(nm\) is the same one that occurs in the
Hilbert-scheme elliptic-genus product.  There the K3 coefficients
are \(2f(nm,\ell)\), and the positive-\(m\) product is an inverse.
The boundary factors and the factor of two distinguish that index
from the present square root.

\subsection{The automorphic line}
\label{ssec:view1-package}

\begin{definition}[The Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin section]
\label{def:view1-borcherds-gritsenko-nikulin-section}
For \(X=S\times E\), write
\(\mathcal D_X=\Delta_5\) for the section of
\(\mathcal L^5\otimes\nu_5\) specified above.
Here \(\nu_5\) is the Maass character, also denoted
\(\nu_{\Delta_5}\), whose values are given below.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The meaning of \(\mathcal D_X\)]
\label{rem:view1-D-X-naming}
The subscript names the geometric realization problem.
The section itself is defined on Siegel space.
A geometric interpretation requires a parameter space \(B\), a map
\(\sigma:B\to[\Sp_4(\Z)\backslash\mathbb H_2]\), and an isomorphism
from an independently constructed line on \(B\) to
\(\sigma^*(\mathcal L^5\otimes\nu_5)\).
The introduction of the three Fourier variables does not define
this map or line isomorphism.
\end{remark}

\section{Weight, character and divisor}
\label{sec:view1-weight-multiplier-divisor}

\subsection{The weight}
\label{ssec:view1-borcherds-weight}

\begin{proposition}[The Borcherds weight]
\label{prop:view1-borcherds-gritsenko-weight-identity}
For the fixed Jacobi input, put \(c_1(0)=f(0,0)=10\).
Then
\[
 \operatorname{wt}(\mathcal D_X)
 =\frac{f(0,0)}2=5
 =\frac{c_1(0)}2=\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Delta_5).
\]
Here \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}\) denotes the automorphic weight.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the weight formula in
Theorem~\ref{thm:view1-borcherds-gritsenko-nikulin-lift}.
\end{proof}

Integral weight makes \(j(g,Z)^5\) single-valued on the Siegel
group.  A nontrivial character remains.  The input metaplectic
representation in Chapter~\ref{ch:singular-theta-lift} concerns a
different group.

The same weight formula applies to the other seven diagonal-divisor
forms in Appendix~\ref{app:borcherds-product-expansion}.
For a congruence input its constant term is the weighted contribution
of all cusps.  The parameter pair \((t,N)\), consisting of the Jacobi
index and congruence level, must be retained.

\subsection{The Maass character}
\label{ssec:view1-maass-multiplier}

The character \(\nu_5:\Sp_4(\Z)\to\{\pm1\}\) is determined on
the usual generators by
\[
 \nu_5\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix}=1,\qquad
 \nu_5\begin{pmatrix}I_2&B\\0&I_2\end{pmatrix}
       =(-1)^{b_1+b_2+b_3},
 \quad B=\begin{pmatrix}b_1&b_2\\b_2&b_3\end{pmatrix},
\]
and, for \(U=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\Z)\),
\[
 \nu_5\begin{pmatrix}U^{-t}&0\\0&U\end{pmatrix}
 =(-1)^{(1+a+d)(1+b+c)+ad}.
\]
These are Maass's formulas \cite{MAASS:1}, also reproduced in
\cite[(1.3)--(1.5)]{GN}.
In particular \(\nu_5^2=1\).
The character is nontrivial, since translation by
\(B=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\) has value \(-1\).
Thus \(\Delta_5\) is a section of the character-twisted Hodge line,
whereas \(\Delta_5^2\) is a section of \(\mathcal L^{10}\).

\subsection{The diagonal divisor}
\label{ssec:view1-diagonal-divisor}

Let
\[
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{diag}}
 =\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\tau&0\\0&\sigma\end{pmatrix}:
                         \tau,\sigma\in\mathbb H_1\right\}.
\]
The divisor of \(\Delta_5\) on \(\mathbb H_2\) is
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\Delta_5)
 =\sum_{\mathcal H\in\Sp_4(\Z)\cdot\mathcal H_{\mathrm{diag}}}
       \mathcal H,
\]
where each distinct component occurs once
\cite[Theorem 1.4]{GC}.  It descends to the Humbert divisor of
discriminant one.  The order here is measured upstairs, or on the
quotient stack with its line.  Coarse quotient ramification should
not be inserted into this multiplicity.

In the rank-three lattice
\[
 M_0=\Z f_2\oplus\Z f_3\oplus\Z f_{-2},\qquad
 (f_2,f_{-2})=-1,\quad f_3^2=2,
\]
all other basis pairings vanish.  The three simple roots are
\[
 \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\qquad
 \delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\qquad \delta_3=f_3.
\]
They span the type-II lattice
\(M_{II}=\Z(2f_2)\oplus\Z f_3\oplus\Z(2f_{-2})\).

Their Gram matrix and product degrees are
\[
 ((\delta_i,\delta_j))=
 \begin{pmatrix}2&-2&-2\\-2&2&-2\\-2&-2&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 (1,1,0),\ (0,1,1),\ (0,-1,0).
\]
Each corresponding factor has exponent \(1\).
Its wall is a component of the simple Humbert divisor.  The reciprocal
\(\Delta_5^{-2}\) consequently has order \(-2\) there.
The separating calculation below also proves order one directly at
\(z_2=0\).

\subsection{Linear reflections and the slash action}
\label{ssec:view1-maass-character-walls}

The action on the tube coordinates has a different character from the
weight-five slash action.  To specify both, put
\[
 \zeta=z_3f_2+z_2f_3+z_1f_{-2},\qquad
 s_\beta(v)=v-(v,\beta)\beta\quad(\beta^2=2),
\]
and let \(s_\beta Z\) denote the period corresponding to
\(s_\beta\zeta\).
The three reflections permuting the simple roots have normals
\[
 \alpha_1=f_{-2}-f_2,\qquad
 \alpha_2=f_2-f_3,\qquad
 \alpha_3=f_{-2}-f_3.
\]

The orthogonal realization separates the two signs intrinsically.
Let \(L\) and \(\sigma:\Sp_4(\Z)\to\Gamma_{3,2}\subset\SO(L)\)
be the exterior-square lattice and homomorphism of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exceptional-iso}. Its cusp frame satisfies
\(\sigma(g)w(Z)=j(g,Z)w(gZ)\).

\begin{definition}[Homogeneous orthogonal character]
\label{def:homogeneous-orthogonal-character}
On the nonzero null cone over \(\mathbb H_2\), set
\[
 \widehat\Delta_5(tw(Z))=t^{-5}\Delta_5(Z).
\]
The character of its orthogonal transformation law is
\[
 \widetilde\nu_5:\Gamma_{3,2}\times\{\pm\Id_L\}\longrightarrow\{\pm1\},
 \qquad
 \widetilde\nu_5(\eta\sigma(g))=\eta^{-5}\nu_5(g)
 \quad(\eta\in\{\pm1\}).
\]
We also write \(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}=\widetilde\nu_5\).
\end{definition}

The definition is independent of the lift \(g\), since
\(\ker\sigma=\{\pm\Id_4\}\) and \(\nu_5(-\Id_4)=1\).
The two orthogonal factors intersect trivially:
\(\dim L=5\) gives \(\det(-\Id_L)=-1\).
Homogeneity forces \(\widetilde\nu_5(-\Id_L)=-1\), and the frame
relation gives
\(\widehat\Delta_5(\sigma(g)w)=\nu_5(g)\widehat\Delta_5(w)\).
Extend each rank-three reflection by the identity on the complementary
hyperbolic plane in \(L\).

\begin{lemma}[Reflection signs and symplectic lifts]
\label{lem:view1-maass-multiplier-restriction}
The linear tube action satisfies
\[
 \Delta_5(s_{\delta_i}Z)=-\Delta_5(Z),\qquad
 \Delta_5(s_{\alpha_i}Z)=\Delta_5(Z).
\]
For the block-diagonal symplectic lifts \(g_\beta\) below,
the Maass and slash-action values are instead
\[
 \nu_5(g_{\delta_i})=1,\qquad \nu_5(g_{\alpha_i})=-1,
 \qquad \Delta_5|_5g_\beta=\nu_5(g_\beta)\Delta_5.
\]
The homogeneous orthogonal values are
\[
 \widetilde\nu_5(s_{\delta_i})=-1,\qquad
 \widetilde\nu_5(s_{\alpha_i})=1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take \(g_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(U_\beta^{-t},U_\beta)\),
with
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 \beta&\delta_1&\delta_2&\delta_3\\ \hline
 U_\beta&
 \begin{psmallmatrix}1&-2\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}&
 \begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\2&-1\end{psmallmatrix}&
 \begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}\\[5pt]
 \beta&\alpha_1&\alpha_2&\alpha_3\\ \hline
 U_\beta&
 \begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}&
 \begin{psmallmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}&
 \begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\1&-1\end{psmallmatrix}
\end{array}
\]
Each determinant is \(-1\).
The action \(Z\mapsto U_\beta^{-t}ZU_\beta^{-1}\)
is \(s_\beta\) on the tube coordinates.
For \(U=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\),
its matrix in the basis \((f_2,f_3,f_{-2})\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a^2&-2ab&b^2\\
 -ac&ad+bc&-bd\\
 c^2&-2cd&d^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Substitution verifies all six reflections.

Maass's formula gives \(+1\) in the first three cases and \(-1\)
in the last three.  Since \(j(g_\beta,Z)=\det U_\beta=-1\),
the functional equation gives
\(\Delta_5(s_\beta Z)=-\nu_5(g_\beta)\Delta_5(Z)\).
The cusp frame obeys \(w(s_\beta Z)=s_\beta w(Z)\), so the frame
relation also gives \(\sigma(g_\beta)=-s_\beta\).
Consequently \(\widetilde\nu_5(s_\beta)=-\nu_5(g_\beta)\).
These equations prove all three assertions.
\end{proof}

Write \(\varepsilon_5\) for the character of this linear tube action.
It is the restriction of \(\widetilde\nu_5\) to the reflections
fixing the complementary hyperbolic plane.
For the reflection group \(W^{(2)}(M_{II})\) and the chamber
symmetry group \(S_3\), the lemma gives
\[
 \varepsilon_5(w)=\det(w),\qquad
 \varepsilon_5(a)=1\quad(a\in S_3).
\]
These are the signs used in the alternating denominator identity
\cite[Propositions 2.1--2.2]{GN}.

The divisibility of \(\beta\in M_0\) is the positive generator
of the ideal \((\beta,M_0)\subset\Z\).
The type-I walls carry no divisor of \(\Delta_5\).
Their primitive normals have divisibility
one in \(M_0\), whereas a type-II normal has divisibility two.
The latter admits the half-normal in the dual lattice that supports
the negative Fourier index of the lift.  This divisor criterion is
made explicit in Chapter~\ref{ch:singular-theta-lift}.

\section{The coefficientwise determinant}
\label{sec:view1-K0-determinant-identification}

For each \(\gamma=(n,\ell,m)\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}\), choose
a finite super vector space \(V_\gamma=V_\gamma^{\bar0}\oplus
V_\gamma^{\bar1}\) with
\[
 \operatorname{sdim}V_\gamma
 =\dim V_\gamma^{\bar0}-\dim V_\gamma^{\bar1}=f(nm,\ell).
\]
Such spaces exist by placing \(|f(nm,\ell)|\) basis vectors
in the parity given by its sign.
This realizes the integer without specifying a bracket or geometric
coefficient complex.
We use the split Grothendieck group of finite super vector spaces
with the additional relation \([\Pi V]=-[V]\), where \(\Pi\)
reverses parity.  Its classes are determined by superdimension.

The graded sum \(V=\bigoplus_\gamma V_\gamma\) has
finite-dimensional total-height components.

For \(x^\gamma=q^nr^\ell s^m\), define the completed Berezinian by
\[
 \operatorname{Ber}_{V}(1-x)=
 \prod_\gamma
 \frac{\det(1-x^\gamma\mid V_\gamma^{\bar0})}
      {\det(1-x^\gamma\mid V_\gamma^{\bar1})}.
\]
Total height makes every truncation finite.

\begin{lemma}[The \(K_0\) superdeterminant]
\label{lem:view1-K0-Fock-superdeterminant}
The Berezinian depends only on the super-Grothendieck classes and is
\[
 \sdet_V(1-x):=\operatorname{Ber}_V(1-x)
 =\prod_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}}(1-x^\gamma)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
The Euler supercharacter of the super-symmetric algebra is its inverse.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An even basis vector contributes \(1-x^\gamma\) to the determinant.
An odd vector contributes its reciprocal.  This proves the first
identity on each finite height truncation and hence on their inverse
limit.  In a super-symmetric algebra, an even generator gives a
polynomial algebra with supercharacter \((1-x^\gamma)^{-1}\).
An odd generator gives an exterior algebra with supercharacter
\(1-x^\gamma\).  Multiplication gives the inverse product.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The automorphic continuation of the determinant]
\label{prop:bgn-identification-recap}
Put \(v_0(Z)=64(qrs)^{1/2}\).
Then
\[
 v_0(Z)\,\sdet_V(1-x)=\mathcal D_X(Z)=\Delta_5(Z)
\]
in the height-completed cusp expansion.  The left side has the
analytic continuation given by
Theorem~\ref{thm:view1-borcherds-gritsenko-nikulin-lift}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The lemma identifies every factor of the product theorem.
The theta coefficient fixes \(v_0\).
On the convergence region this is an equality of holomorphic
functions.  Analytic continuation gives the stated section.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the determinant identity]
\label{rem:view1-bgn-scope}
An Euler class can also come from an actual derived supercomplex.
Its Berezinian still remembers only the signed dimensions used above.
A comparison with a Hall algebra requires compatible extension maps.
A comparison with a Pfaffian requires a family, a skew pairing,
an orientation and modular descent.  None of these operations is
defined by the product of Euler classes.
\end{remark}

\section{The complete boundary factors}
\label{sec:view1-boundary-factors}

The zero-\(s\) factors give the first Fourier--Jacobi coefficient.
Define
\[
 b(q,r)=q^{1/2}(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2})
 \prod_{a\geq1}(1-q^a)^{10}(1-rq^a)(1-r^{-1}q^a).
\]
Separating the factors with \(m=0\) gives the exact identity
\[
 D_5=s^{1/2}b(q,r)
 \prod_{\substack{m>0,\ n\geq0\\\ell\in\Z}}
                    (1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
Thus \([s^{1/2}]D_5=b\).

Writing
\[
 \eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{a\geq1}(1-q^a),\qquad
 \vartheta(\tau,z)=q^{1/8}(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2})
  \prod_{a\geq1}(1-q^a)(1-rq^a)(1-r^{-1}q^a),
\]
one has \(b=\eta^9\vartheta\).
This is the weight-five, index-\(\tfrac12\) Jacobi form whose
additive lift gives \(D_5\) \cite[(13)]{GC}.
Its multiplier on the Jacobi parabolic is
\(v_\eta^{12}\times v_H\), where \(v_\eta\) is the Dedekind
eta multiplier and \(v_H\) is the odd-theta Heisenberg character.

For a K3 surface \(S\) and a chosen Enriques surface \(T\), the
normalized elliptic-index series of Chapter~\ref{ch:pentadic} are
therefore
\[
 F_S=\frac{s\,b^2}{D_5^2},\qquad
 F_T=\frac{s^{1/2}b}{D_5},\qquad F_T^2=F_S.
\]
These quotients have their boundary factors cancelled before
specialization at \(r=1\).  Raw reciprocal cusp forms have poles
there.

\begin{proposition}[The separating leading section]
\label{prop:view1-separating-leading}
For \(z=z_2\) near zero and \(z_1,z_3\in\mathbb H_1\),
\[
 D_5(Z)=2\pi iz\,\eta(z_1)^{12}\eta(z_3)^{12}+O(z^3).
\]
Consequently
\[
 D_5(Z)^2=(2\pi iz)^2\eta(z_1)^{24}\eta(z_3)^{24}+O(z^4).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(r=1\), the theta quotient gives \(\phi_{0,1}(\tau,0)=12\).
Hence
\[
 \sum_\ell f(n,\ell)=
 \begin{cases}12,&n=0,\\0,&n>0.\end{cases}
\]
After removing \(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2}\) from \(D_5\), the factors
with \(n,m>0\) cancel at \(r=1\).  The two boundary directions give
\[
 \left.\frac{D_5}{r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2}}\right|_{r=1}
 =(qs)^{1/2}\prod_{a\geq1}(1-q^a)^{12}(1-s^a)^{12}
 =\eta(z_1)^{12}\eta(z_3)^{12}.
\]
This calculation first holds on a product convergence region and then
continues along the diagonal.  The quotient is even in \(z\), since
the remaining factors pair \(\ell\) and \(-\ell\).

Finally \(r^{1/2}-r^{-1/2}=2\pi iz+O(z^3)\).
\end{proof}

The eta factors never vanish on \(\mathbb H_1\).
This proves the simple order on the diagonal and fixes its leading
section.  An eventual geometric line comparison must reproduce both
the factor \(2\pi i\) and the two eta powers.

\section{The square and the counting normalization}
\label{sec:view1-squared-scalar}

Set
\[
 \Xi=D_5^2=\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}},\qquad
 \Delta_{10}=\Delta_5^2=4096\,\Xi.
\]
The character of \(\Delta_5\) disappears on squaring.
Both \(\Xi\) and \(\Delta_{10}\) are weight-ten forms, with
different leading coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[The weight-ten normalization]
\label{prop:view1-square-chi-10}
Both counting papers \cite{OPand,OB} denote \(\Xi\) by
\(\chi_{10}\).  Its product is
\[
 \Xi=qrs\prod_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}
                  (1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{2f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
In the unscaled notation of this book,
\[
 \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\chi_{10}=\Delta_{10}=\Delta_5^2.
\]
Thus this last bare \(\chi_{10}\) is \(4096\) times the form
bearing that name in the two cited counting papers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Squaring the product doubles its exponents and squares its
prefactor.  The product in \cite[(12)]{OPand} and
\cite[(3)]{OB} has prefactor \(qrs\) and coefficients
\(c(4nm-\ell^2)=2f(nm,\ell)\).
It is therefore \(\Xi\), coefficient by coefficient.
The unscaled equalities are the stated notation.
\end{proof}

For primitive nonzero K3 curve classes
\(\beta_h^2=2h-2\), the reduced disconnected Gromov--Witten theorem
of Oberdieck--Pixton \cite[Theorem 1]{OB} is
\[
 \sum_{g,h,d\geq0}N_{g,h,d}\,u^{2g-2}q^{h-1}s^{d-1}
 =-\frac1{\Xi(q,e^{iu},s)}.
\]
The right side means its Laurent expansion at \(u=0\).
The DT/PT and GW/PT comparisons in Chapter~\ref{ch:pentadic}
give the rational-coefficient form of the primitive DT series,
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{OP}}^X=-\Xi^{-1}=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The DT Euler variable is \(-r\).  Its expansion at \(r=0\), a
product expansion in the chamber \(|r|>1\), and a genus expansion
at \(r=e^{iu}\) are different expansions of the same rational
coefficients.  Arbitrary imprimitive classes require additional
statements.

The convention
\(Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}^{K3\times E}:=-Z_{\mathrm{OP}}^X/4096
=\Delta_5^{-2}\) names a rescaled scalar function.
Constructing a protected state space and a trace with this value is
a further problem.  Squaring also loses the order-two character of
the line and the sign of its section.

\section{The root denominator}
\label{sec:view1-2Z-denominator}

For the Gritsenko--Nikulin Lie superalgebra
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\), let
\(\operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})\) denote its
Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator in the character convention
\(e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,\zeta)}\).
The change from product degrees to roots is
\[
 \alpha(n,\ell,m)=2nf_2-\ell f_3+2mf_{-2}
 =n\delta_1+m\delta_2+(n+m-\ell)\delta_3.
\]
It identifies the charge lattice with \(M_{II}\), an index-four
sublattice of \(M_0\).  Direct pairing gives
\[
 q^nr^\ell s^m=e^{-\pi i(\alpha(n,\ell,m),\zeta)}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[The doubled denominator]
\label{prop:view1-GN-doubled-denominator}
In the specified character convention,
\[
 \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})(Z)
 =D_5(2Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Gritsenko--Nikulin's denominator theorem identifies the signed root
multiplicities with \(f(nm,\ell)\), and its Weyl vector is
\(\rho=f_2-\tfrac12f_3+f_{-2}\)
\cite[\S3 and Theorem 4.1]{GN}.
Replacing \(Z\) by \(2Z\) changes each
\(e^{-\pi i(\alpha,\zeta)}\) to
\(e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,\zeta)}\), including the Weyl prefactor.
The scalar is the theta normalization already fixed above.
\end{proof}

The simple imaginary generators and their parities are part of the
Lie superalgebra construction in Chapter~\ref{ch:view2-bkm}.
A graded super vector space with the same Euler dimensions would
give the same product but would not define this denominator algebra.

\section{The orthogonal lift and the geometric comparison}
\label{sec:view1-Sp-Spin-bridge}
\label{sec:view1-summary}

The exceptional isomorphism
\(\PSp_4(\R)\simeq\SO(3,2)_+\) identifies Siegel space with a
type-IV domain.  Chapter~\ref{ch:singular-theta-lift} constructs the
regularized theta lift on that domain, starting from the
weight-\(-\tfrac12\) vector-valued theta components of
\(\phi_{0,1}\).  Its multiplicative product is \(D_5\) in the
monic cusp normalization.

\begin{theorem}[The automorphic target]
\label{thm:view1-package}
The section \(\mathcal D_X=\Delta_5\) has weight five,
Maass character \(\nu_5\), and simple Humbert divisor of
discriminant one.  Its cusp expansion is the height-completed
Borcherds product, its first Fourier--Jacobi coefficient is \(64b\),
and its separating leading section is
\[
 128\pi iz_2\,\eta(z_1)^{12}\eta(z_3)^{12}.
\]
Its square is \(4096\,\Xi\), and its root denominator is
\(64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assertions follow from the product theorem, Maass's formulas,
the diagonal-divisor theorem, and the two boundary calculations.
\end{proof}

The retained hypotheses in
Appendix~\ref{app:obstruction-discharges} specify the D0-HN datum
and quotient orientation.  They also specify Weyl lifts,
the wall datum \((O2_{\mathrm{atlas}})\), and the finite
Pfaffian datum \((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\).
The relative Pfaffian--Dirac statement is treated in
Chapter~\ref{ch:pfaffian-dirac} under these data.

A geometric realization must define
\(\operatorname{Pf}_{\mathrm{prot}}(\mathfrak D_X^{\mathrm{DI}})\)
on its own line and compare it with \(\sigma^*\mathcal D_X\).
The line isomorphism must preserve the character and both boundary
sections computed above.  An identification of Hall or chiral
operations has additional content beyond this line comparison.
The mirror-discriminant interpretation likewise requires an
independently defined period map and divisor comparison.
