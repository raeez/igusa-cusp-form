\chapter{K3 lattices and spherical objects}
\label{ch:k3xe-setup}

The three real simple roots of the Igusa denominator have matrix
\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2&-2\\-2&2&-2\\-2&-2&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We first realize this matrix as the Euler form of three line bundles
on a projective K3 surface.  Their extension spaces then give a doubled
quiver with two arrows between each pair of vertices.  A second choice
of the line bundles makes their Hilbert polynomials equal, so that
polystability supplies a formal differential graded algebra.

These constructions take place on the surface.  Pushing the bundles
onto a fiber of $S\times E$ introduces a normal deformation and cancels
the Euler form.  To compare the resulting threefold charges with
Igusa exponents, we must therefore distinguish the surface pairing,
the threefold Euler form, and the quadratic Gram map.  We compute all
three before considering their compatibility with Hall operations.

\section{Cohomological and algebraic Mukai lattices}
\label{sec:k3xe-mukai-lattice}
\label{subsec:k3-mukai-lattice}

Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective K3 surface.  Write
$\omega_S\in H^4(S,\Z)$ for the class of a point, normalized by
$\int_S\omega_S=1$.  The N\'eron--Severi group $\NS(S)$ is the image
of the first Chern class map $\Pic(S)\to H^2(S,\Z)$.  Its rank is
$\rho(S)$.

\begin{definition}[Mukai lattice]
\label{def:mukai-lattice}
The cohomological Mukai lattice is
\[
\Lambda_S=\widetilde H(S,\Z)
=H^0(S,\Z)\oplus H^2(S,\Z)\oplus H^4(S,\Z).
\]
We write its elements as $(r,D,s)$, with the last coordinate denoting
$s\omega_S$, and use the pairing
\[
\langle(r,D,s),(r',D',s')\rangle=D\cdot D'-rs'-r's.
\]
The algebraic Mukai lattice is its sublattice
\[
\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}=\Z\oplus\NS(S)\oplus\Z.
\]
For $F\in D^b\Coh(S)$, its Mukai vector is
\[
v(F)=\operatorname{ch}(F)\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(S)}
=\bigl(\rk F,c_1(F),\rk F+\operatorname{ch}_2(F)\bigr).
\]
Here Chern characters and ranks of a complex are alternating sums,
and $\operatorname{ch}_2(F)$ denotes its integral over $S$.
\end{definition}

The formula uses $\operatorname{td}(S)=1+2\omega_S$ and
$\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(S)}=1+\omega_S$.  The last coordinate is
integral because $D^2$ is even.  The two lattices have different ranks:
$\Lambda_S$ has rank $24$, whereas $\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}$ has rank
$\rho(S)+2$.

The Euler pairing is
$\chi_S(F,G)=\sum_i(-1)^i\dim_\C\Ext^i_S(F,G)$.
The numerical Grothendieck group is $K_0(S)$ modulo the radical
of this pairing.

\begin{proposition}[Riemann--Roch and numerical classes]
\label{k3:prop:rr}
For $F,G\in D^b\Coh(S)$,
\[
\chi_S(F,G)=-\langle v(F),v(G)\rangle.
\]
The Mukai vector induces an isomorphism from the numerical
Grothendieck group of $S$ to $\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The involution $(r,D,s)^\vee=(r,-D,s)$ satisfies
\[
\int_S(r,D,s)^\vee(r',D',s')=rs'+r's-D\cdot D'.
\]
Hirzebruch--Riemann--Roch identifies this expression with
$\chi_S(F,G)$ when the vectors are $v(F)$ and $v(G)$.

The vectors of $\mathcal O_S$, a point sheaf $\mathcal O_x$, and a
line bundle $\mathcal O_S(D)$ are
\[
(1,0,1),\qquad (0,0,1),\qquad (1,D,1+D^2/2).
\]
Their integer combinations generate $\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}$.
Its pairing is nondegenerate by the Hodge index theorem.  Thus the
kernel of $v$ is precisely the radical of the Euler form, which
proves the assertion about numerical classes.
\end{proof}

For a zero-dimensional subscheme $Z\subset S$ of length $a$, the exact
sequence $0\to I_Z\to\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal O_Z\to0$ gives
\[
v(I_Z)=(1,0,1-a),\qquad
\langle v(I_Z),v(I_Z)\rangle=2a-2.
\]
In particular, a point sheaf and an ideal of a point have different
Mukai vectors.  The numerical quotient is essential in
Proposition~\ref{k3:prop:rr}: quotienting the algebraic Grothendieck
group merely by torsion does not produce the full cohomological
lattice.  The construction and Riemann--Roch convention are those of
\cite{Mukai1984,Mukai1987,HuybrechtsK3}.

\begin{example}[Points, ideals and divisors]
\label{ex:mukai-vector-point-and-ideal}
For a point $p$, a zero-dimensional subscheme $Z$ of length $a$, and
a smooth curve $C$ of genus $g$, the vectors are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
F&v_S(F)\\ \hline
\mathcal O_S&(1,0,1)\\
\mathcal O_p&(0,0,1)\\
I_Z&(1,0,1-a)\\
\mathcal O_S(D)&(1,D,1+D^2/2)\\
I_C=\mathcal O_S(-C)&(1,-C,g)\\
\mathcal O_C&(0,C,1-g).
\end{array}
\]
The ideal sequences give the second, third and last rows.  The
exponential Chern character gives the line-bundle rows, and
adjunction gives $C^2=2g-2$.  In particular,
\[
v_S(\mathcal O_p)^2=0,\quad v_S(I_Z)^2=2a-2,\quad
\langle v_S(\mathcal O_S),v_S(\mathcal O_p)\rangle=-1.
\]
The last equality agrees with $\chi_S(\mathcal O_S,\mathcal O_p)=1$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The hyperbolic sign]
\label{rem:mukai-sign-convention}
The K3 lattice $H^2(S,\Z)$ is
$U^{\oplus3}\oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus2}$, where $U$ has basis $e,f$ with
$e^2=f^2=0$ and $e\cdot f=1$.  The vectors $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,0,1)$
have pairing $-1$.  Replacing the second by its negative identifies
their span with $U$.  Hence
\[
\Lambda_S\cong U^{\oplus4}\oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus2}
\]
is even and unimodular of signature $(4,20)$.
Changing only the sign of the hyperbolic off-diagonal entries gives
an isometric integral lattice.  Riemann--Roch fixes which sign belongs
to the chosen Mukai vector.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Two hyperbolic planes]
\label{lem:two-hyperbolic-planes}
The full lattice $\Lambda_S$ contains an orthogonal primitive copy of
$U\oplus U$.  Such a copy need not lie in $\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take two summands in the displayed decomposition.  For a projective
surface of Picard rank one, the algebraic lattice has rank three and
cannot contain a rank-four sublattice.
\end{proof}

\section{Three spherical line bundles}
\label{k3:sec:spherical}

An object $L\in D^b\Coh(S)$ is spherical if
$\Ext^0(L,L)=\Ext^2(L,L)=\C$ and its other self-extension groups
vanish.  Every line bundle on a K3 surface is spherical, since its
self-extensions are $H^*(S,\mathcal O_S)$.  The problem is to arrange
the extensions between three different line bundles.

We use the following consequence of the K3 period theorem.  A
primitive even sublattice of signature $(1,r-1)$ in the K3 lattice is
the N\'eron--Severi lattice of some projective K3 surface.  If a
positive vector in that lattice is perpendicular to no vector of
square $-2$, a marking can put it in the ample cone.  Indeed, a
generic period makes the prescribed lattice exactly algebraic, and
reflections in square-$-2$ classes move positive chambers to the ample
chamber \cite[Chapters~7, 8 and 14]{HuybrechtsK3}.

\begin{proposition}[A triple with the required Euler form]
\label{k3:prop:first-triple}
For every positive integer $d$, there is a polarized K3 surface
$(S,H)$ with $H^2=2d$ and line bundles $L_0,L_1,L_2$ such that
\[
\dim\Ext^k(L_i,L_j)=
\begin{cases}
1,&i=j,\ k=0,2,\\
2,&i\ne j,\ k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus $(\chi_S(L_i,L_j))_{i,j}=G$.
The bundles can be chosen with $L_0=\mathcal O_S$ and
\[
\chi(L_0(mH))=dm^2+2,\qquad
\chi(L_i(mH))=dm^2-2\quad(i=1,2).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In three orthogonal hyperbolic planes choose bases $e_i,f_i$ and set
\[
D_1=e_1-4f_1,\qquad
D_2=-4f_1+e_2-4f_2,\qquad H=e_3+df_3.
\]
The lattice $N_d=\langle D_1,D_2,H\rangle$ is primitive: the coefficient
minor in rows $e_1,e_2,e_3$ is the identity.  Its Gram matrix is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-8&-4&0\\-4&-8&0\\0&0&2d\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It has signature $(1,2)$.  Every vector perpendicular to $H$ has
square $-8(a^2+ab+b^2)$, so none has square $-2$.  The period theorem
therefore gives a marked surface with $\NS(S)=N_d$ and $H$ ample.

Take $L_i=\mathcal O_S(D_i)$ for $i=1,2$.  Each of
$D_1,D_2,D_1-D_2$ has square $-8$ and is perpendicular to $H$.
A nonzero effective divisor has positive intersection with $H$.
Consequently neither sign of any of these three classes is effective.

For each such class $D$, we obtain
\[
h^0(\mathcal O_S(D))=h^2(\mathcal O_S(D))=0,
\qquad \chi(\mathcal O_S(D))=2+D^2/2=-2.
\]
Hence $h^1(\mathcal O_S(D))=2$, giving all the off-diagonal
extensions.  The diagonal groups follow from sphericality.
Riemann--Roch applied to $D_i+mH$ gives the Hilbert polynomials.
\end{proof}

All three bundles have slope zero with respect to $H$, but their
reduced Hilbert polynomials are different.  A direct sum containing
$L_0$ and one of $L_1,L_2$ is therefore not Gieseker polystable
\cite[Definitions 1.2.3--1.2.4]{HuybrechtsLehn}.
To apply the formality theorem for polystable sheaves, we construct a
triple with one common polynomial.

\begin{theorem}[A common-polynomial spherical triple]
\label{k3:thm:common-triple}
There is a polarized K3 surface $(S,H)$ with $H^2=8$ and three line
bundles $L_1,L_2,L_3$ having the extension groups of
Proposition~\ref{k3:prop:first-triple}.  Each is $H$-Gieseker stable,
and each has Hilbert polynomial $4m^2-2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In $U^{\oplus3}$ put
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=e_1-4f_1,&
D_2&=-4f_1+e_2-4f_2,\\
D_3&=-4f_1-4f_2+e_3-4f_3,&
V&=f_1+f_2+2f_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The sublattice $N=\langle D_1,D_2,D_3,V\rangle$ is primitive, since
its coefficient minor in rows $e_1,e_2,e_3,f_1$ has determinant one.
In the stated basis its matrix is
\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}
-8&-4&-4&1\\-4&-8&-4&1\\-4&-4&-8&2\\1&1&2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Set $H=D_3+4V=e_3+4f_3$.  Then $H^2=8$, $H\cdot D_i=0$, and
$H\cdot V=2$.  The span of the $D_i$ is negative definite, with
\[
\left(\sum_i a_iD_i\right)^2
=-4\left(\sum_i a_i^2+\left(\sum_i a_i\right)^2\right).
\]
Thus $N$ has signature $(1,3)$.  A vector in $N$ perpendicular to
$H$ has zero $V$ coordinate, and its square is divisible by eight.
In particular, $H^\perp\cap N$ contains no square-$-2$ class.

Choose a surface with $\NS(S)=N$ and $H$ ample.  Set
$L_i=\mathcal O_S(D_i)$.  For $i\ne j$, the class $D_j-D_i$ is
nonzero, has square $-8$, and is perpendicular to $H$.  The previous
cohomology argument gives $\Ext^1(L_i,L_j)=\C^2$ and no other
off-diagonal extensions.  A line bundle is Gieseker stable: a proper
subsheaf of the same rank has a nonzero torsion quotient and therefore
a smaller reduced Hilbert polynomial.  Finally,
\[
\chi(L_i(mH))=2+(D_i+mH)^2/2=4m^2-2.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Integral gluing and the root sublattice}
\label{k3:sec:gluing}

The primitive embedding above specifies more than a rational
intersection form.  The ample class and the negative classes do not
split the Picard lattice integrally.  We compute the missing classes
before using the spherical vectors as a root lattice.

For an integral nondegenerate lattice $K$, let
$K^*=\{x\in K\otimes\Q:(x,K)\subset\Z\}$ and
$A_K=K^*/K$.  If $K$ is even, its discriminant quadratic form is
$q_K(x+K)=x^2\bmod 2\Z$.

\begin{proposition}[The Picard gluing]
\label{k3:prop:picard-gluing}
In Theorem~\ref{k3:thm:common-triple}, let
$L=\langle D_1,D_2,D_3,H\rangle$.  Then
\[
[N:L]=4,\qquad N/L\cong\Z/4,
\qquad \det L=-2048,\qquad \det N=-128.
\]
The overlattice is obtained by adjoining the isotropic discriminant
class of $(H-D_3)/4$.  Moreover,
\[
A_N\cong\Z/8\oplus\Z/16.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The change of basis $H=D_3+4V$ has determinant four.  It identifies
$N/L$ with the cyclic group generated by $V+L$.  In $L\otimes\Q$,
\[
V=(H-D_3)/4,\qquad V^2=0,
\qquad \bigl((V,D_1),(V,D_2),(V,D_3),(V,H)\bigr)=(1,1,2,2),
\]
where the last expression lists the four pairings.  Thus $V\in L^*$
and its class is isotropic of exact order four.

The $D_i$ block has eigenvalues $-16,-4,-4$, and the orthogonal
$H$ block is $(8)$.  Hence $\det L=-2048$ and
$\det N=\det L/4^2=-128$.  The greatest common divisors of the
one-, two-, and three-row minors of the displayed matrix of $N$ are
$1,1,8$.  Its Smith invariant factors are therefore $1,1,8,16$,
which give the stated discriminant group.
\end{proof}

The corresponding Mukai lattice is
$M=\Z\oplus N\oplus\Z$.  Write a vector as
$(r,a,b,c,t,s)$, meaning $(r,aD_1+bD_2+cD_3+tV,s)$.  The three
spherical vectors are
\[
v_i=v(L_i)=(1,D_i,-3).
\]
Let $F\subset M$ be their integer span.

\begin{proposition}[The spherical lattice and its complement]
\label{k3:prop:spherical-complement}
The lattice $F$ is saturated in $M$, and its Gram matrix is $-G$.
Its orthogonal complement consists exactly of
\[
C(r,a,c)=(r,a,a,c,4(c-a),3r-16a),\qquad r,a,c\in\Z.
\]
In this parametrization the complement has matrix
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}-6&16&0\\16&-40&-8\\0&-8&8\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det C=256,
\qquad A_C\cong\Z/2\oplus\Z/8\oplus\Z/16.
\]
The inclusion $F\oplus C\subset M$ has quotient
$\Z/2\oplus\Z/4$ and index eight.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient matrix of the $v_i$ has an identity minor in rows
$a,b,c$, proving saturation.  Their pairings are
\[
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=D_i\cdot D_j+6
=\begin{cases}-2&i=j,\\2&i\ne j.\end{cases}
\]
For $x=(r,a,b,c,t,s)$, the differences of the equations
$\langle x,v_i\rangle=0$ first give $b=a$ and $t=4(c-a)$.
The remaining equation gives $s=3r-16a$.  These equations have no
further divisibility condition, so the displayed parametrization
exhausts the integral complement.

Substitution into the Mukai pairing gives the matrix $C$.  Its
entries have greatest common divisor two, and its two-row minors
have greatest common divisor sixteen.  Together with its determinant,
these give Smith factors $2,8,16$.

To compute the quotient, impose the three relations $v_i=0$ in the
free group with coordinate generators $R,A,B,C_0,T,S_0$.
They identify $A=B=C_0=-R+3S_0$.  The three complement generators
then impose
\[
R+3S_0=0,\qquad -2R-10S_0-4T=0,
\qquad -R+3S_0+4T=0.
\]
Eliminating $R$ leaves $4S_0+4T=0$ and $6S_0+4T=0$.
Their relation matrix has Smith factors $2,4$.
As a check on the index, $\det M=128$ and
$\det F\det C=32\cdot256=8^2\det M$.
\end{proof}

The negative of the Mukai pairing on $F$ is the Euler matrix $G$.
This constructs an integral copy of the real-root lattice.  It does
not identify all algebraic charges with that copy.  For example,
$(0,0,1)$ lies outside $F\otimes\Q$: a combination of the $v_i$
with zero divisor part has every coefficient zero.  Point charges
therefore require additional classes even rationally.

\section{The extension quiver and its formal deformation equation}
\label{k3:sec:formal-quiver}

Choose finite-dimensional vector spaces $W_i$, not all zero, and put
$F_W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^3L_i\otimes W_i$, using the common-polynomial
triple.  This sheaf is $H$-polystable.  Its self-extension spaces in
degrees zero and two are
\[
\Ext^0(F_W,F_W)=\bigoplus_i\End(W_i),\qquad
\Ext^2(F_W,F_W)=\bigoplus_i\End(W_i)^*.
\]
The trace pairing identifies each dual matrix space with its original.
The degree-one space is
\[
\Ext^1(F_W,F_W)
=\bigoplus_{i\ne j}\Ext^1(L_i,L_j)\otimes\Hom(W_i,W_j).
\]

Choose two arrows $a:i\to j$ for each $i<j$.  A basis of
$\Ext^1(L_i,L_j)$ assigns a map $X_a:W_i\to W_j$ to each arrow.
Use a Serre-dual basis of $\Ext^1(L_j,L_i)$ to assign
$Y_a:W_j\to W_i$.

Degree-one Serre duality is alternating.
Choose the dual bases so that the quadratic Yoneda product is the
moment map
\[
\mu_i(X,Y)=
\sum_{a:\,\operatorname{head}(a)=i}X_aY_a
-\sum_{a:\,\operatorname{tail}(a)=i}Y_aX_a.
\]
This also fixes the sign of the duality convention.  The trace of
$\sum_i\mu_i$ vanishes because every arrow contributes one commutator.

\begin{theorem}[Pointed formal deformation model]
\label{k3:thm:formality}
The algebra $R\Hom_S(F_W,F_W)$ is formal.  Let $A$ be a local
Artin $\C$-algebra with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_A$.  Choose a
formality comparison.  Deformations with an identified special
fiber are then described by
\[
(X,Y)\in\Ext^1(F_W,F_W)\otimes\mathfrak m_A,
\qquad \mu_i(X,Y)=0,
\]
modulo the gauge group
$\exp(\Ext^0(F_W,F_W)\otimes\mathfrak m_A)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~1.2 of Budur--Zhang applies to every Gieseker-polystable sheaf
on a projective K3 surface, with no genericity condition on its ample
polarization \cite{BudurZhangFormality}.  It gives a quasi-isomorphism
zigzag from $R\Hom_S(F_W,F_W)$ to its cohomology algebra.

Passing to the commutator differential graded Lie algebra preserves
this comparison.  Over the nilpotent ideal $\mathfrak m_A$, its
Maurer--Cartan groupoid is invariant under quasi-isomorphism.  In the
cohomology algebra the differential is zero, so the equation is
$u^2=0$ for $u$ of degree one.  Products of off-diagonal extensions
can land only in the diagonal degree-two spaces.  Serre duality
identifies those products with the displayed $\mu_i$, giving the
quadratic equations and their gauge action.
\end{proof}

The quotient here fixes an identification of the special fiber.
An equivariant comparison with the action of
$\prod_i\GL(W_i)$ is additional structure on the formality map.
Likewise, a cyclic comparison must preserve the trace up to the
specified homotopies.  Formality for one sheaf supplies neither
compatibility with extension flags nor an orientation on a global
moduli stack.  These requirements will enter the Hall construction
separately.

\section{Passage to the threefold}
\label{subsec:k3xe-compact-target}

\begin{definition}[The product threefold]
\label{def:k3xe-threefold}
Let $E$ be a smooth complex elliptic curve with origin $0_E$ and put
$X=S\times E$.  Write $p_S,p_E$ for the projections.  Choose nonzero
holomorphic volume forms on $S$ and $E$ and use their product to
trivialize $K_X$.
\end{definition}

This is a Calabi--Yau threefold in the trivial-canonical sense.  Its
irregularity is $h^{1,0}(X)=1$, and
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=24\cdot0=0,
\qquad \chi(\mathcal O_X)=2\cdot0=0.
\]
The chosen volume form determines the trace used by the Calabi--Yau
structure.

\begin{proposition}[Extensions on a surface fiber]
\label{k3:prop:fiber-ext}
For $t\in E$, let $i_t:S\hookrightarrow X$ be the fiber inclusion.
For perfect complexes $A,B$ on $S$, there is a natural decomposition
\[
\Ext^k_X(i_{t*}A,i_{t*}B)
=\Ext^k_S(A,B)\oplus
\bigl(\Ext^{k-1}_S(A,B)\otimes T_tE\bigr).
\]
In particular, the self-extension dimensions of $i_{t*}L_i$ are
$(1,1,1,1)$ in degrees $0,1,2,3$.  For $i\ne j$, the dimensions
are $(0,2,2,0)$.  All their threefold Euler pairings vanish.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We have $i_{t*}A=A\boxtimes\mathcal O_t$.  The derived K\"unneth
formula for Hom gives
\[
R\Hom_X(i_{t*}A,i_{t*}B)
\simeq R\Hom_S(A,B)\otimes R\Hom_E(\mathcal O_t,\mathcal O_t).
\]
The second factor has cohomology $\C$ in degree zero and $T_tE$ in
degree one.  This follows from a local resolution by the equation of
the point.  Tensoring gives the asserted decomposition.  The two
summands have opposite Euler signs and the same dimensions.
\end{proof}

The normal line $T_tE$ is part of this formula.  A local parameter
identifies it with $\C$ and gives an exterior-generator model for the
normal extension algebra.  Changing the parameter rescales that
generator.  Moreover, $i_{t*}L_i$ has two-dimensional support.
A category consisting only of sheaves supported on curves in $X$
does not contain these objects.

\begin{remark}[The two K3 Euler characteristics]
\label{rem:two-K3-Eulers}
The integer $\chi(\mathcal O_S)=2$ appears in the line-bundle
Riemann--Roch formula.  The integer $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)=24$
appears in the elliptic genus.  With
$\phi_{0,1}=r^{-1}+10+r+O(q)$, the K3 elliptic genus is
$2\phi_{0,1}$, whose specialization at the elliptic variable zero
is $24$.  The Borcherds product of $\phi_{0,1}$ instead has weight
$10/2=5$ \cite{EZ:1,Borcherds95,GN}.  These are different operations
on the input coefficients.
\end{remark}

The distinction persists in the CHL examples.  Write $k_N$ for the
weight of the relevant genus-two form and $w_N=k_N/2$ for its
square-root weight.  The eta products entering the additive seeds
have weights $12,8,6,5,4$ at $N=1,2,3,4,5$, respectively.
The common factor $\vartheta_1(\tau,z)^2/\eta(\tau)^6$ has weight
$-2$, so the weights are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
k_N&10&6&4&3&2\\
w_N&5&3&2&3/2&1.
\end{array}
\]

The prime-level formula is $k_N=24/(N+1)-2$ for $N=2,3,5$
\cite{JS06Dyon,GK09CHL}.  At $N=4$, the eta product is
$\eta(\tau)^4\eta(2\tau)^2\eta(4\tau)^4$, giving weight five
before the Jacobi factor \cite[Table~1 and (3.17)]{GK10Composite}.
Thus the numerical equality $3+\chi(\mathcal O_S)=5$ at $N=1$
is not a formula for this sequence of weights.  It supplies no
comparison between a chiral coefficient and an automorphic weight.

\section{Paired charges and the Gram map}
\label{subsec:kunneth-formal-mukai}
\label{sec:gram-map-polarization}
\label{subsec:igusa-variables-gram-index}

Let $\omega_E\in H^2(E,\Z)$ satisfy $\int_E\omega_E=1$.  Since
$H^{\mathrm{odd}}(S,\Z)=0$, every even cohomology class on $X$ has
unique components in
$\widetilde H(S,\Z)\otimes(\Z\oplus\Z\omega_E)$.

\begin{definition}[Paired formal charge]
\label{def:formal-even-mukai-sector}
The formal charge lattice and its algebraic sublattice are
\[
\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}=\Lambda_S\oplus\Lambda_S,
\qquad
\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{alg}}=\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}
\oplus\Lambda_S^{\mathrm{alg}}.
\]
We order a charge as $c=(Q,P)$ by writing
\[
v_X=P\otimes1_E+Q\otimes\omega_E.
\]
The notation $\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{phys}}$ will mean this same formal
lattice when used without a specified stability condition.
For a perfect complex $\mathcal F$ on $X$, its actual charge is
$v_X(\mathcal F)=\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal F)
\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(X)}$.
\end{definition}

Both components are invariant under translation in $E$.  For an
actual complex, $P=v(Li_t^*\mathcal F)$ and
$Q=v(Rp_{S*}\mathcal F)$, so both are algebraic integral Mukai
vectors.  Conversely, external products with $\mathcal O_E$ and
$\mathcal O_t$ generate both summands numerically.  These formulas
also explain why $Q$ and $P$ cannot be assigned support dimensions
merely from their order in the pair.

Riemann--Roch on $X$ gives the skew form
\[
\chi_X(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
=\langle Q,P'\rangle-\langle P,Q'\rangle.
\]
Indeed, duality changes the sign of the $\omega_E$ component, and
integration on $S$ turns the Mukai pairing into the negative Euler
pairing.  A symmetric cross-pairing on the same group is an auxiliary
form and is not this threefold Euler form.

\begin{definition}[Gram degrees]
\label{def:gram-index-lattice}
\label{def:gram-map-cocycle}
The Gram-index group is $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gram}}=\Z^3$.  The Gram map
is
\[
\Pi_X(Q,P)=\left(\frac{\langle Q,Q\rangle}{2},
\langle Q,P\rangle,\frac{\langle P,P\rangle}{2}\right).
\]
For $\gamma=(n,\ell,m)$ set
$T(\gamma)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n&\ell/2\\\ell/2&m\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Gram matrix and Fourier character]
\label{prop:gram-matrix-convention}
For $\Pi_X(Q,P)=(n,\ell,m)$, the Gram matrix of $Q,P$ is $2T(\gamma)$,
and its determinant is $4nm-\ell^2$.  If
$Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&z\\z&\sigma\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$, $r=e^{2\pi iz}$ and $s=e^{2\pi i\sigma}$, then
\[
e^{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}(T(\gamma)Z)}=q^nr^\ell s^m.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Evenness of $\Lambda_S$ makes $n,m$ integral.  The matrix and
exponential identities follow by multiplying their two-by-two entries.
\end{proof}

At the standard cusp, we use the positive additive semigroup
\[
\begin{split}
\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}={}&\{(n,\ell,m):m>0,\ n\ge0\}\\
&\cup\{(n,\ell,0):n>0\}
\cup\{(0,\ell,0):\ell<0\}.
\end{split}
\]
The omitted coordinate $\ell$ ranges over all integers in the first
two sets.  Its Jacobi-active subset $\Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}$ consists
of the triples with $f(nm,\ell)\ne0$, where
$\phi_{0,1}=\sum f(a,b)q^ar^b$.  Positivity of an exponent is an
arithmetic condition.  It does not assert existence of a sheaf with
that charge.

\begin{proposition}[The ideal-sheaf charge]
\label{k3:prop:ideal-charge}
Let $Y\subset X$ have dimension at most one, with fundamental
one-cycle
\[
[Y]=\beta\otimes\omega_E+d\omega_S\otimes1_E
\]
and holomorphic Euler characteristic $n_Y=\chi(\mathcal O_Y)$.
Then
\[
P(I_Y)=(1,0,1-d),\qquad Q(I_Y)=(0,-\beta,-n_Y),
\]
and
\[
\Pi_X(I_Y)=(\beta^2/2,n_Y,d-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ideal sequence gives
$\operatorname{ch}(I_Y)=1-[Y]-n_Y\omega_S\omega_E$.
Here the degree-six coefficient is $-n_Y$ because $c_1(X)=0$ and
$Y$ has dimension at most one.  Multiplication by
$\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(X)}=1+\omega_S$ gives the two displayed
components.  Their Mukai pairings give the three Gram coordinates.
\end{proof}

In this sector $d$ is the degree of projection of the fundamental
cycle to $E$.  Its Gram exponent is $d-1$.  The distinction persists
outside this sector, where the Gram map remains quadratic and a
support-degree function requires a specified class of objects.

\subsection{The quadratic additivity defect}
\label{subsec:gram-map-bilinear-cocycle}

For charges $c=(Q,P)$ and $c'=(Q',P')$, define
\[
B(c,c')=\bigl(\langle Q,Q'\rangle,
\langle Q,P'\rangle+\langle Q',P\rangle,
\langle P,P'\rangle\bigr).
\]
This symmetric bilinear map measures the failure of the Gram degrees
to add.

\begin{lemma}[Polarization]
\label{lem:gram-additivity-defect}
\label{lem:b-cocycle-identity}
For all formal charges,
\[
\Pi_X(c+c')=\Pi_X(c)+\Pi_X(c')+B(c,c'),
\qquad B(c,c)=2\Pi_X(c).
\]
If $(\delta q)(c,c')=q(c)+q(c')-q(c+c')$, then $B=-\delta\Pi_X$.
In particular,
\[
B(b,c)-B(a+b,c)+B(a,b+c)-B(a,b)=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding $(Q+Q')^2/2$ gives the first coordinate and its cross term
$\langle Q,Q'\rangle$.  Expanding $(Q+Q',P+P')$ gives the two middle
cross terms.  The third coordinate is the same calculation with $P$.
Putting $c'=c$ gives the diagonal identity.  Bilinearity cancels the
four terms of the final equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The primitive is quadratic]
\label{rem:B-quadratic-not-linear}
\label{rem:polarization-not-bott}
\label{rem:quadratic-obstruction}
An additive map $q$ has $\delta q=0$.  Since $B$ is nonzero, an
additive change of coordinates cannot have coboundary $-B$.
The identity $B=-\delta\Pi_X$ instead uses the quadratic map
$\Pi_X$.  The diagonal value $2\Pi_X(c)$ is its polarization identity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Primitive formal lifts}
\label{subsec:formal-gram-triple-lift}

\begin{lemma}[A saturated lift of every Gram triple]
\label{lem:primitive-lift-every-gram-triple}
Choose two orthogonal hyperbolic planes in $\Lambda_S$, with bases
$e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2$.  For every $(n,\ell,m)\in\Z^3$, the vectors
\[
Q=e_1+nf_1,\qquad P=\ell f_1+e_2+mf_2
\]
have Gram degree $(n,\ell,m)$ and span a primitive rank-two sublattice.
The greatest common divisor of the coordinates of $Q\wedge P$ is one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The hyperbolic pairings give $Q^2=2n$, $P^2=2m$ and $Q\cdot P=\ell$.
The coefficient minor in rows $e_1,e_2$ is the identity matrix.
It proves saturation and gives coefficient one on $e_1\wedge e_2$
in $Q\wedge P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Formal and geometric lifts]
\label{rem:pointwise-not-hall-support}
The lemma constructs an integral cohomological pair for each prescribed
Gram matrix.  Algebraic pairs must lie in
$\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{alg}}$.  A stable sheaf with such a pair requires
further geometric conditions and a nonempty moduli space.  Even when
individual pairs are realized, a comparison with Hall multiplication
requires compatibility with addition of classes in exact triangles.
\end{remark}

\section{An additive extension of Gram degrees}
\label{sec:normal-ordered-extension}
\label{subsec:central-extension-additive-gram}

A Hall operation adds classes of objects.  The Gram map is quadratic,
so it cannot itself serve as an additive grading map.  Introduce a
second coordinate to record its cross terms.

\begin{definition}[Normal-ordered extension]
\label{def:normal-ordered-extension}
On the set
$\widehat\Gamma_X=\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{gram}}$
define
\[
(c,T)\star(c',T')=(c+c',T+T'+B(c,c')).
\]
The \emph{normal-ordered} Gram map and the raw placement are
\[
\overline\Pi_X(c,T)=\Pi_X(c)-T,
\qquad i_0(c)=(c,0).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The extension splits]
\label{thm:normal-ordered-extension-group-law}
The operation $\star$ makes $\widehat\Gamma_X$ an abelian group.
Its identity is $(0,0)$, its inverse formula is
\[
(c,T)^{-1}=(-c,-T+2\Pi_X(c)),
\]
and $\overline\Pi_X$ is a homomorphism.  The map
\[
\Psi:\widehat\Gamma_X\longrightarrow
\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}\oplus\Gamma_{\mathrm{gram}},
\qquad (c,T)\longmapsto(c,T-\Pi_X(c))
\]
is an isomorphism to the ordinary direct sum.  In particular,
$c\mapsto(c,\Pi_X(c))$ is an additive section of the charge projection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cocycle identity proves associativity, and symmetry of $B$ proves
commutativity.  Since $B(c,-c)=-2\Pi_X(c)$, the displayed inverse
multiplies with $(c,T)$ to $(0,0)$.  Polarization gives
\[
\begin{split}
\Pi_X(c+c')-T-T'-B(c,c')
&=\Pi_X(c)-T+\Pi_X(c')-T',
\end{split}
\]
which proves additivity of $\overline\Pi_X$.  The same identity shows
that $\Psi$ is additive, with inverse $(c,S)\mapsto(c,S+\Pi_X(c))$.
\end{proof}

The extension is thus split as an abelian group.  Its role is to
retain the correction coordinate in a proposed graded construction.
The raw placement $i_0$ is a set map, not a homomorphism, and the
additive section has zero normal-ordered Gram degree.  Neither map
provides an additive lift of arbitrary raw Gram degrees.

\subsection{Products of raw placements}
\label{subsec:normal-ordering-flag-formula}

\begin{lemma}[The flag formula]
\label{lem:multiplication-of-raw-placements}
For $c_1,\ldots,c_k\in\Gamma_X^{\mathrm{form}}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
i_0(c_1)\star\cdots\star i_0(c_k)
&=\left(\sum_i c_i,\sum_{i<j}B(c_i,c_j)\right),\\
\overline\Pi_X\bigl(i_0(c_1)\star\cdots\star i_0(c_k)\bigr)
&=\sum_i\Pi_X(c_i).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Adding one factor introduces
$B(\sum_{i<k}c_i,c_k)=\sum_{i<k}B(c_i,c_k)$.  Induction gives the
first identity.  Iterated polarization gives
$\Pi_X(\sum_i c_i)=\sum_i\Pi_X(c_i)+\sum_{i<j}B(c_i,c_j)$,
which proves the second.
\end{proof}

This is an identity of charge groups.  To use it on moduli of flags,
one must construct the additional coordinate and its transition maps
on those moduli.  A group law alone does not give that geometric data.

\subsection{An obstruction along a root string}
\label{subsec:raw-gram-descent-no-go}

\begin{definition}[Raw regrading]
\label{def:raw-pi-pushforward}
For a vector space $V=\bigoplus_cV_c$ graded by formal charges, define
\[
(\Pi_{X,*}^{\mathrm{raw}}V)_\gamma
=\bigoplus_{\Pi_X(c)=\gamma}V_c.
\]
A fixed-lift choice instead retains only $V_{L(\gamma)}$, where
$\Pi_X(L(\gamma))=\gamma$ for the selected degrees.
\end{definition}

Vector-space regrading is always defined when the indicated sums
exist.  It need not preserve the degree of a multiplication or a Lie
bracket.

\begin{theorem}[The fixed-lift root-string obstruction]
\label{thm:raw-gram-descent-no-go}
Let $\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_c\mathfrak p_c$ be a charge-graded Lie
superalgebra.  Suppose $x\in\mathfrak p_c$ and
$y\in\mathfrak p_{c'}$ satisfy
\[
[x,y]\ne0,\qquad [x,[x,y]]\ne0,\qquad \Pi_X(c)\ne0.
\]
The original bracket cannot be homogeneous for raw Gram degree on
both of these channels.  Consequently, fixed raw lifts of the real
simple generators cannot preserve the nonzero second root strings
of the matrix $G$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Homogeneity of the first bracket requires
\[
\Pi_X(c+c')=\Pi_X(c)+\Pi_X(c'),
\quad\text{hence }B(c,c')=0.
\]
Homogeneity of the second requires $B(c,c+c')=0$.  But
\[
B(c,c+c')=B(c,c)+B(c,c')=2\Pi_X(c)\ne0.
\]
This is a contradiction.  For the real simple generators attached to
$G$, the off-diagonal Cartan integer is $-2$.  The corresponding
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ root string has nonzero first and second brackets,
so it meets the stated hypotheses \cite{GN}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the obstruction]
\label{rem:no-go-scope}
The theorem concerns the original bracket on homogeneous chosen
charges.  It does not exclude a different bracket, a different
grading, or a comparison that mixes source lifts.  The additive map
$\overline\Pi_X$ removes this degree obstruction for an algebra
already graded by $\widehat\Gamma_X$.  Constructing such an algebra
and comparing its primitive bracket remain separate problems.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Operations preserved by additive regrading}
\label{k3:subsec:additive-regrading}

For a homomorphism $f:A\to B$ of abelian groups, let
\[
(f_*V)_b=\bigoplus_{a:\,f(a)=b}V_a.
\]
We use direct sums throughout this construction.  Cohomological
degree and super parity are retained as separate gradings.

\begin{proposition}[Additive regrading]
\label{k3:prop:regrading}
On graded $\Q$-complexes, $f_*$ is exact and symmetric monoidal.
It preserves homogeneous products, Lie superbrackets, coalgebra
maps, and their identities.  It commutes with the direct-sum bar construction of an augmented
differential graded algebra, including every finite length truncation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A degree component of $f_*$ is a direct sum of complexes, so exactness
holds over $\Q$.  The tensor comparison is the identification
\[
\begin{split}
(f_*V\otimes f_*W)_b
&=\bigoplus_{f(a)+f(a')=b}V_a\otimes W_{a'}\\
&=(f_*(V\otimes W))_b.
\end{split}
\]
The equality uses additivity of $f$.  This identification preserves
the symmetry and its Koszul signs, since it changes neither
cohomological degree nor parity.  It therefore preserves all
identities expressed by the stated operations.

For an augmented algebra $A_\bullet$, write $\bar A_\bullet$ for the
augmentation ideal.  The bar coalgebra has underlying complex
$\bigoplus_{j\ge0}(\bar A_\bullet[1])^{\otimes j}$, where $[1]$
lowers cohomological degree by one.  Its differential comes from the
internal differential and multiplication.  Exactness, the tensor
comparison, and preservation of these maps identify the two bar
constructions.  The same argument applies after restricting to
length at most any fixed integer.
\end{proof}

Taking $f=\overline\Pi_X$ gives the appropriate additive regrading
once the extended grading exists.  Pairings of degree zero, bracket
filtrations and finite Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt constructions are
preserved for the same reason.  Completed products require an
additional finiteness or continuity argument.  The finite versions,
including separate even and odd dimensions and zero-degree radicals,
are developed in Appendix~\ref{app:stability-support}.

\begin{remark}[The root comparison]
\label{rem:normal-ordered-recognition-target}
The root coordinates used later identify a Gram triple with
\[
\alpha(n,\ell,m)=2nf_2-\ell f_3+2mf_{-2}
\in\Lambda_{II}^{2,1}.
\]
Their form has $f_3^2=2$, $(f_2,f_{-2})=-1$, and all other pairings
zero.  Thus $\alpha(n,\ell,m)^2=2\ell^2-8nm$.
The three triples $(1,1,0)$, $(0,1,1)$ and $(0,-1,0)$ give matrix
$G$.  This arithmetic identification is independent of the saturated
surface embedding $F\subset M$.  A geometric realization must
compare the corresponding operations, as well as these lattices.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Sheaves representing the three simple Gram degrees]
\label{prop:type-ii-compact-mukai-representatives}
Choose $p\in S$ and a length-two subscheme $Z\subset S$.  The three
pairs of coherent sheaves
\[
x_{\delta_1}=(I_Z,I_p),\qquad
x_{\delta_2}=(I_p,I_Z),\qquad
x_{\delta_3}=(I_p,\mathcal O_p)
\]
have Gram degrees $(1,1,0)$, $(0,1,1)$ and $(0,-1,0)$,
respectively, when their vectors are ordered as $(Q,P)$.
These degrees are also represented by coherent sheaves on $X$:
for a fixed $t\in E$, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_1&=i_{t*}I_Z\oplus p_S^*I_p,\\
\mathcal F_2&=i_{t*}I_p\oplus p_S^*I_Z,\\
\mathcal F_3&=i_{t*}I_p\oplus p_S^*\mathcal O_p.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The vectors $v_S(I_Z)=(1,0,-1)$, $v_S(I_p)=(1,0,0)$ and
$v_S(\mathcal O_p)=(0,0,1)$ give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
&Q^2/2&Q\cdot P&P^2/2\\ \hline
x_{\delta_1}&1&1&0\\
x_{\delta_2}&0&1&1\\
x_{\delta_3}&0&-1&0.
\end{array}
\]
For any sheaf $A$ on $S$, the external-product formulas give
\[
v_X(i_{t*}A)=v_S(A)\otimes\omega_E,\qquad
v_X(p_S^*A)=v_S(A)\otimes1_E.
\]
They identify the charges of $\mathcal F_i$ with the displayed
pairs.  All supports are proper because $X$ is projective.
\end{proof}

The sheaves $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$ have rank one and a
surface-supported direct summand.  The sheaf $\mathcal F_3$ has surface and
curve summands.  Thus this construction gives actual coherent
charges, but not pure sheaves of one fixed dimension.  A realization
by stable objects in a specified heart, with the required extension
operations and oriented coefficients, is a further problem.  The
spherical line bundles of \S\ref{k3:sec:spherical} answer a different
question: they realize the additive Euler matrix of the real roots.

\section{Geometric support over the elliptic curve}
\label{sec:k3xe-hybrid-ran-carrier}
\label{subsec:projection-locality-obstruction}

The support of an object gives another constraint on a proposed
factorization construction.  A finite configuration on $E$ can
record objects whose projection is finite.  A curve projecting
dominantly to $E$ requires a different parameter space.

\begin{lemma}[Projection of an effective curve]
\label{lem:projection-locality-obstruction}
Let $Z=\sum_i m_i[C_i]$ be an effective one-cycle in $S\times E$.
If $(p_E)_*Z=b[E]$ with $b>0$, then $p_E(|Z|)=E$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proper image of an irreducible curve $C_i$ in $E$ is a point or
all of $E$.  A dominant component contributes
$m_i\deg(C_i/E)>0$ to $b$, whereas a component mapping to a point
contributes zero.  If $b>0$, some component is dominant.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Support degree and Gram exponent]
\label{rem:geometric-vs-borcherds-degree}
For ideal sheaves in Proposition~\ref{k3:prop:ideal-charge}, the
support degree is $d$ and the third Gram coordinate is $d-1$.
For a sheaf on one surface fiber, the projection is a point even
though its support has dimension two.  Thus projection-finiteness
and support dimension are distinct conditions.  A moduli problem
must specify both before it can be assigned to a finite-configuration
or dominant-support parameter space.
\end{remark}

The finite-configuration space is the Ran prestack of $E$: its
$T$-points are nonempty finite subsets of $\Hom(T,E)$.  We call
objects projecting onto $E$ wrapped objects.  Combining the two
support types requires moduli for wrapped objects and correspondences
for mixed extensions.  Their existence and compatibility do not
follow from adjoining a second set of points to the Ran prestack.
Appendix~\ref{app:stability-support} states the stability, finiteness,
and equivariance conditions for such a construction.

\begin{definition}[Finite Harder--Narasimhan type]
\label{k3:def:hn-type}
Fix a stability condition and a collection of factor labels.
A Harder--Narasimhan filtration is a finite filtration with semistable
quotients of strictly decreasing phase.  When every quotient has a chosen factor label, its
\emph{retained HN type} is the ordered word of factor and phase labels.
\end{definition}

A finite set of such words can bound the types of objects used in an
extension correspondence.  The finite-window proof and the further
conditions needed for moduli of these objects are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:stability-support}.

The surface calculation supplies concrete spherical objects, a root
sublattice, and a formal deformation equation.  The threefold
calculation supplies their normal extensions and actual paired
charges.  A global Hall comparison must connect these constructions
through extension correspondences with a specified coefficient
theory, trace and orientation.  The following chapters develop the
arithmetic target before returning to those geometric operations.
