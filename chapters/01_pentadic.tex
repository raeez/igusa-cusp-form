\chapter*{The square-root realization problem}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The square-root realization problem}
\label{ch:frontispiece}

Counting curves on a product of a K3 surface and an elliptic curve
produces the reciprocal of a genus-two modular form. The geometric
realization problem asks for more: a space of protected states,
operations on those states, and an oriented square root of their
determinant. These are different constructions. Their relation can
be tested by asking what information survives a signed count.

Let \(S\) be a smooth complex projective K3 surface, so
\(K_S\simeq\mathcal O_S\) and \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\).
Let \(E\) be a smooth elliptic curve with an origin, and put
\(X=S\times E\). Choose a nonzero effective primitive class
\(\beta_h\in H_2(S,\Z)\) with \(\beta_h^2=2h-2\).
Here primitive means not an integer multiple greater than one of another
integral class. For \(d\geq0\), the class \((\beta_h,d)\) in
\(H_2(X,\Z)\) is the sum of \(\beta_h\) in a K3 fiber and
\(d\) copies of an elliptic fiber.

A one-dimensional subscheme \(C\subset X\) has this curve class
and an integer \(n=\chi(\mathcal O_C)\). Translation in \(E\)
moves \(C\) without changing its class. The reduced
Donaldson--Thomas (DT) invariant counts translation orbits with the
Behrend weight:
\[
 \DT_{n,(\beta_h,d)}=
 \int_{\operatorname{Hilb}^{(\beta_h,d),n}(X)/E}\nu\,de.
\]
The Hilbert scheme parametrizes these subschemes, and \(\nu\) is
the integer-valued Behrend function of the quotient. The integral is
the weighted Euler characteristic: each level set \(\nu^{-1}(a)\)
contributes \(a\,e(\nu^{-1}(a))\), with the stack weighting for
finite stabilizers.

The reduction removes the trivial obstruction
direction induced by the holomorphic symplectic form of \(S\).
It is this reduced quotient theory that has a nonzero counting series.

To state its automorphic expression, write
\[
 \mathcal H_2=\{Z=Z^t\in M_2(\C):\Imag Z>0\},\qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}z_1&z_2\\z_2&z_3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 (q,r,s)=(e^{2\pi iz_1},e^{2\pi iz_2},e^{2\pi iz_3}).
\]
Let \(\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}\) be the monic Igusa cusp form of
weight ten, with coefficient \([qrs]\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=1\).

For fixed \(h,d\), the counting series
\(\sum_n\DT_{n,(\beta_h,d)}y^n\) is a Laurent series at
\(y=0\). Let \(R_{h,d}(y)\in\Q(y)\) be its rational
representative, whose existence follows from reduced DT/PT
rationality \cite[Theorem 3]{OberdieckReducedPT}.
The primitive counting identity is an equality with rational
coefficients in \(r\):
\[
 Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}(r,q,s):=
 \sum_{h,d\geq0}R_{h,d}(-r)q^{h-1}s^{d-1}
 =-\frac{1}{\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}(Z)}.
\]
Expansion of each rational coefficient at \(r=0\) recovers
\(\sum_n\DT_{n,(\beta_h,d)}(-r)^n\).

For each \(h\), the invariant is computed on a projective K3 surface
carrying \(\beta_h\). Primitive deformation invariance makes this
choice immaterial. The coefficient variable for Euler characteristic is \(y=-r\).
The variables \(q\) and \(s\) record the K3 square through \(h-1\)
and elliptic degree through \(d-1\).

The equality uses reduced
DT/stable-pair (Pandharipande--Thomas, PT) comparison,
primitive Gromov--Witten (GW)/PT comparison, and the
Oberdieck--Pixton theorem \cite[Theorem 3(i)]{OberdieckReducedPT},
\cite[Proposition 5]{OPand}, \cite[Theorem 1]{OB}.
The respective comparisons and expansion domains are stated below.

The theta-normalized square root is the weight-five Igusa form
\(\Delta_5\), fixed by
\[
 \chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
 \qquad [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5=64,
 \qquad 4096=64^2.
\]
The subscript BPS refers to the Bogomolny--Prasad--Sommerfield
protected-state interpretation being sought. The rescaled scalar
\(Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{BPS}}:=-Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}/4096\)
is \(\Delta_5^{-2}\). This definition alone supplies neither a
protected state space nor a trace on it. Such a trace requires the
complex, cyclic pairing, completion, and comparison hypotheses
specified in \S\ref{sec:beilinson-chain-placement}.

\paragraph*{Squaring loses a character.}
Consider the flat complex line on a circle obtained from
\(\R\times\C\) by \((t,a)\sim(t+1,-a)\).
Parallel transport around the circle multiplies a vector by \(-1\).
Its tensor square has transport \(+1\), just like the trivial line.
The two lines therefore have identical flat tensor squares but are
not isomorphic as flat lines.

More generally a line may be twisted
by any order-two flat line without changing its square.
A determinant section consequently does not determine an oriented
Pfaffian section: the square-root line and its transport remain
additional data. This is the distinction used by
Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar-pfaffian-data-not-section}.

For \(\Delta_5\), this lost datum is an order-two automorphic
character. Recovering it geometrically is one part of the compact
realization problem. A signed count gives the difference of two
parity dimensions. Recovering the algebra of states also requires
the individual dimensions and the multiplication.
The first denominator calculation below shows where these separate
questions occur.

\chapter{The geometric realization problem}
\label{ch:pentadic}

An infinite product can be realized as the character of an algebra,
as an index of geometric complexes, or as the determinant of a family.
To compare these realizations, one must compare what is multiplied.
The root algebra has brackets and two parity dimensions at each root.
Geometric complexes have extension maps, orientations and specialization.
A determinant line has transport and a section.

The Igusa product gives a precise common target.
Its root lattice fixes the charges, its Jacobi seed fixes the signed
multiplicities, and its character fixes the automorphic line.
Hilbert schemes provide independent geometric coefficient complexes.
Their indices determine the scalar comparison, while their operations
pose the stronger realization problem.

\section{The lattice, roots and Weyl vector}
\label{op:sec:root-datum}

A lattice is a finite free abelian group with an integral
symmetric pairing. The denominator uses the Lorentzian lattice
\(\La^{2,1}=\HypPlan\oplus\langle2\rangle\), with basis
\((f_2,f_3,f_{-2})\), pairings
\((f_2,f_{-2})=-1\), \((f_3,f_3)=2\), and all other pairings zero.
The two positive and one negative directions give signature
\((2,1)\). Here $U(k)$ is the rank-two lattice with Gram
matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&k\\k&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Changing the sign of one basis vector identifies the displayed
hyperbolic plane with $U$.

The index-four sublattice
\(\La^{2,1}_{II}=\Z(2f_2)\oplus\Z f_3\oplus\Z(2f_{-2})\)
is abstractly \(U(4)\oplus\langle2\rangle\), with determinant
\(-32\). For integral indices \((n,\ell,m)\in\Z^3\), define
\[
 \alpha(n,\ell,m)=2nf_2-\ell f_3+2mf_{-2}.
\]
The three simple real roots
\(\delta_1=2f_2-f_3\), \(\delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3\),
\(\delta_3=f_3\) have Gram matrix
\[
 ((\delta_i,\delta_j))=
 \begin{pmatrix}2&-2&-2\\-2&2&-2\\-2&-2&2\end{pmatrix}
 =4\Id_3-2\mathbf J_3.
\]
Here \(\mathbf J_3\) is the all-ones matrix. The reflections
\(s_{\delta_i}(x)=x-(x,\delta_i)\delta_i\) preserve this form.
They generate \(W^{(2)}(\La^{2,1}_{II})\).

Choose the chamber
\(\Poly_{II}\) containing the Weyl vector
\[
 \rho=\tfrac12(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
      =f_2-\tfrac12f_3+f_{-2},\qquad (\rho,\delta_i)=-1.
\]
Thus its wall inequalities in this convention are
\((x,\delta_i)\leq0\) in the chosen negative cone.

\section{Automorphic sections and the Jacobi seed}
\label{sec:retained-e3-five-readings}

The square root transforms as a section of a line, rather than as
an invariant function. Write \(\Sp_4(\Z)\) for the group preserving
the standard alternating form on \(\Z^4\). The notation
\(\Sp(2,\Z)\) denotes the same group. Its block matrix
\(g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
acts by \(gZ=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}\).
The weight-one automorphic line \(\mathcal L\) on the quotient
stack is defined by this factor \(\det(CZ+D)\).
A form of weight \(k\) and character \(\nu\) satisfies
\[
 F(gZ)=\nu(g)\det(CZ+D)^kF(Z).
\]

The product of the ten even genus-two theta constants is normalized
so that its \(q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}\) coefficient is \(+64\).
With this choice of sign it is \(\Delta_5\).
Its character \(\nu_{\Delta_5}\) has order two
\cite{Igusa1962,MAASS:1}. In particular
\[
 \mathcal D_X:=\Delta_5\in
 H^0(\Sp_4(\Z)\backslash\mathcal H_2,
       \mathcal L^{\otimes5}\otimes\nu_{\Delta_5}),\qquad
 \Delta_{10}:=\Delta_5^2\in M_{10}(\Sp_4(\Z)).
\]
The alternative names \(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\chi_{10}=\Delta_{10}\)
refer to this theta normalization. The monic form is
\(D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5\), so \(\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2\).

The product exponents come from one elliptic function with modular
symmetry. A weak Jacobi form of weight zero and index one is a
holomorphic function \(\phi(\tau,z)\) on \(\Hpl\times\C\).
It obeys the transformation laws
\begin{align*}
 \phi\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{z}{c\tau+d}\right)
 &=e^{2\pi i cz^2/(c\tau+d)}\phi(\tau,z),\\
 \phi(\tau,z+\lambda\tau+\mu)
 &=e^{-2\pi i(\lambda^2\tau+2\lambda z)}\phi(\tau,z)
 \quad(\lambda,\mu\in\Z),
\end{align*}
for \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\in\SL_2(\Z)\). Its Fourier expansion has nonnegative powers
of \(q_\tau=e^{2\pi i\tau}\).

The normalized seed is
\[
 \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=\sum_{n\geq0,\ell\in\Z}f(n,\ell)
 q_\tau^n e^{2\pi i\ell z}=e^{-2\pi iz}+10+e^{2\pi iz}+O(q_\tau).
\]
It is half the K3 elliptic genus. Its coefficients depend on
\(4n-\ell^2\), vanish below \(-1\), and satisfy
\(f(1,0)=108\), \(f(1,\pm1)=-64\), \(f(1,\pm2)=10\)
\cite{EZ:1,DMVV}. The elliptic law explains the discriminant:
replacing \(\ell\) by \(\ell+2\lambda\) replaces \(n\) by
\(n+\lambda\ell+\lambda^2\), preserving \(4n-\ell^2\).

\section{The automorphic product and denominator}
\label{sec:edge-1-2}

To expand at a cusp, choose the positive indices
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=
 \{(n,\ell,m):m>0,\ n\geq0,\ \ell\in\Z\}
 \cup\{(n,\ell,0):n>0,\ \ell\in\Z\}
 \cup\{(0,\ell,0):\ell<0\}.
\]
Only \(4nm-\ell^2\geq-1\) contributes. Set
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{act}}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}:
 f(nm,\ell)\ne0\}\). Expand each factor in its positive monomial
in a cusp chamber where \(|q^nr^\ell s^m|<1\) on this support.
For example \(\Imag z_1=\Imag z_3=t\), \(\Imag z_2=-a\),
with \(0<a<t\) and \(t\) sufficiently large, selects such a
chamber.

The support condition bounds \(\ell\) at fixed \((n,m)\).
The remaining degree-zero factor is \(1-r^{-1}\). These facts
make coefficient extraction finite in the associated completion.
The Borcherds product is
\[
 \Delta_5(Z)=64q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
 \prod_{(n,\ell,m)\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}
 (1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]

A lattice-graded Lie superalgebra has even and odd root spaces.
The signed multiplicity is their superdimension
\(\smult\mathfrak g_\alpha=\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha^{\bar0}
-\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha^{\bar1}\).
The Gritsenko--Nikulin Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) has
\(\smult\mathfrak g_{\alpha(n,\ell,m)}=f(nm,\ell)\)
on the active support. Its denominator identity reads
\[
 \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})
 =\sum_{w\in W^{(2)}}\sgn(w)w\left(e^\rho-
 \sum_{a\in\La^{2,1}_{II}\cap\R_{>0}\Poly_{II}}
 m(a)e^{\rho+a}\right)
 =64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]

The symbols \(e^\alpha\) are lattice characters. Doubling \(Z\)
converts the half-exponent product convention to these denominator
characters. The integers \(m(a)\) are the imaginary-simple
correction coefficients \cite[Theorem 2.3(a)]{GN}.

The first correction can be seen without reconstructing the whole
algebra. Remove the leading monomial from the product and take
\(s\)-degree zero. Up to \(q\)-degree one, its factors are
\[
 (1-r^{-1})(1-qr)(1-q)^{10}(1-qr^{-1}).
\]
The coefficient of \(q\) is
\[
 -(1-r^{-1})(r+10+r^{-1})=-r-9+9r^{-1}+r^{-2}.
\]
Thus the constant term in \(r\) is \(-9\).

In lattice notation
it occurs at \(\rho+2f_2\). But
\[
 \rho^2=-2+\tfrac12=-\tfrac32,\qquad
 (\rho+2f_2)^2=-6+\tfrac12=-\tfrac{11}{2}.
\]
Reflections preserve the norm, so this exponent is outside the Weyl
orbit of \(\rho\). The uncorrected alternating sum cannot give
the product. The imaginary-root correction is necessary.

Signed multiplicities still leave a second question. At
\(\delta_{123}=\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:bkm-delta123-presentation-count} computes
\((\dim\mathfrak g^{\bar0}_{\delta_{123}},
\dim\mathfrak g^{\bar1}_{\delta_{123}})=(29,93)\).
Their difference is \(-64=f(1,1)\). The pair \((30,94)\) has the
same difference. The exponent \(-64\) therefore cannot by itself
recognize the parity-resolved root space, still less its brackets.
This is why the compact primitive-algebra comparison requires more
than the scalar denominator.

\section{The regularized theta lift}
\label{sec:edge-1-5}

The Jacobi transformation law also explains the product's
modularity. Introduce the rank-five lattice
\(L=\La^{3,2}=\HypPlan\oplus\HypPlan\oplus\langle2\rangle\)
of signature \((3,2)\), and set \(M=L(-1)\).
The type-IV domain of negative oriented two-planes in \(L\) is the
domain of positive oriented two-planes in \(M\).
The exterior-square representation identifies its chosen component
with \(\mathcal H_2\), and its arithmetic group with
\(\Gamma_{3,2}=\wedge^2\Sp_4(\Z)/\{\pm\Id\}\).
This rank-five lattice carries the theta kernel. The rank-three
cusp quotient carries the product indices.

The index-one elliptic theta functions are
\(\theta_{1,\mu}(\tau,z)=\sum_{\ell\equiv\mu\ (2)}
q_\tau^{\ell^2/4}e^{2\pi i\ell z}\), for \(\mu=0,1\).
Writing \(\phi_{0,1}=h_0\theta_{1,0}+h_1\theta_{1,1}\) gives
\([q_\tau^{n-\ell^2/4}]h_{\ell\bmod2}=f(n,\ell)\).
The vector \(h\) has weight \(-1/2\) and Weil representation
\(\rho_M=\rho_L^\vee\). Indeed the nontrivial theta component
has eigenvalue \(i\) under \(T:\tau\mapsto\tau+1\),
so its coefficient has eigenvalue
\(-i\). In particular \([q_\tau^0]h_0=10\).

For a positive plane \(P\) in \(M\), decompose a lattice vector
as \(\lambda_++\lambda_-\), with \(\lambda_+\in P\) and
\(\lambda_-\in P^\perp\). The
kernel and additive lift are
\begin{align*}
 \Theta_M(\tau,P)&=\sum_{\gamma\in M^*/M}e_\gamma
 \sum_{\lambda\in M+\gamma}
 e^{\pi i\tau\lambda_+^2+\pi i\overline\tau\lambda_-^2},\\
 \Phi_M(P,h)&=\int^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\SL_2(\Z)\backslash\Hpl}
 v\sum_\gamma h_\gamma(\tau)
 \overline{\Theta_{M,\gamma}(\tau,P)}\,\frac{du\,dv}{v^2}.
\end{align*}
Here \(\tau=u+iv\), squares use the form of \(M\), and
\(e_\gamma\) is the standard basis of the group algebra \(\C[M^*/M]\).
The Gaussian has absolute value
\(e^{-\pi v(\lambda_+^2-\lambda_-^2)}\), which decays.

The Hermitian pairing has weight \((1,1)\). Multiplication by
\(v\) makes the integrand invariant. Regularization means the
constant term of the meromorphic continuation obtained by inserting
\(v^{-s}\) in the truncated integral, as specified in
\S\ref{ssec:singular-theta}.

Borcherds's theorem constructs a multiplicative form \(\Psi_M\)
from this additive function. A homogeneous null coordinate
\(w=X_M+iY_M\) satisfies \((w,w)_M=0\) and
\((w,\overline w)_M>0\). Its oriented plane is
\(P(w)=\operatorname{span}_{\R}(X_M,Y_M)\).
With \(k=[q_\tau^0]h_0/2=5\) and
\(C_B=\Gamma'(1)/2+\log\sqrt{2\pi}\), the precise relation is
\[
 \Phi_M=-4\log\|\Psi_M\|-4kC_B,\qquad
 \|\Psi_M\|=|\Psi_M(w)|\,|Y_M|^k,\qquad
 |Y_M|=\sqrt{(Y_M,Y_M)_M}.
\]

The theorem applies to weight \(-1/2\) inputs of type \(\rho_M\)
with integral coefficients at nonpositive indices
\cite[Theorem 13.3]{B}. Its general output weight varies with the
input. For this chosen seed, the primitive-cusp product with leading
coefficient \(+1\) is \(D_5\).

Choose the ordered basis \((f_1,f_2,f_3,f_{-2},f_{-1})\) of
\(L\), with nonzero pairings
\((f_1,f_{-1})_L=(f_2,f_{-2})_L=-1\) and \(f_3^2=2\).
In this basis the cusp frame is
\(w=(z_2^2-z_1z_3,z_3,z_2,z_1,1)\). Substitution gives \(w^2=0\)
and \(|Y_M|^2=2\det(\Imag Z)\). Therefore
\[
 \Phi_M(P,h)=-4\log\!\left(
 |\Delta_5(Z)|[2\det(\Imag Z)]^{5/2}\right)
 +4\log64-20C_B.
\]
The norm determines no phase.

The positive leading coefficient
\(64\) fixes the theta normalization separately. Thus the
multiplicative lift, denoted
\(\Theta_{\mathrm{sing}}^{\mathrm{Bor}}(h)=64D_5\), is
\(\Delta_5\), and its denominator is
\(64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\). These are the automorphic,
denominator, and theta descriptions of one form.

\section{Geometric coefficients and the normalized half-index}
\label{op:sec:half-index}

The seed already has a geometric coefficient theory.
For a smooth projective complex variety $Y$ of dimension $d$, put
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:elliptic-coefficient}
 \mathcal E_{q,r}(Y)=r^{-d/2}\bigotimes_{j\ge1}
 \left(
 \Lambda_{-rq^{j-1}}T_Y^*\otimes
 \Lambda_{-r^{-1}q^j}T_Y\otimes
 \operatorname{Sym}_{q^j}T_Y^*\otimes
 \operatorname{Sym}_{q^j}T_Y
 \right).
\end{equation}
Here $\Lambda_tV=\sum_i t^i\Lambda^iV$ and
$\operatorname{Sym}_tV=\sum_i t^i\operatorname{Sym}^iV$.
An exterior power $\Lambda^iV$ has internal parity $i$ modulo two.
The minus signs record this parity in the displayed class.
Thus each coefficient is an actual finite direct sum of vector
bundles with parity, rather than only a virtual rank.

Apply derived global sections coefficient by coefficient.
The resulting complex has total parity given by internal parity
plus cohomological degree. Its signed Euler characteristic is
\[
 \operatorname{Ell}(Y;q,r)
   =\chi\bigl(Y,\mathcal E_{q,r}(Y)\bigr).
\]
Completion is in nonnegative powers of $q$.
At fixed $q$-degree the $r$-support is finite, and projectivity
makes every coefficient cohomology finite-dimensional.
This fixes both the carrier and the meaning of its index.

For a surface $Y$, let $Y^{[N]}$ be the Hilbert scheme of
length-$N$ zero-dimensional subschemes and define
\[
 F_Y(s,q,r)=\sum_{N\ge0}s^N
              \operatorname{Ell}(Y^{[N]};q,r).
\]
The coefficient complexes are
$R\Gamma(Y^{[N]},\mathcal E_{q,r}(Y^{[N]}))$.

The Hilbert-scheme elliptic-genus theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:Hilbert-product}
 F_Y=\prod_{a>0,\,b\ge0,\,\ell\in\Z}
          (1-s^aq^br^\ell)^{-c_Y(ab,\ell)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Ell}(Y;q,r)=\sum_{b,\ell}c_Y(b,\ell)q^br^\ell.
\end{equation}
This is the numerical McKay comparison for the crepant
Hilbert--Chow resolution.\footnote{L.~Borisov and A.~Libgober,
\emph{McKay correspondence for elliptic genera},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206241}{arXiv:math/0206241},
Corollary~6.7. The coefficient bundle convention is their
equation~(3). The hypotheses here are smoothness and projectivity.}
It does not identify the extension or chiral operations on
the coefficient complexes.

Separate the factors with zero $s$-degree by setting
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:boundary-factor}
 b(q,r)=q^{1/2}r^{1/2}(1-r^{-1})
       \prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^j)^{10}(1-rq^j)(1-r^{-1}q^j),
 \qquad \mathcal B(q,r)=b(q,r)^2.
\end{equation}
The square is
\[
 \mathcal B=q(r+r^{-1}-2)
       \prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^j)^{20}(1-rq^j)^2(1-r^{-1}q^j)^2.
\]
For the monic form $D_5=\Delta_5/64$ and
$\Xi=D_5^2=\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}$, cancellation gives
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:K3-index}
 F_S=\frac{s\mathcal B}{\Xi}
     =\prod_{a>0,b\ge0,\ell\in\Z}
           (1-s^aq^br^\ell)^{-2f(ab,\ell)}.
\end{equation}
The numerator is part of the normalization.
It removes the boundary pole and restores the length-zero
coefficient $1$. The cancelled expression lies in
$\Z[r^{\pm1}][[s,q]]$.

\begin{theorem}[The Enriques half-index]\label{op:thm:Enriques}
Let $T$ be a smooth projective complex Enriques surface.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:Enriques-index}
 F_T=\frac{s^{1/2}b}{D_5}
     =\prod_{a>0,b\ge0,\ell\in\Z}
           (1-s^aq^br^\ell)^{-f(ab,\ell)},
 \qquad F_T^2=F_S.
\end{equation}
These are identities of normalized elliptic-index series.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The canonical cover $\pi:\widetilde S\to T$ is an
\'{e}tale K3 double cover, so $T_{\widetilde S}=\pi^*T_T$.\footnote{
I.~Dolgachev, \emph{A brief introduction to Enriques surfaces},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7744v2}{arXiv:1412.7744v2},
Section~3, pp.~7--8. Only the complex case is used.}
The elliptic characteristic class therefore pulls back,
and integration over the cover multiplies it by two.
Thus $\operatorname{Ell}(T)=\phi_{0,1}$.

Equation~\eqref{op:eq:Hilbert-product} gives the product.
Separating its zero-$s$ factors in $D_5$ gives the quotient,
and squaring doubles every exponent.
\end{proof}

The surface $T$ is a separate geometric input.
No Enriques involution is required on the originally chosen $S$.
The equality $F_T^2=F_S$ compares indices of independently
defined complexes. It supplies no equivalence between their
states or their products.

The canonical line $K_T$ defines a character on the surface $T$.
The automorphic character belongs to a line over Siegel moduli.
A geometric square-root comparison must relate these bases and lines.

The first nontrivial length calculation makes the distinction
visible. At $q=0$, the Enriques seed has coefficients
$r^{-1}+10+r$. The length-two term comes from two length-one
factors or one length-two factor, giving
\begin{align}
 [s^2q^0]F_T&=r^2+11r+66+11r^{-1}+r^{-2},\notag\\
 [s^2q^0]F_S&=3r^2+42r+234+42r^{-1}+3r^{-2}.
 \label{op:eq:length-two}
\end{align}
For example the constant coefficient of the first line is
$\binom{11}{2}+1+10=66$.
The summands count two weight-zero length-one contributions,
the opposite-weight pair, and the weight-zero length-two term.

Only after cancelling the boundary factors may one set $r=1$.
All positive-energy exterior and symmetric factors in
\eqref{op:eq:elliptic-coefficient} then cancel.
The remaining index is the topological Euler characteristic.
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 F_T(s,q,1)&=\prod_{a\ge1}(1-s^a)^{-12}
            =1+12s+90s^2+520s^3+\cdots,\\
 F_S(s,q,1)&=\prod_{a\ge1}(1-s^a)^{-24}
            =1+24s+324s^2+3200s^3+\cdots.
\end{align*}
The raw quotients in \eqref{op:eq:K3-index} and
\eqref{op:eq:Enriques-index} have vanishing numerator and
denominator at $r=1$. Their cancelled series specify the value.

\subsection{A coefficient before taking its index}

The two parities can be calculated directly at positive energy.
Write $\Omega_T=T_T^*$ and let $K_T=\Lambda^2\Omega_T$.
Since $K_T^{\otimes2}\simeq\mathcal O_T$, the $qr$ coefficient
of \eqref{op:eq:elliptic-coefficient} is the internally even bundle
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:Enriques-bundle}
 B_{11}=\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega_T\oplus K_T
                              \oplus\Omega_T\oplus T_T.
\end{equation}
Indeed the expression through $q$-degree one is
\[
 r^{-1}(1-r\Omega_T+r^2K_T)
 \bigl(1+q((1-r)\Omega_T+(1-r^{-1})T_T)\bigr)+O(q^2).
\]
Extracting $qr$ gives
$\Omega_T^{\otimes2}+K_T\otimes\Omega_T+K_T\otimes T_T$.
The rank-two identities
$K_T\otimes T_T\simeq\Omega_T$ and
$K_T\otimes\Omega_T\simeq T_T$ give
\eqref{op:eq:Enriques-bundle}.

On an Enriques surface, $c_2(T)=12$,
$\chi(\mathcal O_T)=1$, and $c_1(T)=0$ rationally.
Its Hodge numbers give $h^1(\Omega_T)=10$.

The K3 cover has no nonzero holomorphic symmetric cotangent
tensors of positive degree.\footnote{S.~Kobayashi,
\emph{The first Chern class and holomorphic symmetric tensor fields}.
See Corollary~8 in
\href{https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/32/2/32_2_325/_pdf}
{J.~Math. Soc. Japan \textbf{32} (1980), 325--329}.}

Pullback and Serre duality therefore make $H^0$ and $H^2$
vanish for $T_T$, $\Omega_T$, and $\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega_T$.

For the last bundle, Riemann--Roch gives

 \operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega_T)=3-4c_2(T),
 \qquad
 \chi(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega_T)=3-48=-45.
\]
The same formula for $T_T$ gives $\chi(T_T)=-10$.
The canonical line has cohomology dimensions $(0,0,1)$.

Adding the four summands yields

 (h^0,h^1,h^2)(T,B_{11})=(0,65,1),
 \qquad \operatorname{sdim}H^*(T,B_{11})=1-65=-64.
\]
Thus the actual coefficient has parity dimensions $(1|65)$.

\section{Reduced counting and the scalar normalization}
\label{sec:level-n-scalar-shadow}

The Hilbert-scheme count in the opening can be compared with maps
and sheaves. A stable pair on \(X\) is a map
\(\mathcal O_X\to F\), with \(F\) pure of dimension one and
zero-dimensional cokernel. The reduced invariant \(\PT_{n,(\beta,d)}\)
is the corresponding Behrend-weighted quotient count by \(E\).
For nonzero K3 class, Oberdieck proves
\[
 \sum_n\DT_{n,(\beta,d)}y^n
 =\sum_n\PT_{n,(\beta,d)}y^n.
\]
There is no degree-zero correction factor: \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)
=\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(E)=24\cdot0=0\)
\cite[\S0.3, Theorem 3(i)]{OberdieckReducedPT}.

For maps, let \(\overline M^{\bullet}_{g,1}(X,(\beta_h,d))\)
be the moduli of possibly disconnected stable maps, with no
contracted connected components, one marked point, and arithmetic
genus \(g\). Choose \(\beta_h^\vee\in H^2(S,\Q)\) with
\(\langle\beta_h,\beta_h^\vee\rangle=1\). If \(\pi_S,\pi_E\)
are the projections, the reduced Gromov--Witten invariant is
\[
 N_{g,h,d}=\int_{[\overline M^{\bullet}_{g,1}(X,(\beta_h,d))]^{\mathrm{red}}}
 \operatorname{ev}_1^*(\pi_S^*\beta_h^\vee\cup\pi_E^*[0_E]).
\]
The insertion removes the translation orbit, and the value is
independent of the dual class. Primitive deformation invariance
makes it depend on \(h\), rather than the chosen primitive class.

A nonzero disconnected contribution has one component nontrivial
over \(S\). Its other components have genus one. Thus its arithmetic
genus is nonnegative \cite[\S1.2]{OPand}.

Write
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{PT}}^{\mathrm{rat}}(y,q,s)
 =\sum_{h,d\geq0}R_{h,d}(y)q^{h-1}s^{d-1}.
\]
The GW/PT comparison of Oberdieck--Pandharipande gives
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{GW}}(u,q,s):=\sum_{g,h,d\geq0}
 N_{g,h,d}u^{2g-2}q^{h-1}s^{d-1}
 =Z_{\mathrm{PT}}^{\mathrm{rat}}(-e^{iu},q,s),
\]
where the right side means its Laurent expansion at \(u=0\)
\cite[Proposition 5]{OPand}. Oberdieck--Pixton prove
\(Z_{\mathrm{GW}}=-1/\chi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}(r,q,s)\) under
\(r=e^{iu}\) \cite[Theorem 1]{OB}. Their theorem proves the
primitive Igusa formula conjectured in \cite{OPand}.

Combining the comparisons gives precisely
\[
 Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}=Z_{\mathrm{PT}}^{\mathrm{rat}}(-r,q,s)
 =-4096\Delta_5^{-2},\qquad
 Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{BPS}}=\Delta_{10}^{-1}
 =-\tfrac1{4096}Z^X_{\mathrm{OP}}.
\]

These are identities of coefficientwise rational functions before
choosing Laurent expansions. The DT/PT expansion is at \(r=0\).
The chosen product chamber instead gives the expansion at
\(r=\infty\), and the GW substitution gives the expansion at
\(u=0\). For example the coefficient of \(q^{-1}s^{-1}\) is
\[
 -\frac{r}{(1-r)^2}
 =\begin{cases}
 -\sum_{n\geq1}n r^n,& |r|<1,\\
 -\sum_{n\geq1}n r^{-n},& |r|>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus the two Laurent series have the same rational representative.

Under the GW substitution,
\(r-2+r^{-1}=2\cos u-2=-u^2+O(u^4)\), so
\(-1/(r-2+r^{-1})=u^{-2}+O(1)\), consistent with genus zero.
The sign \(y=-r\) is independent of the orientation character.
The formula stated here does not include imprimitive K3 classes.

\section{Five distinct numerical invariants}

The counting and algebraic constructions use several Euler
characteristics and ranks. The group $\Gamma_{\mathrm{ind}}=\Z^3$
of triples $(n,\ell,m)$ is called the \emph{Gram-index lattice}.
Its exponent map $\alpha$ identifies it with $\La^{2,1}_{II}$.
Write $r_{\mathrm{Gram}}=3$ for its rank.

The lattice
$\widetilde H(S,\Z)=H^0(S,\Z)\oplus H^2(S,\Z)\oplus H^4(S,\Z)$,
with pairing
$(r,c,a)\cdot(r',c',a')=c\cdot c'-ra'-r'a$,
is called the \emph{Mukai lattice}.
Its algebraic sublattice replaces $H^2(S,\Z)$ by $\NS(S)$.
Here $\NS(S)$ is the image of the first Chern class of line
bundles in integral cohomology.
The five numerical values are
\[
 (\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},\kappa^{\mathrm{Hodge}}_{\mathrm{ch}},
 r_{\mathrm{Gram}},\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
 \kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}})=(0,0,3,5,24).
\]
The first is \(\chi(\mathcal O_X)=2\cdot0=0\).
The Hodge numbers \(h^{0,q}(X)\), for \(q=0,1,2,3\), are
\((1,1,1,1)\), giving the second invariant \(1-1+1-1=0\).

The third counts the three Gram coordinates of the type-II
Heisenberg--Cartan algebra. It is not the rank of the algebraic
Mukai lattice \(H^0(S,\Z)\oplus\NS(S)\oplus H^4(S,\Z)\),
which has rank five when \(\NS(S)\simeq U\oplus\langle-2\rangle\).
The fourth is the Borcherds weight \(f(0,0)/2=10/2=5\).
The last is \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)=1+22+1=24\).

A genus-one Heisenberg bar coefficient is a different quantity.
Its definition requires the chosen bar complex, a trace functional,
and a normalized genus-one class. Denote this coefficient by
\(\kappa^{\mathrm{Heis}}_{\mathrm{ch}}\) when these data are fixed.
The comparison \(\kappa^{\mathrm{Heis}}_{\mathrm{ch}}=r_{\mathrm{Gram}}=3\)
requires a map identifying that normalized coefficient with the
Gram rank. Rescaling the trace rescales the coefficient and leaves
the rank unchanged. The five-entry tuple with
\(\kappa^{\mathrm{Heis}}_{\mathrm{ch}}\) in its third position
therefore assumes this additional comparison.


In particular
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=
\kappa^{\mathrm{Hodge}}_{\mathrm{ch}}+\chi(\mathcal O_S)\)
would give \(5=0+2\), which is false. The values
\(\chi(\mathcal O_S)=2\) and \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)=24\)
are different invariants.

For any admissible Borcherds input
\(\Phi_N\), the weight formula instead reads
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2\), with \(c_N(0)\)
the zero-coset constant coefficient of that input.
The \((t,N;k)=(1,1;5)\) row of the Cl\'ery--Gritsenko catalog
uses \(\phi_{0,1}\) \cite{GC,GN}. The Fake-Monster product
\(\Phi_{12}\) uses constant coefficient \(24\), giving weight
\(12\), with its denominator on the Leech-extension lattice
\(\mathrm{II}_{25,1}\) \cite{BorcherdsGKM88,Borcherds95}.
The two weights belong to different inputs and lattices.

For the cyclic Chaudhuri--Hockney--Lykken (CHL) orbifold inputs with
orders \(N=1,2,3,4,6\), the eta-product cycle shapes are
\(1^{24},1^82^8,1^63^6,1^42^24^4,1^22^23^26^2\).
Their eta weights are \(12,8,6,5,4\). Subtracting two gives the
squared-product weights \(10,6,4,3,2\), hence square-root weights
\(5,3,2,3/2,1\) \cite[Table 1, \S3]{GK10Composite}.

These rows have different orbifold inputs. Even a comparison with
the unchanged number \(0+2\) would have equality at \(N=3\).
The counterexample
at \(N=1\) already disproves a universal identification of the
Borcherds weight with the Hodge and holomorphic Euler invariants.


\section{Charges and extension operations}
\label{op:sec:operations}

The root indices must be compared with geometric charges before
extension operations can be transported.
For two Mukai vectors $(Q,P)$, the quadratic function
\[
 \Pi_X(Q,P)=\bigl((Q,Q)/2,(Q,P),(P,P)/2\bigr)
\]
is called the \emph{Gram map}. Sheaf charges lie in the
algebraic Mukai sublattice. The following calculation also
applies to the full lattice.

The first coordinate of \(\Pi_X(Q+Q',P+P')\) differs from the
sum of the first coordinates by \((Q,Q')\). The middle differs
by \((Q,P')+(Q',P)\), and the last by \((P,P')\).
Thus the Gram map is quadratic, rather than additive.

Its polarization does give an additive refinement.
Let $C=\widetilde H(S,\Z)\oplus\widetilde H(S,\Z)$ and set
\[
 B(c,c')=\Pi_X(c+c')-\Pi_X(c)-\Pi_X(c').
\]
On $C\oplus\Z^3$ define
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:polarized-charge}
 (c,\lambda)\star(c',\lambda')
       =(c+c',\lambda+\lambda'+B(c,c')).
\end{equation}
The map $(c,\lambda)\mapsto(c,\lambda-\Pi_X(c))$
identifies this law with componentwise addition.
In particular it is associative and commutative.

Its group-homomorphic sections over $C$ are
$c\mapsto(c,\Pi_X(c)+L(c))$ for additive maps $L:C\to\Z^3$.
This constructs the charge-group refinement.
A Hall comparison must still transport the coefficient complexes
and their extension maps through that refinement.

There is a second geometric issue before choosing coefficients.
Write $\mathcal M_\alpha$ for translation-invariant moduli stacks
of coherent sheaves on $X=S\times E$ in an extension-closed class.
Their charges add in extensions.

Let $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\alpha,\beta}$ parametrize
short exact sequences with endpoint charges $\alpha,\beta$.
Set
\[
 \mathcal Q_\alpha=[\mathcal M_\alpha/E],\qquad
 \mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}
     =[\mathcal H^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\alpha,\beta}/E].
\]
The quotient uses simultaneous translation of the whole sequence.
Every quotient has a map to the classifying stack $BE$ of
principal $E$-bundles.

\begin{proposition}\label{op:prop:common-torsor}
The reduced extension correspondence has endpoints over $BE$:
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:Hall-span}
 \mathcal Q_\alpha\times_{BE}\mathcal Q_\beta
       \longleftarrow\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}
       \longrightarrow\mathcal Q_{\alpha+\beta}.
\end{equation}
Its left-hand space is canonically
$[(\mathcal M_\alpha\times\mathcal M_\beta)/E_{\mathrm{diag}}]$.
Iterated correspondences are the corresponding flag stacks on
one common torsor. The zero object gives the relative unit $BE$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Over a test scheme, an object in the fiber product is a pair of
$E$-torsors, equivariant families, and an isomorphism of torsors.
Transport across that isomorphism places both families on one torsor.
Repeating a single torsor gives the inverse construction, on both
objects and arrows. An extension on this torsor gives the span.

Either bracketing of three extensions gives a three-step flag.
Taking preimages and quotients recovers the intermediate objects,
and further flags give all refinement compatibilities.
The zero sheaf has every $E$-translation as a stabilizer, so its
quotient is $BE$.
\end{proof}

Quotienting the endpoints independently would replace the fiber
product over $BE$ by a product over a point and discard this
torsor identification. To define Hall operations from
\eqref{op:eq:Hall-span}, choose a coefficient theory with pullback
and pushforward. Their composites require orientation and support
conditions on the correspondence.
Coherent complexes and vanishing-cycle sheaves have different
such operations. The correspondence alone does not identify them.

\subsection{A reduced coefficient as a matrix element}

Reduction can change which functional on an algebra represents
the counting invariant. This has an elementary model.
Let $H$ and $R$ be unital complex algebras and suppose
\[
 I:H\longrightarrow R[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2),\qquad
 I(a)=I_0(a)+\epsilon I_1(a)
\]
is a unital algebra homomorphism, with $\epsilon$ central.
Comparing its two coefficients gives
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:reduced-derivation}
 I_1(ab)=I_0(a)I_1(b)+I_1(a)I_0(b),\qquad I_1(1)=0,
\end{equation}
while $I_0$ is unital and multiplicative.
The reduced coefficient $I_1$ therefore need not be a character.

Nevertheless it is a matrix element of a unital action:
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:reduced-module}
 \rho(a)=\begin{pmatrix}I_0(a)&I_1(a)\\0&I_0(a)\end{pmatrix}
       \quad\text{on the right $R$-module }R\oplus R.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{op:eq:reduced-derivation} is precisely the
off-diagonal equation in $\rho(ab)=\rho(a)\rho(b)$.
The first summand is invariant, and the quotient is another copy
of the module defined by $I_0$.
The coefficient from the second summand to the first is $I_1$,
including its vanishing on the unit.
This model distinguishes a state action from an algebra character.
To use it in reduced curve counting requires an independently
constructed geometric map $I$ and a comparison with the actual state.

\section{Collision coefficients before global integration}
\label{op:sec:collision-coefficients}

The coefficient must retain how supports meet.
The displacement parameter in the following model belongs to a
spectral elliptic curve. It is distinct from the geometric factor
$E$ of $S\times E$ and from the Jacobi parameter curve.
An identification of those roles requires a map between the
corresponding families.

In a formal coordinate near zero displacement, the three equations are
\[
 x(x+h)=0,\qquad y(y+h)=0,\qquad xy=0.
\]
For $h\ne0$ their solutions are $(0,0)$, $(-h,0)$ and $(0,-h)$.
At $h=0$ the three points meet.
The coherent derived zero locus retains the equations through
\[
 K_h=\C[h,x,y]\otimes\Lambda(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3),
 \qquad
 (d\epsilon_1,d\epsilon_2,d\epsilon_3)
       =(x(x+h),y(y+h),xy),\qquad |\epsilon_i|=-1.
\]
Its negative cohomology records relations among the equations.

Adding dual multiplier coordinates gives a different construction:
\begin{equation}\label{op:eq:critical-triangle}
 W_h=Px(x+h)+Qy(y+h)+Txy.
\end{equation}
Its critical equations include derivatives in $P,Q,T$.
The critical completion, its constructible coefficients and its
matrix factorizations are developed in
Chapters~\ref{vc:chap:critical} and~\ref{mc:chap:categories}.
The coherent and constructible comparisons specify different
recipients, gradings and support functors.

One composition already explains why the full coefficient matters.
A matrix factorization of $W_h$ is a parity-graded free module
with an odd operator $D$ satisfying $D^2=W_h\operatorname{id}$.

For $R=\C[h,P,Q,T]$, take the two branch factorizations
$D_0,D_1$ in \eqref{mc:tcrm:eq:objects}.
Their morphism complexes have odd closed maps
$f:D_0\to D_1$ and $g:D_1\to D_0$ represented by a Clifford
generator $\gamma_1$ with $\gamma_1^2=-P$.
Their return is
\[
 [g f]=-P[1_{D_0}]\ne0.
\]
It is killed by $h$, as proved in
Proposition~\ref{mc:tcrm:prop:composites}.
Inverting $h$ contracts the off-diagonal morphisms and loses this
return. A comparison made only after that localization therefore
does not construct the comparison at the collision.

The central critical coefficient also has a nonzero monodromy
summand with zero global cohomology, by
Theorem~\ref{vc:cqm:thm:coefficient} and
Proposition~\ref{vc:cqm:prop:integral}.
Its absence from the global periodic invariant does not make it
the zero sheaf. Thus the coefficient functor and its operations
must be constructed before taking global sections or an index.

\section{The compact Pfaffian realization}
\label{sec:edge-2-3}

A compact construction must produce a square-root line and its
section before comparing it with \(\Delta_5\). The target
\(X=S\times E\) has a holomorphic volume form after one is chosen
on each factor. The construction requires reduced self-Ext complexes
on its moduli of objects, describing infinitesimal deformations and
obstructions. Their determinant lines are candidates for the square
of a Pfaffian line. The reduced complexes, orientations, sections,
and their gluing are the geometric data to be constructed.

A morphism from the obstruction sheaf to the structure sheaf
is called a \emph{cosection}. Its kernel alone does not define a
reduced perfect obstruction theory. The reduction here requires
the actual perfect complex and compatible maps on extension families.

An \(E_3\)-prefactorization datum assigns complexes to open sets
and compatible operations for disjoint insertions, with the
three-dimensional little-disks operations where specified.
The notation
\(\mathfrak A_X\in E_3\text{-}\mathrm{HolFA}(X)\)
means a chosen such holomorphic datum. A Calabi--Yau category alone
is not an asserted construction of this datum
\cite{KontsevichOperadsMotives,TamarkinLittleDisks,
LambrechtsVolicLittleDisks,CostelloGwilliam1,CostelloGwilliam2}.

Choose a stability condition with Harder--Narasimhan filtrations:
finite filtrations whose semistable quotients have strictly decreasing
phases. Let \(R\) select a finite range of their types.
The construction needs a hybrid configuration carrier on \(E\).
It contains ordinary finite configurations and the wrapped strata
for objects supported over the whole elliptic factor.

Charges lie in a lattice \(\Gamma_{X,R}\) with quadratic Gram
map \(\Pi_X\). Vanishing-cycle sheaves \(\Phi_R\) record the
local critical geometry, and an orientation \(o_R\) is needed to
glue them. Hall correspondences \(H_R\) encode extensions and their
composition. The source coalgebra \(C_{X,R}\) and its Koszul cobar
\(\Theta_{\mathrm{Kos},R}\) retain the differentials and higher
operations needed to compare that source with the denominator.
The primitive part after Gram descent is \(P_R^\Pi\).

These objects, a Pfaffian line \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Pf},R}\)
and section \(\mathrm{pf}_{X,R}\), its orientation character
\(\epsilon_{o,R}\), and a recognition map \(\mathrm{Rec}_R\)
form compatible finite data. Their inverse limit is
\[
 \mathfrak D_X^{DI}=\varprojlim_R
 (\mathcal A^{E_3}_{X,R},F^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{X,R},\Gamma_{X,R},
 \Pi_X,\Phi_R,o_R,H_R,P_R^\Pi,C_{X,R},\Theta_{\mathrm{Kos},R},
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{Pf},R},\mathrm{pf}_{X,R},
 \epsilon_{o,R},\mathrm{Rec}_R).
\]
Every entry requires compatible transition maps. This inverse
system specifies the target of the construction.
The compact geometric entries remain to be constructed.

The orientation hypotheses have separate roles. Condition \((D0)\)
requires an orientation through the degeneration, agreeing with the
generic reduced orientation. Condition \((O1)\) requires a square
root of the reduced determinant line on the quotient by \(E\),
extending over the cusp boundary. Condition \((O1)^+\) requires
Weyl-equivariant root lifts with a trivialized sign cocycle on
the actual action groupoid. A reduction to constant-group
cohomology \(H^2(W^{(2)};\mathbb F_2)\) is additional data.

Condition
\((O2_{\mathrm{atlas}})\) requires type-II wall charts with a
rank-one normal Pfaffian factor, a section-compatible root lift
and reflection-invariant units. The reflection changes the normal
coordinate's sign, and the chosen lift has residual character
\(-1\) on each simple reflection. The formal set of twelve signs
has \(2^{12}=4096\) elements; a geometric orbit of that size needs
its own bijection. The equality with \(64^2\) is numerical.

The finite geometric Pfaffian datum \((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\) further
requires the following data at each finite stage, on the reduced
compact quotient \(\mathfrak Q_R\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reduced perfect self-Ext complexes \(K_R\) with odd skew
self-duality \(K_R\simeq K_R^\vee[1]\), compatible with reduction
and the elliptic quotient.
\item Orientations and Pfaffian lines with specified isomorphisms
\(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Pf},R}^{\otimes2}\simeq\mathcal L_{\det,R}\).
\item Finite skew blocks and Pfaffian sections, with the prescribed
active support, signed exponents, and full even and odd dimensions.
\item Type-II wall factorizations
\(\mathrm{pf}_R=\upsilon_Ru_R\mathrm{pf}^{\mathrm{tan}}_R\),
with \(\upsilon_R\) an invariant unit and normal divisor order one.
\item Line comparisons with \(\mathcal L^5\otimes\nu_{\Delta_5}\),
compatible with determinant squares and cusp frames, with leading
coefficient \(64\).
\item Strict section-preserving transitions with closed images and
vanishing \(\lim^1\) for the line and section systems.
\end{enumerate}
These hypotheses retain the finite geometric complexes and maps.
Equality of scalar products supplies none of these data.

The orientation character acts on lattice reflections. To compare
it with automorphy, extend the Maass character to the homogeneous
null cone over \(\mathcal H_2\). At the cusp frame \(w(Z)\), put
\[
 \widehat\Delta_5(tw(Z))=t^{-5}\Delta_5(Z),\qquad t\in\C^\times.
\]

Write \(\sigma:\Sp_4(\Z)\to\Gamma_{3,2}\) for the exterior-square
homomorphism. Its frame relation
\(\sigma(g)w(Z)=\det(CZ+D)w(gZ)\) gives
\(\widehat\Delta_5(\sigma(g)w)=
\nu_{\Delta_5}(g)\widehat\Delta_5(w)\).
The character descends through the kernel \(\{\pm\Id_4\}\),
where it is trivial. Since \(\Gamma_{3,2}\subset\mathrm{SO}(L)\)
and \(\det(-\Id_L)=-1\), it has a unique extension
\[
 \widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}:
 \Gamma_{3,2}\times\{\pm\Id_L\}\longrightarrow\{\pm1\},
 \qquad \widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}(-\Id_L)=(-1)^5=-1.
\]
The central value is forced by the homogeneous degree.

Extend each \(s_{\delta_i}\) by the identity on \(f_{\pm1}\).
Choose \(g_i=\operatorname{diag}(V_i,V_i^{-t})\), where
\[
 V_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V_3=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These matrices are symplectic, with \(\sigma(g_i)=-s_{\delta_i}\) and
\(\det(V_i^{-t})=-1\). Maass's formula gives
\(\nu_{\Delta_5}(g_i)=1\) \cite[(1.5)]{GN}.
Thus \(\Delta_5(s_{\delta_i}Z)=-\Delta_5(Z)\), while
\(\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}(s_{\delta_i})=-1\).
The extended character restricts to the determinant on
\(W^{(2)}(\La^{2,1}_{II})\), since the three reflections generate it.
These are the linear reflection signs of
\cite[Propositions 2.1--2.2]{GN}.

\begin{theorem}[Pfaffian--Dirac]
\label{thm:pentadic-pfaffian-dirac}
Assume \((D0)\), \((O1)\), \((O1)^+\),
\((O2_{\mathrm{atlas}})\), and \((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\) on the
cosection-reduced critical charts of \(K3\times E\), with the
compatible inverse system just described. Then
\[
 \mathrm{Pf}_{\mathrm{prot}}(\mathfrak D_X^{DI})=\Delta_5,
 \qquad \epsilon_o=\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}
       \quad\hbox{on }W^{(2)}(\La^{2,1}_{II}).
\]
The comparison uses the specified automorphic line maps and cusp frames.
\end{theorem}

The orientation comparison checks the three generators:
\(\epsilon_o(s_{\delta_i})=-1=
\widetilde\nu_{\Delta_5}(s_{\delta_i})\).
The section comparison
uses the finite Fourier data and strict transitions in
\((P_{\mathrm{fin}})\). The detailed recognition statement is
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch6-pfaffian-dirac}. Construction of its compact
hypotheses remains a geometric problem.

\begin{remark}[Primitive recognition]
\label{rem:conditioning-named}
The protected primitive Lie superalgebra is
\[
 \mathcal P_X=P_X^{\Pi,\mathrm{red}}=P_X
 :=H^0\Prim_{\mathrm{prot}}(\overline\Pi^\Theta_{X,*}\mathcal F_X)
 /\overline{\operatorname{Rad}\langle\,,\rangle_X}.
\]
Here \(\mathcal F_X\) is the hybrid factorization sheaf,
\(\overline\Pi^\Theta_{X,*}\) is its completed Gram pushforward,
and the closed radical is the part annihilated by its Hall pairing.
The assertion \(\mathcal P_X\cong\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\)
requires the source representatives, relations, pairings, radical
quotient, Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt comparison, and full parity
dimensions of \((S1)\)--\((S10)\) on cofinal relation-closed
windows. The finite Koszul comparisons are additional chain-level
requirements. They are distinct from Pfaffian recognition and are
not implied by equality of signed exponents.
\end{remark}

\section{Mirror periods and geometric comparison objects}
\label{sec:view-4-mirror}

The rank-seventeen lattice-polarized K3 family of
Chapter~\ref{ch:view4-mirror} supplies a classical Siegel period
model. The compact mirror problem asks for a separate
three-dimensional period construction \(\mathcal P_{K3\times E}^{(3)}\),
with a map to \(\mathcal H_2/\Sp_4(\Z)\), a geometric meromorphic
section and a comparison with the automorphic line identifying
that section with \(\Delta_5^{-2}\). The stronger assertion of a
period quotient requires its quotient map and an inverse period
comparison as well.
On a common period cover where the period map is normally
unramified, the predicted polar divisor is
\(2\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{mir}}\), with reduced support the type-II
Humbert--Heegner divisor. Its generic coarse reflection quotient
has order one instead. These base and line conditions belong to
Conjecture~\ref{conj:mirror-discriminant}; the compact comparison
is not used as a theorem hypothesis.

\begin{definition}[The five comparison objects]
\label{def:pentadic-delta5}
With the preceding domains and hypotheses, the five objects are
\[
 (\mathcal D_X,\operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}),
 \mathrm{Pf}_{\mathrm{prot}}(\mathfrak D_X^{DI}),
 \mathrm{Disc}(\mathcal P\to\mathbb D),
 \Theta_{\mathrm{sing}}^{\mathrm{Bor}}(h)).
\]
The automorphic section, denominator, and multiplicative theta lift
agree after the factor \(64\) and substitution \(Z\mapsto2Z\).
The Pfaffian agrees under all five geometric hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pentadic-pfaffian-dirac}. The inverse-square mirror
comparison is conjectural. The primitive-algebra comparison has the
separate hypotheses of Remark~\ref{rem:conditioning-named}.
\end{definition}

\section{Protected traces and the physical realization}
\label{sec:beilinson-chain-placement}

A physical interpretation must specify which states are counted
and how observables act on them. A signed partition function alone
specifies neither operation. If a boundary theory is described by
a factorization differential graded category \(\mathcal C\), a
chosen boundary vacuum \(b\) has an endomorphism algebra
\(A_b=\End_{\mathcal C}(b)\). Composition in \(\mathcal C\)
gives the boundary product. The bar construction records twisting
and coupling data.

Bulk observables are compared with the derived
chiral center \(Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_b)\), whose action
must respect boundary products and locality. A trace on that center
is a further operation.

The compact comparison requires a source coalgebra \(C_X\)
and a cobar comparison with the denominator chiral object
\(\mathsf{Den}_{\Delta_5,E}\).
It must preserve Weyl transport, orientation, Hall pairings,
radical quotients and the Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt filtration.
These are the chiral Koszul
source datum, finite Koszul comparison datum, and strict
Mittag--Leffler conditions of
Definitions~\ref{def:chiral-koszul-source-datum} and
\ref{def:finite-koszul-comparison-datum} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral-koszul-source-datum-consequence}.
They retain chain-level information that the denominator forgets.

For a protected trace, choose a chart algebra $A_b$ in a specified
completed chiral bar--cobar domain.
Its comparison with the coalgebra has the type
\[
 \operatorname{Bar}^{\mathrm{ch}}(A_b)\simeq C_X,
 \qquad\text{or equivalently in that domain}\qquad
 A_b\simeq\Omega^{\mathrm{ch}}C_X.
\]
The first map is a coalgebra map and the second is an algebra map.
Require their filtrations and completions to agree with the
finite inverse system.

The trace further requires concentration and stratification
comparisons on this actual domain, and a degree $-3$ cyclic pairing
\[
 Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_b)\otimes
 Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_b)\longrightarrow\C[-3].
\]
The completion must support the stated chiral-Koszul comparisons,
and the pairing must be compatible with the volume form,
Pfaffian line, and transitions. Finally its scalar normalization
must agree with the product and the reduced enumerative convention.

Let \(t_{h,d,n}\) be the normalized supertrace on the
\((h,d,n)\)-graded summand of the supplied protected complex.
The index \(n\) is Euler characteristic, while \(h\) and \(d\)
record the K3 square and elliptic degree as in the counting series.
Write the full graded trace as
\[
 \mathrm{Tr}^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{\mathrm{gr}}
 ((-1)^F\mathrm{id})
 :=\sum_{h,d\geq0}\sum_{n\in\Z}
 t_{h,d,n}r^nq^{h-1}s^{d-1},
\]
using the specified completion and the DT expansion at \(r=0\).
Here \(F\) denotes parity.

Suppose the specified concentration and stratification comparisons
identify this trace with the normalized reduced state.
Then the protected trace identity is
\[
 \mathrm{Tr}^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{\mathrm{gr}}
 ((-1)^F\mathrm{id})=\Delta_5^{-2},
\]
as an identity after coefficientwise rationalization in \(r\).
A trace at a fixed Euler degree \(n_0\) is the coefficient
\([r^{n_0}]\) in the \(r=0\) expansion, rather than this full
series. The OP scalar is \(-4096\) times the full graded trace.
Construction of this compact trace datum and construction of a
gravity-line algebra acting on boundary states remain distinct
problems.

\begin{remark}[Information retained by the comparisons]
\label{rem:beilinson-cut-delta5}
Choosing a vacuum, passing to cohomology, taking a radical quotient,
completing, and taking a trace each require their own maps. The
scalar identity does not reverse these operations.

The Pfaffian comparison retains a line and its character.
Primitive recognition retains brackets and parity.
The trace comparison retains a cyclic
pairing. A proposed gravitational realization must preserve all
operations used to compose and glue boundary observables.
\end{remark}

A formal local model illustrates the algebraic data without providing
compact gluing. Fix a worldline \(\R_t\times\{0_s\}\times\{p\}\) in
\(\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\times\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}\), with a
formal skyscraper boundary condition at \(p\) and multiplicity
space \(\C^N\). In the formal Darboux chart
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\C^2,p}=\C[[z_1,z_2]]\), the transverse
coordinates act by matrices
\(\phi_1,\phi_2\in\mathfrak{gl}_N\).

Work in the formal completion at \(\phi_1=\phi_2=0\), the
length-\(N\) skyscraper with trivial transverse action. Let
\(\mathrm{Kosz}([\phi_1,\phi_2])\) be the power-series algebra
in the matrix entries, tensored with odd generators \(\eta_{ij}\) in
degree \(-1\), with differential
\(d\eta_{ij}=[\phi_1,\phi_2]_{ij}\). It describes the completed derived
commuting locus. Infinitesimal simultaneous conjugation is imposed
by the continuous Chevalley--Eilenberg cochain complex
\[
 A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}
 =C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{gl}_N,
        \mathrm{Kosz}([\phi_1,\phi_2])).
\]

The ghosts have degree one, and the total differential combines the
Koszul differential with the Lie-algebra action and bracket.
This is a classical formal-local boundary model in the mixed
holomorphic--topological setting with local geometry
\(\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\times\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}\)
\cite{CostelloLiBCOV,CostelloLiOpenClosedBCOV}.
Its use as a compact chiral-Hochschild chart requires a comparison
map and descent, rather than only equality of local expressions.

The simplest gluing obstruction has a precise surface carrier.
For a holomorphic-symplectic surface \((Y,\omega)\) and a symplectic
vector field \(v\), the one-form \(\alpha_v=-\iota_v\omega\) is
closed. Choose local holomorphic functions \(h_i\) with
\(dh_i=\alpha_v\). Their differences on overlaps are locally
constant and define
\[
 \operatorname{per}_Y(v)=[h_i-h_j]\in H^1(Y,\C_Y).
\]
This class vanishes exactly when the primitives can be changed by
constants to glue to one holomorphic Hamiltonian. Equivalently it
is the connecting image for
\(0\to\C_Y\to\mathcal O_Y\xrightarrow{d}
\Omega^1_{Y,\mathrm{cl}}\to0\).

For the threefold \(X=S\times E\), the notation
\(\mathrm{ob}^{\mathrm{hol}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\) names the proposed
compact descent obstruction. Defining its target complex and the
map transporting the local surface Hamiltonians to it remains part
of the compact construction. The surface period class alone supplies
neither. A global theory must also establish locality,
anomaly cancellation, and the quantum master equation. A
three-dimensional gravitational path integral with internal factor
\(K3\times E\) requires both that global field theory and the
gravity-line algebra acting on boundary states. The enumerative
identity alone supplies neither.
