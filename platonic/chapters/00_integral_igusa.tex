\part{Denominators and geometric realizations}
\chapter{From an automorphic product to operations}
\label{comp:ch:entrance}

A protected index counts states with signs. Its coefficients do not
specify the states, their extension operations or the line in which an
operator determinant takes values. The Igusa product makes these
questions concrete: one automorphic function has a root-algebra
construction and a reduced curve-counting interpretation. A geometric
comparison must connect their objects and operations before evaluating
that function.

Use $Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&z\\z&\sigma\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb H_2$,
with $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$, $r=e^{2\pi iz}$ and $s=e^{2\pi i\sigma}$. Write
\[
 \phiweak(\tau,z)=\sum_{n\geq0,\,\ell\in\mathbb Z}
                     f(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell,
 \qquad f(0,0)=10,
\]
for the normalized weak Jacobi form of weight zero and index one.
The K3 elliptic genus is \(2\phiweak\). In the cusp convention
specified below, its multiplicative lift is
\[
 \Dmon(Z)=q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
       \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)},
 \qquad \Dtheta=64\Dmon,\qquad \ChiTen=\Dmon^2.
\]
The order on \((n,\ell,m)\), the theta phase and the coefficient
ring of this product will be fixed before its construction.
The monic section \(\Dmon\) has weight five and an order-two
modular character $\nu_5$.

The Gritsenko--Nikulin presentation supplies a Lie superalgebra
\(\gIg\), with positive half \(\nIg\), for which
\[
 \den(\gIg)=\Dmon(2Z).
\]
The product records signed root dimensions. The presentation supplies
both parity dimensions and the bracket. In particular, the first
interior root has dimensions \((29\mid93)\); its signed dimension
\(-64\) is not its ordinary dimension.

\section{Algebraic and analytic constructions}

A proper positive height makes the bounded root-degree pieces finite.
Complete by total root height, retaining the specified coproduct and
completed tensor product. The ordinary Chevalley chain complex of
\(\nIg\) then has Euler supercharacter, with Weyl vector $\rho$,
\[
 P=\prod_{\alpha>0}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\operatorname{sdim}\gIg_\alpha},
 \qquad e^{-\rho}P=\den(\gIg).
\]
Its enveloping algebra has PBW supercharacter \(P^{-1}\).
The ordinary bar--Chevalley comparison, the current envelope on a
curve and an associative envelope on Ran space have different
carriers; their maps and tensor products are specified separately.

\begin{definition}[Root constructions]
\label{comp:def:root-constructions}
Let \(\RealDatum\) denote the following collection associated with
the fixed minimal Igusa presentation: its positive height-complete
enveloping algebra and Chevalley coalgebra; its level-zero current
envelope; its quantized root datum with a specified Cartan and Borel
completion; and its diagonal Fredholm model on a declared root
Hilbert superspace and convergence domain. An operation-preserving
comparison specifies a map on every component it compares.
\end{definition}

Theorem~\ref{igusa:primitive-presentation-map} constructs the
primitive map from simple-root representatives satisfying the
relations. Surjectivity and equality of the two parity-refined PBW
series make that map an isomorphism. It then induces the ordinary
enveloping, Chevalley and current maps. A Hall-category comparison,
a quantum-double pairing and a Fredholm intertwiner each require
additional structure.

The analytic root operator \(K_Z\) acts on a positive root vector
by \(e^{-\alpha(Z)}\). On its trace-class domain,
\[
 \operatorname{sdet}_F(1-K_Z)=P=e^{\rho}\Dmon(2Z).
\]
An oriented skew doubling realizes this determinant as a Pfaffian
ratio. This constructs an operator model of the scalar product.
A global geometric family and its orientation transport are further
comparisons.

\section{The compact geometric problem}

Let \(X=S\times E\), where \(S\) is a smooth projective K3
surface and \(E\) is an elliptic curve. In the primitive nonzero
K3-class sector and the reduced incidence convention, curve counting
gives
\[
 \mathcal Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{X}=-\ChiTen^{-1}
 =-\bigl(\jmath^*\den(\gIg)\bigr)^{-2},
 \qquad \jmath(Z)=Z/2.
\]
The negative sign and the half-coordinate pullback are part of this
identity. The two copies of the root Fock space reproduce the inverse
square after the Weyl normalization; an identification with geometric
states is additional.

Physical charge is additive, whereas its three Gram invariants are
quadratic. For $c=(Q,P)\in\widetilde H(S,\mathbb Z)^{\oplus2}$,
write $\PiGram(c)=(Q^2/2,Q\cdot P,P^2/2)$ and put
\[
 B(c,d)=\PiGram(c+d)-\PiGram(c)-\PiGram(d).
\]
The group law
\[
 (c,\lambda)(d,\mu)=(c+d,\lambda+\mu+B(c,d))
\]
makes \(c\mapsto(c,\PiGram(c))\) additive. This split central
extension retains the cross term; projecting away that term does not
make the original Gram map additive.

Write $\mathcal L$ for the weight-one modular line.

\begin{definition}[Geometric realization datum]
\label{comp:def:geometric-realization}
A compact realization datum consists of reduced oriented coefficient
complexes on object, extension and flag moduli of \(X\); their
additive charge grading and polarized Gram refinement; point-supported
and wrapped elliptic sectors with their mixed correspondences;
primitive representatives and an integration map to the root
presentation; a specified coproduct and full Hopf pairing when a
double is compared; and a map of orientation lines to the automorphic
line \(\mathcal L^5\otimes\nu_5\), preserving their sections.
\end{definition}

These moduli share an elliptic torsor after translation quotient.
The coefficient maps must therefore act on the common-torsor
extension and flag diagrams. The geometric primitive map satisfies
the same presentation criterion as the algebraic map above.
Its relations, generation and both parity dimensions must be proved
on the actual classes. The coproduct, chiral collision operations,
orientation transport and state comparison retain their own equations.
Their existence is the compact realization problem.
