\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective K3 surface and \(E\) an elliptic
curve. Reduced curve counting on \(S\times E\), in classes primitive
and nonzero in the K3 factor, produces the reciprocal of the monic
Igusa cusp form \(\ChiTen\). Its square root \(\Dmon\) is an
automorphic section of weight five with an order-two character.
A geometric realization asks for extension operations on states,
their parity, an orientation line, and a comparison of its section
with \(\Dmon\).

The automorphic construction begins with the weak Jacobi form
\(\phiweak\), the type-II Lorentzian root lattice, its positive
roots, and its Weyl vector. The Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin
construction supplies a Lie superalgebra \(\gIg\) and the identity
\(\den(\gIg)=\Dmon(2Z)\). Proper-height Chevalley chains give its
homological denominator. A diagonal operator built from signed
multiplicities gives the Fredholm product on an absolute-convergence
chamber. The root Lie superalgebra carries the bracket, while the
minimal virtual superspace carries the signed product.

The compact comparison begins with sheaves, extensions, and flags on
\(S\times E\). It requires reduced critical coefficients and
orientation comparisons on those correspondences, then a root-graded
map preserving the Hall operations. Identifying a Pfaffian with the
automorphic section also requires a map of lines and their transport.
The reduced counting identity supplies the scalar value to recover.

\chapter*{The comparison problem}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The comparison problem}
An extension \(0\to A\to F\to B\to0\) remembers the relative
elliptic position of its endpoints. After translation quotient,
\(A\) and \(B\) must therefore live on the same elliptic torsor.
For \(E\)-stacks \(M,N\), the relevant product is
\[
 [(M\times N)/E_{\mathrm{diag}}]
 \simeq[M/E]\times_{BE}[N/E],
\]
where \(BE\) is the stack of elliptic torsors. The ordinary product
of the two quotients forgets the identification of their torsors.
For \(M=N=E\), the relative product is \(E\), with coordinate
\(y-x\), whereas the ordinary product is a point.

On the full coherent-sheaf stack, the middle-term map of an extension
correspondence is proper on each fixed Hilbert-polynomial component.
Its fibre is the appropriate Quot space. Iterated correspondences
are full flag stacks, and the zero sheaf gives the unit over \(BE\).
A critical cohomological realization must provide the coefficient
maps and orientation transport on these same flags. The desired
Hall--Borcherds map sends actual primitive classes to root vectors
and preserves brackets and both parity dimensions. A separate map
of square-root lines must preserve the orientation character.

The later algebraic and field-theoretic comparisons explain which
operations such maps must retain. The relative Rees and oscillator
calculations supply finite examples. Their compact Hall interpretation
requires a map from geometric states to the specified representations.
A Hochschild centre, a monoidal categorical centre, and a trace on a
representation each have their own carrier and comparison map.
The geometric realization keeps these structures until scalar evaluation.


\chapter*{Notation and normalization}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Notation and normalization}
The ground field has characteristic zero.  Stable categories are presentable and idempotent-complete.  Tensor products preserve colimits separately.  Completions are separated inverse limits of finite weight or proper-height quotients.  The Igusa normalization is
\[
 \Dtheta=64\Dmon,\qquad \ChiTen=\Dmon^2,
\]
with $\Dmon$ monic.  Physical charge is additive; Gram degree is carried through its polarization cocycle.  Bar, factorization, and Hopf coproducts retain distinct notation and distinct geometric meanings.
